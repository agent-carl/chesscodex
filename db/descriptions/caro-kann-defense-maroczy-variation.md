The Fantasy Variation is 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.f3: against the [Caro-Kann Defense](/openings/caro-kann-defense) White supports the e4-pawn with the f-pawn. It is also known as the Maróczy Variation, the name this site uses, and the Tartakower Variation. White invites 3...dxe4 4.fxe4, with a big pawn centre.

### Main lines

- 3...dxe4 4.fxe4 e5 is the main move. Nigel Davies describes it as the sharpest line of the Fantasy and dangerous for Black. The sacrifice [5.Nf3 exd4 6.Bc4](/openings/caro-kann-defense-maroczy-variation-maroczy-gambit) was played as early as 1899 by Géza Maróczy. Grandmasters Lars Schandorff and Sam Shankland prefer 5...Bg4 6.Bc4 Nd7 7.O-O Ngf6 8.c3 Bd6, with sharp, double-edged play, though recent theory suggests that 7.c3 is more critical and gives White a small advantage. After 3...dxe4, the recapture 4.Nc3 transposes to a line of the [Blackmar-Diemer Gambit](/openings/blackmar-diemer-gambit).
- 3...e6 is probably the most solid answer, preparing to use the dark squares with ...c5.
- 3...g6, fianchettoing the king's bishop, has been tried by Yasser Seirawan.
- 3...e5 is rarer and aims at White's weaknesses on the a7–g1 diagonal.
- 3...Qb6, championed by Baadur Jobava, has grown in popularity.

### The idea

The pawn on f3 protects e4. If Black takes on e4, White recaptures with the f-pawn and keeps pawns on d4 and e4. Against the solid 3...e6 Black aims to use the dark squares with ...c5.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.4% of the 2.5 million games and Black 43.2%. Under 1400 it is closer, 49.3% against 46.8%, and from 1400 up White wins about 51 to 53%. In 2,515 master games over the board White won 37.5%, Black 30.4%, and 32.2% were drawn.
