The Becker Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 h6, a way for Black to keep the gambit pawn in the [King's Knight's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kings-knights-gambit). Black prepares a pawn chain h6, g5, f4 that defends the f4-pawn while avoiding the [Kieseritzky Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kieseritzky-gambit).

### The idea

After 3...g5 at once White can undermine the chain with 4.h4, which practically forces 4...g4. With the pawn already on h6, Black is not forced to play ...g4 when White attacks the chain with h4.

The pawn on h6 also matters in lines where White plays g3. In most such positions ...fxg3 would allow Bxg5, which captures a pawn, develops a piece and attacks Black's queen at once. Here the h6-pawn prevents it.

### Main lines

- 4.d4 g5 is the main line, usually followed by 5.Nc3, 5.g3, 5.h4 or 5.Bc4.
- 4.Nc3 usually transposes to the main line after 4...g5 5.d4, but it has independent lines too.
- 4.b3 is another option, preparing a queenside fianchetto.

Transpositions are common. After 4.d4 g5 5.Bc4 Bg7 6.O-O the game is in a common line of the [Hanstein Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-hanstein-gambit), and 4.Bc4 most often transposes to the [Fischer Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-fischer-defense) after 4...d6. The most notable independent line, rarely reached by transposition, is 4.d4 g5 5.Nc3 Bg7 6.g3 fxg3 7.hxg3 d6.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.3% of the 874,000 games and Black 47.2%. The result turns with rating: under 1400 White leads 54.1% to 43.1%, while from 2200 up Black wins 51.0% against 43.7%. There are too few master games over the board, 133, to say much.
