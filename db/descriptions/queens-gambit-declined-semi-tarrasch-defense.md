The Semi-Tarrasch Defense is a variation of the [Queen's Gambit Declined](/openings/queens-gambit-declined): 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Nf3 c5. This page reaches it by the move order 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 d5 4.Nc3 c5. Unlike the regular [Tarrasch Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-tarrasch-defense), where Black plays ...c5 on move three, Black does not have to accept an isolated queen's pawn, because after the usual 5.cxd5 Black can take back with the knight. In return Black gives White a central advantage. Its ECO codes are D40 to D42.

### Main lines

- 5.cxd5 Nxd5, the main line. White chooses between 6.e4 and 6.e3, which lead to different kinds of middlegame. [6.e4](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-semi-tarrasch-defense-exchange-variation) is the most common and usually continues 6...Nxc3 7.bxc3 cxd4 8.cxd4 Bb4+ 9.Bd2 Bxd2+ 10.Qxd2 O-O. The position is similar to the Exchange Variation of the [Grünfeld Defense](/openings/grunfeld-defense-exchange-variation), but Black has exchanged the dark-squared bishop instead of fianchettoing it. This line is more drawish than 6.e3.
- 5.cxd5 cxd4, advocated by Wesley So, has become more popular over time. The most common continuation is 6.Qxd4 exd5 7.e4 dxe4 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8 9.Ng5 Be6 10.Nxe6+ fxe6, an endgame in which Black has doubled isolated pawns.
- 5.cxd5 exd5 is rarer. The usual continuation is 6.Bg5 Be6. White can also play 6.g3, which transposes to the main line of the Tarrasch after the usual 6...Nc6.
- 5.e3, the Symmetrical Variation (D40). White keeps the tension for now, and 5...Nc6 is the normal reply. White can then give Black an isolated pawn, accept one in return for active piece play, or play 6.a3, planning dxc5, b4 and Bb2 to make use of the extra tempo if Black keeps the symmetry. The answer 6...Ne4 was chosen by Bobby Fischer in his 1971 Candidates match against Tigran Petrosian.

### Who plays it

Ding Liren played the Symmetrical Variation against Hikaru Nakamura in the last round of the 2022 Candidates Tournament and won. The victory helped him qualify for the 2023 World Championship, which he went on to win.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.3% of the 2.2 million games and Black 44.3%. Under 1400 it is exactly even, 48.1% each. From 2200 up White wins 46.0% and Black 43.6%, with 10.5% draws. Over the board masters draw a lot: in 5,405 games 57.9% were drawn, White won 28.5% and Black 13.6%.
