The Czech Benoni, also known as the Hromádka Benoni after Karel Hromádka, is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 c5 3.d5 e5. Black meets 3.d5 with ...e5, instead of 3...e6, the [Modern Benoni](/openings/benoni-defense-modern-variation), or 3...b5, the [Benko Gambit](/openings/benko-gambit).

### Character

The Czech Benoni is more solid than the Modern Benoni, but also more passive. The middlegames that arise are characterised by much manoeuvring.

### Plans for both sides

In most lines Black prepares a pawn break with ...b7–b5 on the queenside or ...f7–f5 on the kingside. White may set up with Nc3, e4, h3, Bd3, Nf3 and g4, gaining space on the kingside and preventing ...f5.

### Who plays it

Grandmaster Ben Finegold often plays the Czech Benoni and notably beat Shakhriyar Mamedyarov with it.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 49.8% of the 396,187 games and White 45.0%. Under 1400 White is well ahead, 52.4% against 44.4%, and from 2200 up the results are level, 46.9% for Black against 46.4%. Masters have done better with White: in 3,030 games over the board White won 44.5%, Black 24.4%, and 31.1% were drawn.
