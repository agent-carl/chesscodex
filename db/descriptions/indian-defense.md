The Indian Defense, or Indian Game, is 1.d4 Nf6, the starting point of a large family of openings. They are all to some degree hypermodern defences: Black invites White to build an imposing pawn centre and plans to undermine and eventually destroy it. The hypermodern school championed them in the 1920s, but they were fully accepted only after Russian players showed in the late 1940s that they are sound. Since then the Indian defences have become a popular answer to 1.d4, as they often give a balanced game with winning chances for both sides.

### The name

The earliest known use of the term dates from 1884, and it referred to the openings played by the Indian player Moheschunder Bannerjee against John Cochrane. Savielly Tartakower popularised it in the early 1920s, and the modern names King's Indian, Queen's Indian and Old Indian were attributed by Richard Réti to Hans Kmoch.

### After 2.c4

The usual second move is 2.c4, taking a bigger share of the centre and preparing Nc3.

- 2...e6 is Black's most popular reply. It leads to the [Nimzo-Indian](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense) (3.Nc3 Bb4), the [Queen's Indian](/openings/queens-indian-defense) (3.Nf3 b6), the [Bogo-Indian](/openings/bogo-indian-defense) (3.Nf3 Bb4+) and the [Catalan](/openings/catalan-opening) (3.g3), among others.
- 2...g6, the main alternative, prepares a kingside fianchetto and leads to the [King's Indian](/openings/kings-indian-defense) or the [Grünfeld](/openings/grunfeld-defense).
- 2...c5 is the [Benoni Defense](/openings/benoni-defense), and 2...d6 the [Old Indian](/openings/old-indian-defense). 2...e5 is the [Budapest Gambit](/openings/indian-defense-budapest-defense).

Black can also return to classical openings with ...d5, such as the Queen's Gambit Declined or the Slav; these are not counted as Indian openings.

### White's other second moves

The main alternative is 2.Nf3. Against 2...d5 White can play c4 and reach the Queen's Gambit, or avoid it with the [London System](/openings/queens-pawn-game-london-system), the [Colle System](/openings/queens-pawn-game-colle-system) or the [Torre Attack](/openings/queens-pawn-game-torre-attack). White can also play the [Trompowsky Attack](/openings/trompowsky-attack), 2.Bg5, or 2.Nc3, which leads to the [Richter-Veresov Attack](/openings/richter-veresov-attack) after 2...d5 3.Bg5 or the [Jobava London](/openings/rapport-jobava-system) after 2...d5 3.Bf4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.3% of the 182 million games and Black 46.2%. White's score hardly changes with rating, while Black wins 47.7% under 1400 and 43.9% from 2200 up, where more games are drawn. In 626,798 master games over the board White won 31.9%, Black 23.3%, and 44.8% were drawn.
