The Anderssen Variation of the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.d3, the most popular alternative to 5.O-O in the [Morphy Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-morphy-defense). The pawn defends e4, and it indirectly threatens Black's e-pawn with Bxc6 and Nxe5, since ...Qd4 would no longer hit an undefended pawn.

### Black's replies

- 5...b5 6.Bb3 is usually followed by 6...Bc5, a much more active square for the bishop than e7. After 6...Be7 7.O-O the game transposes to the Martinez Variation.
- 5...d6 intends ...b5 and ...Na5 to trade White's bishop. White can answer 6.c3, which after 6...Be7 again transposes to the Martinez Variation, or 6.c4, the Duras Variation, which stops ...b5.
- 5...Bc5 prepares to meet Bxc6 and Nxe5 with ...Qd4, hitting the knight and lining up queen and bishop against f2. Play typically continues 6.c3 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.O-O O-O, and although White often plays 9.Nbd2 or 9.h3, the strongest move is 9.Bg5. After 9...h6 10.Bh4 g5 White must choose between the safer 11.Bg3 and the sharper 11.Nxg5.

### 5.d3 or 5.O-O?

If White wants a Ruy Lopez with d2–d3, it is objectively slightly better to castle first and play 6.d3 only after 5...Be7, the Martinez Variation, because after 5.d3 Bc5 it is not clear that White has an advantage. On the other hand, 5.d3 avoids the [Open Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-open).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.5% of the 2.1 million games and Black 44.2%. From 2200 up the gap narrows to 48.5% against 44.4%. In 6,428 master games over the board 42.5% were drawn, White won 33.3% and Black 24.3%.
