The Classical Variation of the [Scotch Game](/openings/scotch-game) (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 exd4 4.Nxd4 Bc5) puts immediate pressure on White's centralised knight, which at first is defended only by the queen. White has to exchange it, defend it or move it.

### The idea

Black develops the bishop with tempo and after 5.Be3 almost always adds a third attacker on d4 with ...Qf6. White's main choices are 5.Be3, reinforcing the knight, 5.Nb3, moving it while attacking the bishop, and 5.Nxc6, exchanging.

### Main lines

- 5.Be3 Qf6 6.c3 Nge7, the traditional line. 7.Bc4 is the most notable seventh move, met strongly by 7...Ne5; Alexey Sokolsky's 1940s analysis claimed equality after 7...Ne5 and 8...Qg6, so 7.Bc4 fell out of fashion until Garry Kasparov and others revived it in the 1990s. 7.Qd2 is the [Blackburne Attack](/openings/scotch-game-classical-variation-blackburne-attack), and 6...Qg6 the [Millennium Variation](/openings/scotch-game-classical-variation-millennium-variation). Black must avoid 5...Nf6??, which loses the bishop to 6.Nxc6 and 7.Bxc5.
- 5.Nb3 Bb6, the [Potter Variation](/openings/scotch-game-potter-variation), named after William Norwood Potter and revived with success in the 2000s by Vassily Ivanchuk and Magnus Carlsen. White usually continues 6.Nc3 with Qe2, Be3 or Bg5 and long castling.
- 5.Nxc6 Qf6, the [Intermezzo Variation](/openings/scotch-game-classical-variation-intermezzo-variation): Black's in-between move threatens mate on f2, so White cannot save the knight and Black can recapture on c6 with the d-pawn, avoiding an isolated pawn. White usually defends with 6.Qd2 or 6.Qf3.
- 5.Nf5 is another way to move the knight; after 5...d5 6.Nxg7+ Kf8 Black loses the right to castle but gets a big lead in development.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Active development with direct pressure on d4.
- After 5.Nxc6 the in-between move 5...Qf6 lets Black avoid an isolated pawn.

**Weaknesses**

- In the Potter Variation White moves the knight to safety with tempo on the bishop.
- Some natural moves are mistakes: after 5.Be3, 5...Nf6?? loses the bishop, and 5...d6? leaves Black with tripled c-pawns after 6.Nxc6 bxc6 7.Bxc5 dxc5.

### Who should play it

The Classical Variation suits Black players who want active piece play against the Scotch and are ready to learn concrete lines after 5.Be3, 5.Nb3 and 5.Nxc6.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are close under 1400 (47.9% for White against 48.2%), but White leads from there on: 49.3% against 46.5% at 1400–1799, 49.7% against 45.1% at 1800–2199 and 50.7% against 42.2% from 2200 up. In about 7,300 master games White won 32.2%, Black 23.9%, and 43.9% were drawn.
