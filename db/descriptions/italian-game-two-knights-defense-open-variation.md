The Open Variation of the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.d4, in which White opens the centre at once and aims for rapid development. After the usual 4...exd4 the position is the same as in the [Scotch Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-scotch-gambit) after 3.d4 exd4 4.Bc4 Nf6, its most common line. White then chooses between 5.e5, the most common move, 5.O-O and 5.Ng5.

### Advance Variation: 5.e5

5.e5, often adopted by Evgeny Sveshnikov, has gradually become the main line. Black can reply 5...Ne4 or 5...Ng4, but the most common and natural move is 5...d5, and after 6.Bb5 Ne4 7.Nxd4 Bc5 the play is sharp.

### Traditional line: 5.O-O

After 5.O-O Black is considered to equalise by removing White's last centre pawn with 5...Nxe4.

- 6.Re1 d5 7.Bxd5 Qxd5 8.Nc3 is the Anderssen Attack. White regains the piece, but Black is judged to have a comfortable position after 8...Qa5 or 8...Qh5. 6...d5 7.Nc3 is the Canal Variation.
- 6.Nc3 is the Nakhmanson Gambit. If Black takes the piece with 6...dxc3 7.Bxf7+ Kxf7 8.Qd5+, the natural 8...Ke8 gives White adequate compensation, but 8...Kf6 has been analysed to give Black a substantial edge with best play. Returning the knight with 6...Nxc3 7.bxc3 d5 8.Bb5 Be7 also leads to a better position for Black.
- 5...Bc5 invites 6.e5 d5, the extensively analysed [Max Lange Attack](/openings/italian-game-scotch-gambit-max-lange-attack).

### Perreux Variation: 5.Ng5

The tricky [5.Ng5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-perreux-variation) is best met by 5...d5 6.exd5 Qe7+, after which White most often has to play 7.Kf1. Black can also play 5...Ne5, with the typical continuations 6.Qxd4 Nxc4 7.Qxc4 d5 8.exd5 Qxd5 9.Qe2+ and 6.Bb3 h6 7.f4 hxg5 8.fxe5 Nxe4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.3% of the 3.1 million games and Black 43.4%. From 2200 up the results are much closer, 47.5% against 44.8%. In 1,712 master games over the board 39.9% were drawn, Black won 31.2% and White 28.9%.
