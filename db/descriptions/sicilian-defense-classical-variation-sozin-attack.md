The Sozin Attack is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 Nc6 6.Bc4, a sharp line against the [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-classical-variation) of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). The bishop goes to an aggressive square on c4. The line is named after the Russian master Veniamin Sozin.

### The idea

Black usually answers 6...e6 to limit the range of White's bishop. White can then build pressure against the e6-pawn by pushing the f-pawn, f4 and f5. The resulting lines are razor-sharp and highly theoretical.

### Main lines

After 6...e6 White chooses which side to castle:

- 7.Bb3 a6 8.O-O, castling kingside, is the Fischer-Sozin Attack, named after Bobby Fischer and Sozin, who originated it in the 1930s.
- 7.Be3 Be7 (or 7...a6) 8.Qe2 and 9.O-O-O, castling queenside, is the [Velimirović Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-velimirovic-attack).

Instead of 6...e6 Black can play Benko's move, 6...Qb6, which forces White to decide what to do with the knight on d4. It typically leads to more positional play than the Sozin and Velimirović main lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.3% of the 1 million games and Black 47.0%. Under 1400 Black is slightly ahead, 48.5% against 48.0%; from 2200 up White leads 48.4% to 45.3%. Masters over the board split the results almost exactly: in 8,387 games White won 32.9%, Black 33.7%, and 33.4% were drawn.
