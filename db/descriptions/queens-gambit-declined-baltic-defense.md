The Baltic Defense is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 Bf5, an unusual way to decline the [Queen's Gambit](/openings/queens-gambit). It is also called the Grau Defense or the Sahovic Defense, and its ECO code is D06. In most lines of the Queen's Gambit Declined Black struggles to develop the light-squared bishop; the Baltic solves that at once by bringing the bishop out on move two.

### The idea

Developing the bishop first has a cost: the bishop on f5 is exposed to e2–e4, and the b7-pawn loses a defender. If Black takes back on d5 with the queen at the wrong moment, White gains time: after 3.cxd5 Qxd5? 4.Nc3 Qa5 5.Bd2, followed by 6.e4 hitting the bishop, White has a strong advantage.

### Main lines

White has several replies, including 3.Nf3, 3.cxd5, 3.Qb3 and 3.Nc3.

- 3.Nf3 e6, and the white queen often comes to b3 to hit b7, met by ...Nc6: 4.Qb3 Nc6, or 4.e3 Nf6 5.Qb3 Nc6, or 4.Nc3 Nf6 5.Qb3 Nc6.
- 3.cxd5 Bxb1 4.Qa4+ Qd7 5.Qxd7+ Nxd7 6.Rxb1 Ngf6 7.Nf3. Black can also play 4...c6 5.dxc6 Nxc6, the [Argentinian Gambit](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-baltic-defense-argentinian-gambit).
- [3.Qb3](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-baltic-defense-queen-attack), the Queen Attack. John L. Watson recommends 3...e5 4.Qxb7 Nd7 5.Nc3 exd4 6.Nxd5 Bd6 7.Nf3.
- 3.Nc3 e6 4.Qb3 Nc6. Here 5.cxd5 exd5 6.Qxd5? is a mistake because of 6...Nxd4.

### Who plays it

The Baltic has never been widely accepted among masters, but world-class grandmasters have used it, including Paul Keres and Alexei Shirov.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.7% of the 4 million games and Black 42.9%. Below 2200 the split hardly changes with rating: 52.8% against 43.9% under 1400, and 53.3% against 42.0% between 1800 and 2199. From 2200 up the two sides are level, 46.3% against 46.2%. In 1,072 master games over the board White won 43.8%, Black only 19.2%, and 36.9% were drawn.
