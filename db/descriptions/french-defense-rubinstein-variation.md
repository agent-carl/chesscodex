The Rubinstein Variation is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4: in the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) Black gives up the centre at once by taking on e4. It is named after Akiba Rubinstein and can also arise from the [Tarrasch Variation](/openings/french-defense-tarrasch-variation), 3.Nd2 dxe4. After 4.Nxe4 White has freer development and more space in the centre, which Black plans to neutralise by playing ...c5 at some point.

### The idea

Black accepts White's space advantage in return for a solid position. This line has had a modest revival and appears in many grandmaster games as a drawing weapon, but theory still gives White a slight edge.

### Main lines

After 3...dxe4 4.Nxe4 Black has two main set-ups.

- [4...Nd7](/openings/french-defense-rubinstein-variation-blackburne-defense), the main line, whose most frequent top-level exponent is Georg Meier. Play might continue 5.Nf3 Ngf6 6.Nxf6+ Nxf6, and Black is ready for ...c5.
- [4...Bd7 5.Nf3 Bc6](/openings/french-defense-rubinstein-variation-fort-knox-variation), the Fort Knox Variation, which activates the light-squared bishop. Alexander Rustemov often plays it.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.7% of the 4.5 million games and Black 43.4%. The split hardly changes with rating: 49.6% against 46.1% under 1400, and 49.3% against 41.9% from 2200 up. Masters over the board draw half their games: in 5,832 games 49.8% were drawn, White won 36.0% and Black 14.2%.
