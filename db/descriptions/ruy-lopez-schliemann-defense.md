The Schliemann Defense, also called the Jaenisch Gambit or Jaenisch–Schliemann Gambit, is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 f5: against the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) Black hits back at once with the f-pawn. It is a sharp line in which Black plays for a kingside attack, often sacrificing one or two pawns. Its ECO code is C63.

### Origins

The variation was originated by Carl Jaenisch, who analysed it in the December 1847 issue of Le Palamède. The Handbuch des Schachspiels later named it after the German lawyer Adolph Schliemann (1817–1872), but the line Schliemann actually advocated in the 1860s was a gambit in the Cordel Defense, 3...Bc5 4.c3 f5.

### Main lines

White's most common answers are 4.d3 and 4.Nc3.

- [4.Nc3](/openings/ruy-lopez-schliemann-defense-dyckhoff-variation) fxe4 5.Nxe4. Now 5...d5 leads to the most heavily analysed variations of the gambit, with great complications, while 5...Nf6 generally leads to quieter play.
- 4.d3 is the other main reply.
- [4.exf5](/openings/ruy-lopez-schliemann-defense-jaenisch-gambit-accepted) is inferior: after 4...e4 5.Qe2 Qe7 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.Nd4 Nh6 Black has the better of it.

The deferred version, 3...a6 4.Ba4 f5, is rarely seen and considered worse for Black, because White answers 5.d4 with an advantage. Viktor Korchnoi still used it to draw against Anatoly Karpov in the 1974 Candidates Final, and Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa beat Vidit Gujrathi with it in the 2024 Candidates Tournament.

### Who plays it

The Schliemann is considered a good practical weapon but positionally risky, especially against a strong, well-prepared opponent. Teimour Radjabov is currently the only top player who uses it regularly, with mixed results.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 50.5% of the 1.7 million games and White 44.5%. Only under 1400 is White ahead, 49.3% against 47.5%. Between 1800 and 2199 Black wins 50.9% against 44.2%, and from 2200 up 48.2% against 44.7%. Master games over the board look different: in 4,363 games White won 38.9%, Black 20.1%, and 41.0% were drawn.
