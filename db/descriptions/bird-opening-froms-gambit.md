From's Gambit is 1.f4 e5: Black answers [Bird's Opening](/openings/bird-opening) by offering the e-pawn at once. It is named after the Danish player Martin Severin From (1828–1895). White can avoid it easily by transposing to the [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit) with 2.e4, which is one of its possible drawbacks: a player who does not want to face the King's Gambit may prefer another reply to 1.f4.

### Main lines

If White accepts with 2.fxe5, Black chooses between the main line 2...d6 and the rarer 2...Nc6.

- 2...d6 3.exd6 Bxd6. White must now play 4.Nf3, because 4.Nc3 runs into 4...Qh4+ 5.g3 Qxg3+ 6.hxg3 Bxg3#. Black then has two options: 4...g5, driving the knight away, and 4...Nf6, threatening ...Ng4 and ...Nxh2.
- [4...g5](/openings/bird-opening-froms-gambit-lasker-variation), the Lasker Variation, was introduced by the future World Champion Emanuel Lasker in Bird–Lasker, Newcastle upon Tyne 1892. IM Timothy Taylor, in his 2005 book on the Bird, considers it dubious and recommends 5.d4 g4 6.Ne5 for White, or the sharper 5.g3 g4 6.Nh4, in both cases with an advantage for White, although play stays extremely sharp.
- 2...Nc6. Here Taylor recommends 3.Nc3 Nxe5 4.d4, planning e4, rather than 3.Nf3 g5, when Black stands well.

### A trap for White

The worst answer to From's Gambit is 2.g4, which allows 2...Qh4#, the Fool's Mate.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins slightly more often: 48.7% against 47.5% for White in 2.9 million games. Black is ahead or level below 2200, for example 49.1% against 45.8% under 1400 and 49.0% against 47.0% between 1800 and 2199. From 2200 up White leads, 49.1% against 45.6%. In 406 master games over the board White won 38.4%, Black 27.3%, and 34.2% were drawn.
