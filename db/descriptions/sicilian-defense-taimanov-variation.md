The Taimanov Variation (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6) is a flexible system in the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense), named after Mark Taimanov. It can also be reached by 2...Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e6. Black develops the knight to a natural square and keeps the placement of the other pieces open.

### The idea

One of Black's ideas is to develop the king's bishop actively to b4 or c5. Taimanov's own plan was 5...a6, stopping Nb5, followed by ...Nge7, ...Nxd4 and ...Nc6, but the modern treatment is to develop the knight to f6 instead, for example 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Be2 a6 7.Be3 Nf6.

### Main lines

- 5.Nc3, more common nowadays:
  - 5...Qc7, the [Bastrikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation-bastrikov-variation), the usual move order in recent years; 6.Be3 is the [English Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation-bastrikov-variation-english-attack);
  - 5...a6, Taimanov's move. 6.Nxc6 bxc6 7.Bd3, despite its apparent simplicity, has given Black difficulties in reaching equality;
  - 5...d6, usually transposing to the [Scheveningen Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-scheveningen-variation);
  - 5...Nf6, the [Four Knights Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-four-knights-variation).
- 5.Nb5, the [Szén Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation-szen-variation), stopping ...Bb4 or ...Bc5. After 5...d6 6.c4 White sets up a Maróczy Bind favoured by Anatoly Karpov; 6...Nf6 7.N1c3 a6 8.Na3 b6 is a type of Hedgehog. The Kasparov Gambit 8...d5, played twice in the 1985 World Championship, virtually disappeared from master practice after Karpov–Van der Wiel, Brussels 1986.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very flexible: Black can choose between ...Qc7, ...a6, ...d6 and ...Nf6 set-ups.
- Natural development that keeps Black's options open.

**Weaknesses**

- Against 5.Nb5 and 6.c4 Black gets a cramped Maróczy Bind structure.
- The simple 5...a6 6.Nxc6 bxc6 7.Bd3 has caused Black problems.

### Who should play it

The Taimanov suits Black players who want a flexible, positionally rich Sicilian that can transpose to the Scheveningen or the Four Knights when it suits them.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are close: Black leads 49.2% against 47.3% under 1400 and 48.4% against 47.6% at 1400–1799, while at 1800–2199 it is 47.7% for White against 47.3%, and from 2200 up 47.3% against 45.5%. In about 35,000 master games White won 32.8%, Black 27.2%, and 40.0% were drawn.
