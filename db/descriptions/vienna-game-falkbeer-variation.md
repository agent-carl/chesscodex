The Falkbeer Variation of the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game) (1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6) is Black's most common reply to 2.Nc3. It is named after Ernst Falkbeer. Black develops and attacks e4 at once, and White usually continues with 3.f4, 3.Bc4, 3.g3 or 3.Nf3.

### The idea

Against 3.f4 Black strikes back in the centre with 3...d5, much as in the [Falkbeer Countergambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit) of the King's Gambit. The opening is full of transpositions, to the King's Gambit, the [Bishop's Opening](/openings/bishops-opening), the Four Knights and the Petrov.

### Main lines

- [3.f4](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit), the classical reply. Black should not take the pawn: 3...exf4 4.e5 forces the knight back to g8. The main line is 3...d5 4.fxe5 Nxe4, a sharp position:
  - 5.Nf3, the traditional main move, usually 5...Be7 ([Breyer Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-breyer-variation)); White gets open lines and attacking chances, but Black can usually hold the balance with correct play;
  - 5.Qf3, an aggressive queen move advocated by Ben Finegold;
  - 5.d3, the Oxford Variation, once unpopular but now more common;
  - 4.d3 is the [Steinitz Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-steinitz-variation), and 4.exd5 can transpose to the King's Gambit.
  Raymond Keene wrote that 3.f4 is considered too risky at grandmaster level, though Anthony Santasiere and Weaver Adams advocated it.
- 3.Bc4, the [Stanley Variation](/openings/vienna-game-stanley-variation), a position also reached from the Bishop's Opening. 3...Nxe4 4.Qh5 leads to sharp lines including the [Frankenstein-Dracula Variation](/openings/vienna-game-frankenstein-dracula-variation); 3...Nc6 4.d3 gives the Berlin-Vienna hybrid, and 3...Bb4 is the [Reversed Spanish](/openings/vienna-game-stanley-variation-reversed-spanish).
- 3.g3, the [Mieses Variation](/openings/vienna-game-mieses-variation), a quiet fianchetto line. Vasily Smyslov played it on a few occasions, notably to beat Lev Polugaevsky in the 1961 USSR Championship. The main line is 3...d5 4.exd5 Nxd5 5.Bg2 Nxc3 6.bxc3.
- 3.Nf3 Nc6 transposes to the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game), and 3.Nf3 Bb4 to [Petrov's Three Knights Game](/openings/petrovs-defense-three-knights-game).
- 3.a3 is the [Mengarini Variation](/openings/vienna-game-mengarini-variation).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Natural development with pressure on e4.
- Against 3.f4 Black has the central counter 3...d5.

**Weaknesses**

- Black must know some sharp lines, such as 3.Bc4 Nxe4 4.Qh5 and the 3.f4 gambit.
- Accepting 3.f4 with 3...exf4 is a mistake: 4.e5 drives the knight back to g8.

### Who should play it

2...Nf6 suits Black players who meet 1.e4 with 1...e5 and want an active, principled answer to the Vienna.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins about 51% below 2200: 50.4% against 45.4% under 1400, 51.6% against 44.5% at 1400–1799 and 51.2% against 44.0% at 1800–2199. From 2200 up it is 48.8% against 43.7%. In about 6,600 master games 45.1% were drawn; White won 29.2% and Black 25.7%.
