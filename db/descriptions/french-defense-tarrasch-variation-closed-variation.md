The Closed Variation of the [Tarrasch Variation](/openings/french-defense-tarrasch-variation) is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6, one of Black's two main replies to 3.Nd2 in the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense). While the other, 3...c5, aims to break open the centre, 3...Nf6 aims to close it.

### The idea

After 4.e5 Nfd7 Black attacks White's centre with ...c5 and, in the main line, ...f6. Because White's knight is on d2 rather than c3, White can support d4 with c2-c3.

### Main lines

- 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Bd3 c5 6.c3 Nc6 7.Ne2 is the main line. The knight goes to e2 to leave f3 for the other knight. After 7...cxd4 8.cxd4 f6 9.exf6 Nxf6 10.Nf3 Bd6 Black has freed the pieces at the cost of a backward pawn on e6.
- On move six Black can also play 6...b6, intending ...Ba6 to trade off the light-squared bishop, a recurring idea in the French.
- White can keep the pawn on e5 with 4.e5 Nfd7 5.c3 c5 6.f4 Nc6 7.Ndf3, but White's development is slowed, and Black gets dynamic chances if the position can be opened to Black's advantage.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.4% of the 3.4 million games and Black 43.6%. White's lead is similar under 1400, 51.1% against 45.8%, and from 2200 up, 50.7% against 42.7%. In 13,304 master games over the board White won 41.6%, Black 23.4%, and 35.0% were drawn.
