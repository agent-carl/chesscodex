The Modern Steinitz Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 d6, the most common fourth-move sideline of the [Morphy Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-morphy-defense) in the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez). It is also called the Deferred Steinitz or Neo-Steinitz Defense, and Alexander Alekhine, José Raúl Capablanca and Paul Keres played it often.

### The idea

Black defends e5 with the d-pawn, as in the ordinary [Steinitz Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-steinitz-defense), 3...d6. The difference is the inserted ...a6 and Ba4: Black can break the pin on the c6-knight with a timely ...b5, which gives more flexibility. In the ordinary Steinitz White can almost always force Black to give up the strong point on e5; in the Modern Steinitz Black keeps it.

### Main lines

Most natural moves are playable for White, including 5.c3, 5.c4, 5.Bxc6+, 5.d4 and 5.O-O.

- After 5.c3, 5...f5 is the sharp Siesta Variation, while 5...Bd7 6.d4 leads to a manoeuvring game.
- 5.Bxc6+ bxc6 6.d4 and 5.O-O Bg4 6.h3 h5 both give sharp play.
- 5.c4 and 5.d4 are older lines that are not considered testing for Black.

### Noah's Ark Trap

After 5.d4 Black can break the pin at once with 5...b5 6.Bb3 Nxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4. Now the natural 8.Qxd4? is a mistake because of 8...c5 9.Qd5 Be6 10.Qc6+ Bd7 11.Qd5 c4, and White's bishop is trapped, as in Steiner–Capablanca, Budapest 1929.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.3% of the 1.1 million games and Black 45.3%. The balance changes with rating: White leads 53.0% to 43.2% under 1400, while from 2200 up Black is ahead, 47.3% against 45.1%. In 6,364 master games over the board White won 36.3%, Black 24.7%, and 39.0% were drawn.
