The Euwe Variation is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Bd7, a main line of the [Advance French](/openings/french-defense-advance-variation). Instead of the usual 5...Qb6, Black plays the bishop move first: since ...Bd7 is usually played sooner or later anyway, Black makes it at once and waits for White to show their hand.

### History

5...Bd7 was mentioned by Greco as early as 1620. It is known as the Euwe Variation and was popularised by Viktor Korchnoi in the 1970s.

### White's sixth move

- 6.a3 is answered by 6...f6, after which modern theory says Black at least equalises. The lines are complex, but the main point is that a3 is a wasted move when Black's queen is not on b6, so Black uses the extra tempo to attack White's centre at once.
- 6.Be2 and 6.dxc5 are the common continuations.
- 6.Bb5 is immediately refuted by 6...Nxe5.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 50.6% of the 2.5 million games and White 44.7%. The picture changes with rating: Black wins 54.0% between 1400 and 1799, but from 2200 up White is slightly ahead, 47.3% against 46.4%. In 5,444 master games over the board 36.0% were drawn, White won 34.2% and Black 29.8%.
