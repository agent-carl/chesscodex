The Blackmar Gambit is 1.d4 d5 2.e4 dxe4 3.f3, the original form of the [Blackmar-Diemer Gambit](/openings/blackmar-diemer-gambit). White attacks the pawn on e4 at once, before developing the queen's knight.

### History

The gambit is named after Armand Blackmar, a relatively little-known New Orleans player of the late 19th century. He popularised these moves and was the first to publish analysis of the opening.

### Why it gave way

The popularity of the Blackmar Gambit was short-lived. It was basically unsound, because Black can answer the immediate 3.f3 with 3...e5 and get a superior position. In 1889 Ignatz von Popiel came up with 3.Nc3 instead, and the German master Emil Josef Diemer later popularised 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.f3. In that move order 4...e5 is ineffective, since 5.dxe5 hits the knight on f6 and after 5...Qxd1+ 6.Kxd1 it has to retreat. This is the modern [Blackmar-Diemer Gambit](/openings/blackmar-diemer-gambit-accepted).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.8% of the 1.2 million games and Black 45.6%. Black does better at both ends: 49.0% against 46.9% under 1400, and 50.5% against 44.1% from 2200 up. There are only 3 master games.
