The Hartlaub-Charlick Gambit, also called the Blackburne-Hartlaub Gambit, is 1.d4 e5 2.dxe5 d6, a branch of the [Englund Gambit](/openings/englund-gambit). Black offers to exchange the d-pawn for White's e-pawn: after 3.exd6 Bxd6 Black is a pawn down but ahead in development.

### History

The line was the original idea of Henry Charlick (1845–1916), the second Australian chess champion, who introduced 2...d6 in the early 1890s to avoid the closed openings. For this reason 1.d4 e5 as a whole is also known as the Charlick Gambit.

### Main lines

- 3.exd6 Bxd6 is the direct acceptance. A sample continuation is 4.Nf3 Nf6 5.Bg5 h6 6.Bh4, when White remains a pawn up with some advantage.
- 3.Nf3 first delays the capture. After 3...Bg4 4.e4 Nd7 the game transposes into a gambit line of the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense) played by Blackburne; Black gets partial compensation after 5.exd6 Bxd6 6.Be2 Ngf6 7.Nc3 Qe7. White obtains a large advantage, however, after 3...Bg4 4.Bg5 Qd7 5.exd6 Bxd6 6.Nbd2.

The Englund Gambit as a whole is considered weak.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are almost even: White wins 48.1% of the 4.5 million games and Black 47.8%. From 2200 up White does better, 51.5% against 42.9%. There are only 14 master games, too few to judge.
