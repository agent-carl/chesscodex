The St. George Defense is 1.e4 a6, an unorthodox reply in which Black prepares ...b5 and a queenside fianchetto instead of fighting for the centre at once. It is also known as the Baker Defense, the Birmingham Defense and the Basman Counterattack, and is classified as B00.

### Origins

The first known game with it was a simultaneous game on 11 December 1868 between an English amateur, J. Baker, and Wilhelm Steinitz, later the first official World Champion. Baker won.

Its most famous appearance came at the 1980 European Team Championship in Skara, Sweden, where Tony Miles beat the reigning World Champion Anatoly Karpov with it. It got the name "Birmingham Defense" at that time, after Miles's home town. Much of the theory was worked out by the English IM Michael Basman, who also played it.

Boris Spassky reached it by transposition in the 22nd game of his 1966 World Championship match against Tigran Petrosian: 1.d4 b5 2.e4 Bb7 3.f3 a6. It did not go well. Petrosian won and reached the 12 points he needed to keep his title.

### The idea

Players of the St. George are usually willing to give up the centre in order to attack from the flank, and to avoid theory. The move 1...a6 also keeps a white bishop off b5, so Black can continue in the style of the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) with ...e6 and ...d5, with sharp play and quick chances on the queenside.

### Main lines

The major lines start 1.e4 a6 2.d4 b5 and then branch.

- 3.Nf3 Bb7 4.Bd3 e6 5.O-O Nf6 is the main line. The set-up with [4...e6](/openings/st-george-defense-polish-variation) is called the Polish Variation on this site.
- 3.c4, the Three Pawns Attack or St. George Gambit: 3...e6 4.cxb5 axb5 5.Bxb5 Bb7. Black can also play 3...Bb7 and offer the b-pawn for White's e-pawn.
- The Neo-Spanish Variation imitates the Ruy Lopez: 3.Nf3 Bb7 4.Bd3 d6 5.O-O Nf6 6.Nbd2 Nbd7 7.Re1 e5. After 8.c3 c5 9.Bc2 Be7 10.h3 O-O the game has simply become the Breyer Defense of the Spanish Game.
- 2...e6 3.Nf3 d5 gives a hybrid of the St. George and the French, with Advance, Exchange and Two Knights versions.

### Who should play it

The St. George suits players who want to leave theory early and are happy to play against a broad white centre. It is a surprise weapon: Miles's win over Karpov shows what it can do, Spassky's loss to Petrosian what can go wrong.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.1% of the 3.7 million games and Black 45.8%. The gap narrows as the rating rises: under 1400 White wins 51.6% against 44.5%, and from 2200 up 47.3% against 46.6%. In 463 master games over the board White won 47.9%, Black 28.1%, and 24.0% were drawn.
