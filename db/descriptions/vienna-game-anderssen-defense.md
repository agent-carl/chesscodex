The Anderssen Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Bc5, an offbeat but playable answer to the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game). José Raúl Capablanca played it against Ilya Kan at Moscow 1936.

### 3.Nf3

After 3.Nf3, the natural 3...Nc6, which transposes to the [Three Knights Game](/openings/three-knights-opening), runs into 4.Nxe5 Nxe5 5.d4 Bd6 6.dxe5 Bxe5 7.Bd3, with a large advantage for White. Stronger is 3...d6. The main line then runs 4.d4 exd4 5.Nxd4 Nf6 6.Bg5 h6 7.Bh4 O-O 8.Nb3, and Nick de Firmian gives 8...Bb4 9.Bd3 Re8 10.O-O Bxc3 11.bxc3 g5 12.Bg3 Nxe4, when Black's chances are at least equal. 4.Na4 Nd7 5.d3 Ngf6 6.Be2 O-O 7.O-O c6 8.Nxc5 Nxc5 9.Ne1 Ne6 10.c3 d5 is about even.

### Other third moves

- 3.Bc4 lets Black choose between ...Nf6, ...Nc6 and ...d6.
- 3.f4 d6 leads to the King's Gambit Declined.
- 3.Qg4 is weak: in Tsikhelashvili–Karpov, USSR 1968, 3...Nf6 4.Qxg7 Rg8 5.Qh6 Bxf2+ gave Black a large advantage, since 6.Kxf2 Ng4+ would win White's queen.
- 3.Na4, the Hamppe Variation, can be met by 3...Bxf2+ 4.Kxf2 Qh4+ 5.Ke3 Qf4+ 6.Kd3 d5, the wild line of the famous Immortal Draw, Hamppe–Meitner, Vienna 1872. The quiet 3...Be7 also leaves Black with a good game.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.8% of the 2.8 million games and Black 45.1%. From 2200 up White's lead is a little larger, 51.1% against 42.7%. In 304 master games over the board 39.1% were drawn, White won 31.6% and Black 29.3%.
