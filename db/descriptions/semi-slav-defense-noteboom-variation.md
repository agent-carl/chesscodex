The Noteboom Variation is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 c6 4.Nf3 dxc4, named after Daniël Noteboom. It arises from the "triangle setup" of the [Semi-Slav Defense](/openings/semi-slav-defense): after 4.Nf3 Black has the extra option of taking the c4-pawn.

### A typical line

White can continue 5.a4, 5.e3, 5.e4 or even 5.Bg5. A typical line runs 5.a4 Bb4 6.e3 b5 7.Bd2 a5 8.axb5 Bxc3 9.Bxc3 cxb5 10.b3 Bb7 11.bxc4 b4 12.Bb2 Nf6 13.Bd3 Nbd7 14.O-O O-O. The position is an interesting one: Black has two connected passed pawns on the queenside, while White has the bishop pair and dominates the centre.

### Avoiding it

White can only sidestep the Noteboom on move 4. The [Marshall Gambit](/openings/semi-slav-defense-marshall-gambit), 4.e4, and 4.e3 are the ways to do it; 4.e3 usually transposes to the [Meran](/openings/semi-slav-defense-meran-variation) but gives up the lines with Bg5. The triangle setup is less suitable for Black when White plays 3.Nf3 instead of 3.Nc3, since 3...e6 can then be met by 4.e3 or 4.Qc2, again avoiding the Noteboom.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.0% of the 935,736 games and Black 47.4%. The result turns with rating: White wins 55.1% under 1400, while from 2200 up Black is ahead, 48.6% against 45.6%. In 2,682 master games over the board 38.3% were drawn, Black won 31.5% and White 30.2%.
