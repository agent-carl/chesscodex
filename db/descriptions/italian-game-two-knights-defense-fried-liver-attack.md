The Fried Liver Attack (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxf7) is one of the most famous attacks in chess. White sacrifices a knight on f7 to drag Black's king into the open, then attacks it with queen, bishop and pawns. It arises from the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense) when Black recaptures on d5 with the knight.

### Origins

The Fried Liver has been known for centuries. The earliest known example is a game played by Giulio Cesare Polerio before 1606, and the defence with the king protecting the d5-knight is described in a manuscript probably written around 1580–1590. It is also called the Fegatello Attack. The name comes from an Italian dish of liver wrapped in netting, just as Black's king is wrapped in White's mating net.

### The idea

After 6.Nxf7 Kxf7 7.Qf3+, the king must go to e6 to protect the pinned d5-knight. White then brings more pieces into the attack with 8.Nc3, and d4 or O-O and Re1 follow. Black is a piece up but the king is stuck in the centre. Black usually defends with ...Nb4 and ...c6 to hold the d5-knight.

### Main lines

- 6.Nxf7, the Fried Liver itself: 6...Kxf7 7.Qf3+ Ke6 8.Nc3 Nb4, and ...c6 to reinforce the pinned knight. Here 9.Bb3, 9.Qe4 and 9.O-O keep more attacking potential than 9.a3.
- The [Lolli Attack](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-lolli-attack) 6.d4, which Bobby Fischer considered "incredibly strong". Because of it, 5...Nxd5 is rarely played at a high level.

Black can avoid the whole line on move five:

- [5...Na5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-polerio-defense), the Polerio Defense, generally considered best.
- [5...Nd4](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-fritz-variation), the Fritz Variation, and [5...b5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-ulvestad-variation), the Ulvestad Variation.
- On move four, [4...Bc5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack), the Traxler Counterattack, a wild counter-sacrifice.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths (for White)**

- A very dangerous attack with simple, natural moves.
- Black's defence is hard to find over the board, especially in fast games.
- It has not been proven to be decisive, but it is extremely difficult to meet in practice.

**Weaknesses (for White)**

- Well-informed opponents never allow it: they play 5...Na5 instead.
- White gives up a whole piece. If the attack is not accurate, Black keeps the extra material.
- After 4.Ng5, White must also know the main lines after 5...Na5, where it is White who is attacked.

### Who should play it

The Fried Liver is a classic weapon for attacking players at club level and in fast games, and a great way to learn attacking chess. With Black, the lesson is simple: after 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5, play 5...Na5 instead of 5...Nxd5.

### The numbers

The Lichess numbers are the most one-sided on this site. Below 1400, White wins 71% and Black only 27%. Between 1400 and 1800 it is 69% against 28.5%, and between 1800 and 2200 it is 64% against 32%. Even above 2200 White scores 54% against 42%. There are almost no master games with it (63 in the database), because strong players don't allow it.
