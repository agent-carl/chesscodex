### 6...g6

Grandmaster Daniel King recommends 6...g6, which gives Black a more defensive kingside pawn structure. The idea is to counterattack later on the g1–a7 diagonal with a move like ...Qb6, preventing White from castling. An example line is 6...g6 7.Nf3 Bg7 8.a4 Nc6 9.Bd3 Qb6. The knight goes to c6 rather than the usual Najdorf square d7, because c6 is a more flexible square for it with the queen on b6.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.0% of the 405,165 games and Black 45.1%. Black is slightly ahead below 1800, while from 2200 up White's lead grows to 52.5% against 41.4%. In 4,469 master games over the board 38.0% were drawn, White won 32.4% and Black 29.7%.
