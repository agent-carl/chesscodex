The Icelandic Gambit, or Palme Gambit, is 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Nf6 3.c4 e6, a sharp line of the [Modern Scandinavian](/openings/scandinavian-defense-modern-variation). Icelandic masters invented it while looking for an alternative to the more common 3...c6. Black sacrifices a pawn for rapid development.

### The critical line

The most critical line in this double-edged variation is thought to be 4.dxe6 Bxe6 5.Nf3.

### After 3.c4

With 3.c4 White tries to keep the extra pawn at the cost of leaving the light-squared bishop inactive. Black's most common answer is 3...c6, the Scandinavian Gambit. Taking it with 4.dxc6 Nxc6 gives Black too much central control and development; John Emms called it "a miserly pawn grab". Most common is 4.d4 cxd5, which transposes to the [Panov Attack](/openings/caro-kann-defense-panov-attack) of the Caro-Kann Defense.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 51.8% of the 2.4 million games and White 44.0%, and Black stays ahead in every rating band, from 2200 up included (49.0% against 44.7%). In 411 master games over the board the results were nearly even: White won 33.8%, Black 32.4%, and 33.8% were drawn.
