The Knight Attack (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5) is White's most direct answer to the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense). The knight and the bishop on c4 together attack f7, which only Black's king defends.

### Origins

The Two Knights was first recorded by Giulio Cesare Polerio around 1580, and 4.Ng5 is White's traditional reply. It was common in the Romantic era but was criticised by the Classical school for moving a piece twice and attacking before development is complete; Siegbert Tarrasch famously called it "a duffer's move". It revived in the 1990s when Alexander Morozevich and Nigel Short took it up, though it remains less common than 4.d3. World champions Steinitz, Fischer, Karpov, Kasparov and Anand have all played it.

### The idea

After 4.Ng5 the only good defence is 4...d5, blocking the bishop. After 5.exd5 White practically wins a pawn by force, but Black gets quick development and the initiative in return. In the 19th century Mikhail Chigorin showed Black's compensation in the line 5...Na5 6.Bb5+ c6 7.dxc6 bxc6. The whole variation is known for extensive theory and sharp tactical play.

### Main lines

- 4...d5 5.exd5 ([Normal Variation](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-knight-attack-normal-variation)), then:
  - 5...Na5, the [Polerio Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-polerio-defense), also called the Chigorin Gambit: 6.Bb5+ c6 7.dxc6 bxc6, when 8.Bd3, 8.Be2 and 8.Qf3 are all common. 8.Be2 h6 9.Nf3 e4 10.Ne5 was the main line from the 19th century, but 8.Bd3 has overtaken it. 6.d3, which Paul Morphy preferred, tries to hold the pawn;
  - 5...Nd4, the [Fritz Variation](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-fritz-variation), best met by 6.c3;
  - 5...b5, the [Ulvestad Variation](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-ulvestad-variation), introduced by Olav Ulvestad in 1941; White's only good reply is 6.Bf1!;
  - 5...Nxd5?!, considered too risky to play, though not definitively refuted; 6.Nxf7 is the [Fried Liver Attack](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-fried-liver-attack).
- 4...Bc5!?, the [Traxler Counterattack](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack), ignoring the threat to f7 and leading to wild complications.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- White wins a pawn by force after 4...d5 5.exd5.
- Black must know a lot of theory; natural moves such as 5...Nxd5 are dangerous.

**Weaknesses**

- White moves the knight twice early, and Black gets a lead in development and the initiative for the pawn.
- In the old main line White ends up behind in development, though a pawn up with the better pawn structure.

### Who should play it

4.Ng5 suits White players who are happy to grab a pawn and defend, and who are ready for a lot of theory; Black players of the Two Knights must know the Polerio, Fritz and Traxler lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess White does very well at low ratings, 56.2% against 40.7% under 1400 and 53.0% against 44.0% at 1400–1799. From 1800 the results level out: 49.0% against 47.2% at 1800–2199 and 48.3% against 46.2% from 2200 up. In about 3,500 master games White won 32.9%, Black 24.5%, and 42.6% were drawn.
