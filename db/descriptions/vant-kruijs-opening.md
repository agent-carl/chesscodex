The Van't Kruijs Opening (1.e3) is a modest first move that releases the king's bishop and supports a later d4. It is an irregular opening, classified under A00, but according to ChessBase it ranks sixth in popularity among White's twenty possible first moves.

### Origins

The opening is named after the Dutch player Maarten van 't Kruijs (1813–1885), who won the sixth Dutch championship in 1878. There are several 19th-century examples, but it is not popular in the modern era and is not a common choice for grandmasters. Its ability to transpose into many other openings has attracted players such as Aron Nimzowitsch, Bent Larsen and Pavel Blatny, and Benjamin and Schiller (1987) called it a "chameleon".

### The idea

1.e3 makes only a small claim in the centre and blocks the queen's bishop, so it is somewhat passive compared with 1.e4. Its value lies in transposition: play may reach the [English Opening](/openings/english-opening) (c4), a Queen's Pawn Game (d4), the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) with a delayed d4, the [Bird Opening](/openings/bird-opening) (f4), [Larsen's Opening](/openings/nimzo-larsen-attack) (b3) or the [Stonewall Attack](/openings/queens-pawn-game-stonewall-attack).

### Main lines

- 1...Nf6, the most common reply, usually met by 2.Nf3, transposing to a [Réti Opening](/openings/reti-opening). White can follow with d4, possibly reaching a Queen's Gambit Declined, or set up a Hedgehog-like system with b3, c4, Nbd2 and Be2.
- 1...d5 and 1...c5 can be met in the same way.
- 1...e5, another common reply. 2.c4 gives a reversed Sicilian, while 2.d4 exd4 3.exd4 d5 transposes to the [French Defense Exchange Variation](/openings/french-defense-exchange-variation).
- 1...g6 is recommended by Benjamin and Schiller, who consider e3 set-ups weak against a fianchetto. 1...b6, 1...c6 and 1...e6 are also fully playable.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very flexible, with many transpositions.

**Weaknesses**

- Passive: White takes only a modest stake in the centre.
- The pawn on e3 obstructs the queen's bishop.

### Who should play it

1.e3 suits players who like to reach familiar structures by unusual move orders, for example reversed Sicilian positions, rather than fighting for an opening advantage.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black scores better at every level: 48.9% against 46.7% for White under 1400, 49.8% against 46.2% at 1400–1799, 48.8% against 46.6% at 1800–2199 and 47.3% against 46.1% from 2200 up. There are fewer than a thousand master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
