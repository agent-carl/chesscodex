The King's Knight Opening is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3, White's most popular second move after 1.e4 e5. The knight attacks Black's undefended e5-pawn, prepares kingside castling and supports a later d2-d4. Two of the most analysed openings in chess, the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) and the [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game), start from this position.

### Black's replies

- [2...Nc6](/openings/kings-knight-opening-normal-variation) is the usual answer. It defends the pawn and develops a knight, and White's third move then decides the opening.
- [2...Nf6](/openings/petrovs-defense), the Petrov Defense, counterattacks e4 instead. It is popular at the top level and has a drawish reputation, though it has many sharp lines.
- [2...d6](/openings/philidor-defense), the Philidor Defense, defends the pawn but typically leads to passive positions for Black. It fell out of favour by the early 20th century, yet it has a dependable reputation.

The other second moves are rare and generally considered dubious. The [Latvian Gambit](/openings/latvian-gambit), 2...f5, and the [Elephant Gambit](/openings/elephant-gambit), 2...d5, are risky pawn sacrifices. The [Damiano Defense](/openings/kings-pawn-game-damiano-defense), 2...f6, is weak and can be met by 3.Nxe5 or 3.Bc4 with advantage for White. The [Busch-Gass Gambit](/openings/kings-pawn-game-busch-gass-gambit), 2...Bc5, was something of an online fad in the early 2020s and is well met by 3.Nxe5 Nc6 4.Nf3.

### White's alternatives to 2.Nf3

White has four frequent alternatives on move two. The [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit), 2.f4, offers a pawn to undermine Black's centre. The [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game), 2.Nc3, also frequently features an f2-f4 push. The [Bishop's Opening](/openings/bishops-opening), 2.Bc4, frequently transposes to the Italian Game or the Vienna. The [Center Game](/openings/center-game), 2.d4, opens the centre at once.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.7% of the 340 million games and Black 44.6%. The split hardly moves with rating: 51.5% against 44.4% under 1400 and 48.1% against 44.4% from 2200 up. In 289,898 master games over the board 51.5% were drawn, White won 29.0% and Black 19.5%.
