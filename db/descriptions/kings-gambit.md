The King's Gambit (1.e4 e5 2.f4) is the most famous gambit in chess. White sacrifices the f-pawn on move two to open the f-file, build a big centre with d4 and attack Black's king. It is the symbol of the romantic era of chess, when attack and sacrifice mattered more than safety.

### Origins

The King's Gambit appears in Lucena's book of 1497. Ruy López de Segura was the first to publish analysis of it, and Giulio Cesare Polerio also examined it. It was one of the most popular openings of the Romantic era: the famous "Immortal Game" of 1851, Adolf Anderssen's win against Lionel Kieseritzky in London, began with it. It declined from the late 19th century as defensive technique improved, and Wilhelm Steinitz argued that it was logically flawed. In 1960 Boris Spassky beat Bobby Fischer with it in a famous game at Mar del Plata. Fischer then wrote an article titled "A Bust to the King's Gambit", recommending 3...d6, now called the [Fischer Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-fischer-defense). Today it is rare at elite level, although Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura have played it, and it remains popular at club level.

### The idea

After 2...exf4, White plans Nf3, d4, Bc4 or Bd3, and O-O, attacking along the open f-file and aiming at f7. If Black tries to keep the pawn with ...g5, both sides' kings become exposed and sharp tactics follow. White's own king is weakened by f4, which is the main risk of the whole opening.

### Main lines

- [King's Gambit Accepted](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted) 2...exf4:
  - [King's Knight's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kings-knights-gambit) 3.Nf3, the main line, answered by the [Fischer Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-fischer-defense) 3...d6, the [Modern Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-modern-defense) 3...d5, the [Cunningham](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-cunningham-defense) 3...Be7, or the old ...g5 lines.
  - [Bishop's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit) 3.Bc4: allows ...Qh4+ but gets quick development.
- King's Gambit Declined:
  - [Falkbeer Countergambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit) 2...d5: Black counterattacks in the centre.
  - [Classical Variation](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-classical-variation) 2...Bc5: stops White castling quickly by controlling g1.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Leads to open, tactical positions where White has the initiative from move two.
- Many opponents are unfamiliar with it and go wrong early.
- Great for learning attacking play: open files, development and king safety.

**Weaknesses**

- Objectively, Black can equalise or even get the better game with accurate defence (for example 3...d5 or 3...d6).
- White's own king is weakened for the rest of the game.
- If the attack fails, White is often simply a pawn down.

### Who should play it

The King's Gambit is for attacking players who enjoy risk and want to take the initiative at any cost, and for anyone who wants to learn tactics in open positions. It is effective against players who try to hold the extra pawn at all costs, or who defend passively. It is weakest against calm, well-prepared opponents who return the pawn at the right moment and develop quickly.

### The numbers

Below 1800 on Lichess, White scores 53–54% and Black only 43%. Above 2200 the score is level at 47% each. In master games over the board it is even worse for White: White wins 29%, Black 35% and 36% are drawn. The King's Gambit is one of the few main openings where Black outscores White among masters.
