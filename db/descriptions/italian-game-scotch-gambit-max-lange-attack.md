The Max Lange Attack is a sharp open game named after the German master Max Lange, who suggested it in 1854. It can arise from several openings, including the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense), the [Scotch Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-scotch-gambit), Petrov's Defense, the Bishop's Opening and the Giuoco Piano. This page has the Scotch Gambit order, 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 exd4 4.Bc4 Nf6 5.O-O Bc5, one move before the attack itself, 6.e5.

### The idea

White gives up the d4-pawn, castles and then attacks the f6-knight with e5. Black usually hits back in the centre with ...d5, and the play becomes sharp and tactical.

### Main lines

The other common move order is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.d4 exd4 5.O-O Bc5 6.e5. After [6.e5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-max-lange-attack) Black has two main replies.

- 6...d5 is the more common. The main line continues 7.exf6 dxc4 8.Re1+ Be6 9.Ng5 Qd5 10.Nc3 Qf5 11.Nce4 O-O-O with complex play.
- 6...Ng4 is playable but rarely seen.

Two traps sit in the main line. After 9.Ng5, taking with 9...Qxf6 runs into 10.Nxe6 fxe6 11.Qh5+ followed by 12.Qxc5. And after 10.Nc3, the greedy 10...dxc3 loses to 11.Qxd5, since 11...Bxd5 is illegal: the bishop on e6 is pinned against the king by the rook on e1.

White also has alternatives. After 6...d5 the critical 7.Bb5 leads to play with strong ideas for both sides, usually with a slight edge for White. Lev Gutman and Stefan Bücker analysed 8.fxg7 Rg8 9.Bg5 in the magazine Kaissiber and consider it good for White. Black usually answers 9...Be7 10.Bxe7 Kxe7.

### Who should play it

The Max Lange is for players on both sides who know the lines: one wrong move in the main line can lose at once, as the traps above show. The game H. Vatter vs. John Nunn, 1986, followed the main line up to 11...O-O-O.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 60.3% of the 1.5 million games and Black 36.6%. Below 1400 the margin is smaller, 54.8% against 42.7%. Between 1400 and 2199 White wins about 60% and Black about 37%, and from 2200 up 56.3% against 38.8%. In 231 master games over the board White won 39.4%, Black 34.6%, and 26.0% were drawn.
