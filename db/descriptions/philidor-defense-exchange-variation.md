The Exchange Variation of the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense) (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 exd4) is Black's most common answer to 3.d4. Black releases the central tension and gives up the centre, aiming for quick, harmonious development instead.

### Origins

The Philidor became a popular opening after François-André Danican Philidor advocated 2...d6, though he himself recommended the risky 3.d4 f5!?. It appeared in the famous Opera Game of 1858, in which Paul Morphy beat two strong amateurs, the Duke of Brunswick and Count Isouard. The defence then declined as positional play developed and had almost vanished from top-level chess by the First World War. Its popularity in master play has risen slightly over the last two decades, and it is fairly popular in rapid, blitz and bullet.

### The idea

After 3...exd4 White usually recaptures with the knight. Black develops with ...Nf6, ...Be7 and ...O-O and gets a compact defensive position, at the cost of less space.

### Main lines

- 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 Be7, the main line, called the Antoshin Variation after Vladimir Antoshin; Levon Aronian is a modern exponent. Black gets a strong defensive position after 6...O-O. White's sixth moves include 6.Bf4, which has become more popular, 6.g3, 6.Be2 and 6.Bc4; a typical line is 6.Bf4 O-O 7.Qd2 Nc6 8.O-O-O Nxd4 9.Qxd4.
- 4.Nxd4 g6, the [Larsen Variation](/openings/philidor-defense-larsen-variation), fianchettoing the bishop; Bent Larsen played it in a few games, including a draw with Mikhail Tal in 1969.
- 4.Nxd4 d5 5.exd5, the rare [Paulsen Attack](/openings/philidor-defense-paulsen-attack).
- 4.Qxd4, which Paul Morphy favoured, intending 4...Nc6 5.Bb5 Bd7 6.Bxc6 Bxc6 7.Nc3 Nf6 8.Bg5 and long castling; it was played in many 19th-century games.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- A strong defensive position once Black has castled.
- Clear, simple development with ...Nf6, ...Be7 and ...O-O.

**Weaknesses**

- Black gives up the centre, so White gets more space.

### Who should play it

The Exchange Variation suits Black players who meet 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 with the Philidor and want a solid set-up; the [Hanham Variation](/openings/philidor-defense-hanham-variation) (3...Nd7) keeps the pawn on e5 instead.

### The numbers

On Lichess White's lead grows with rating up to 2200: 50.2% against 45.7% under 1400, 51.3% against 44.3% at 1400–1799 and 53.4% against 41.5% at 1800–2199. From 2200 up it is 51.3% against 41.7%. In about 4,100 master games White won 38.5%, Black 25.9%, and 35.7% were drawn.
