The Queen's Indian Defense (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 b6) is a solid hypermodern defence. Black fianchettoes the queen's bishop to b7 to control the central light squares e4 and d5 with pieces rather than pawns. It is the natural partner of the [Nimzo-Indian](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense): when White avoids the Nimzo with 3.Nf3, the Queen's Indian is one of Black's main answers.

### Origins

The Queen's Indian follows hypermodern principles: Black controls the centre with pieces from a distance instead of occupying it with pawns. From the 1950s to the 1980s the classical main line was 4.g3 Bb7. From the 1980s, 4...Ba6 became the topical line. Grandmasters such as Alexander Beliavsky, Veselin Topalov and Magnus Carlsen have played the 5.Qc2 line against it. Tigran Petrosian favoured 4.e3 as White, and Garry Kasparov used the Petrosian Variation 4.a3 early in his career.

### The idea

With ...b6 and ...Bb7 (or ...Ba6), Black fights for the e4-square and keeps a flexible pawn structure. Black usually follows with ...Be7 or ...Bb4+, ...O-O and ...d5 or ...c5, depending on White's setup. White tries to gain space with e4, often preparing it with Nc3, Qc2 or a3, and to prove that Black's position is a little passive.

### Main lines

- [Fianchetto Variation](/openings/queens-indian-defense-fianchetto-variation) 4.g3: White's most popular line. Black can answer 4...Bb7, the [traditional line](/openings/queens-indian-defense-fianchetto-variation-traditional-line), considered drawish, or the modern 4...Ba6, which attacks c4.
- [Petrosian Variation](/openings/queens-indian-defense-kasparov-petrosian-variation) 4.a3: prepares Nc3 without allowing ...Bb4, and aims for e4. Black contests the centre with ...Bb7 and ...d5.
- 4.e3 and [4.Nc3](/openings/queens-indian-defense-kasparov-variation): natural developing lines that lead to complex middlegames.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very solid, with a sound structure and no early weaknesses.
- Flexible: Black adapts the setup to White's plan.
- Combines well with the Nimzo-Indian in one repertoire against 1.d4.

**Weaknesses**

- Some lines, especially 4.g3 Bb7, are drawish, so Black's winning chances are limited.
- The 4.a3 line gives White a pleasant space advantage if Black is passive.
- Black needs a separate answer if White plays 3.Nc3 or the [Catalan](/openings/catalan-opening) with g3.

### Who should play it

The Queen's Indian suits positional players who want a solid, low-risk defence to 1.d4 and are comfortable with slow manoeuvring. It works well against White players who want a sharp fight, because it gives them few targets. It is less satisfying for players who need to win with Black.

### The numbers

On Lichess the Queen's Indian is balanced at club level: White scores 48–49.5% and Black 47% below 1800. From 1800 up, White edges ahead, 47.5–48% against 44–45.5%. In master games over the board, 52% of games are drawn, White wins 28% and Black 19.5%.
