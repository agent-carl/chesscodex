The Hanham Variation is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 Nd7, a solid line of the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense). Instead of giving up the centre with ...exd4, Black keeps the pawn on e5 and sets up with ...Nd7, ...Be7 and ...c6. It is named after the American master James Moore Hanham, who favoured 3...Nd7.

### The idea

Black's aim is a solid defence of e5. Larry Kaufman compares the Hanham to the closed lines of the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez), where Black also holds the e5-pawn, and says it would be quite popular and on a par with the major defences to 1.e4, "except for the annoying detail that Black can't actually reach the Hanham position by force".

### Main lines

- 4.Bc4 is awkward for Black. 4...Ngf6 loses a pawn to 5.dxe5 Nxe5 6.Nxe5 dxe5 7.Bxf7+ Kxf7 8.Qxd8, and 4...Be7, a common mistake, loses a pawn to 5.dxe5 Nxe5 6.Nxe5 dxe5 7.Qh5. The best answer is 4...c6, but after 5.O-O Be7 6.dxe5 dxe5 7.Ng5 Bxg5 8.Qh5 White gets the bishop pair.
- 4.Nc3 Ngf6 leads to the main set-up. Tony Kosten called the order 3...Nf6 4.Nc3 Nbd7 the "improved" Hanham, because it avoids 4.Bc4. The main line continues 5.Bc4 Be7 6.O-O O-O, usually followed by Re1, c6 and a4. Here 6.Ng5 is considered inferior: after 6...O-O 7.Bxf7+ Rxf7 8.Ne6 Qe8 9.Nxc7 Qd8 10.Nxa8 b5 Black gets a strong initiative.

Aron Nimzowitsch favoured 3...Nf6 to avoid 3...Nd7 4.Bc4, but then White can choose 4.dxe5. More recently Black has also tried to reach the Hanham through 1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3.

### The numbers

On Lichess the two sides are almost level: Black wins 47.8% of the 1.5 million games and White 47.6%. Below 1800 White is slightly ahead, and between 1800 and 2199 Black is, 48.3% against 47.1%. From 2200 up White wins 50.3% against 43.5%. In 910 master games over the board White won 52.7%, Black 20.4%, and 26.8% were drawn.
