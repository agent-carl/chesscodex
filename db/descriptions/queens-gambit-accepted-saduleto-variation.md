The Saduleto Variation, more widely known as the Central Variation, is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.e4, a line of the [Queen's Gambit Accepted](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted). White immediately builds a strong pawn centre while Black's pawn sits on c4. It is an old move that became popular again in the 1990s. Raetsky and Chetverik call the line straightforward and critical, and say that anyone playing the Queen's Gambit Accepted must be ready to meet it.

### Main lines

- [3...e5](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted-central-variation-mcdonnell-defense) is the main reply, opposing White's centre in a highly theoretical system. White's best answer is 4.Nf3; 4.d5 is a common mistake. Play most often continues 4...exd4 5.Bxc4 Nc6 6.O-O Be6. Black can also play 4...Bb4+.
- [3...b5](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted-central-variation-greco-variation) tries to keep the pawn. It is fairly risky and rarely seen, though its popularity rose in the 2020s after the discovery of 6...Qb6 in the line 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5 6.Nc3.

### Other replies

- [3...Nc6](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted-central-variation-modern-defense) is a modern line that challenges the centre with ideas like those of the Chigorin Defense.
- [3...Nf6](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted-central-variation-alekhine-system) is another modern line. It provokes 4.e5, usually followed by 4...Nd5 5.Bxc4 Nb6, a pattern also seen in the [Alekhine Defense](/openings/alekhine-defense).
- [3...c5](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted-central-variation-rubinstein-defense), the Rubinstein Variation, undermines White's centre at d4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.7% of the 4.7 million games and Black 41.8%. White leads 54.7% to 41.3% under 1400, but from 2200 up the margin narrows to 48.4% against 44.2%. In 6,212 master games over the board White won 35.1%, Black 19.8%, and 45.1% were drawn.
