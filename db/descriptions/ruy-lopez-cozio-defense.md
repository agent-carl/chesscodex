The Cozio Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nge7, an old-fashioned and uncommon third move for Black in the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez). Bent Larsen used it occasionally with success, yet it remains one of the least explored variations of the Ruy Lopez.

### The idea

The knight goes to e7 so that Black avoids doubled c-pawns: if White takes the knight on c6, Black recaptures with the other knight, ...Nxc6.

### The Cozio Deferred

The same idea can be played a move later, 3...a6 4.Ba4 Nge7, the Cozio Defense Deferred, and it is frequently reached by transposition from the Cozio itself. Levon Aronian has advocated it, and in recent years Fabiano Caruana, Dommaraju Gukesh and Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa have also tried 3...a6 4.Ba4 Nge7. The critical line runs 5.c3 Ng6 6.d4 exd4 7.cxd4 Bb4+ 8.Nc3 d5 9.exd5 Bxc3+ 10.bxc3 Qxd5 11.O-O O-O, after which White keeps the bishop pair and Black has the better pawn structure.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.9% of the 3.1 million games and Black 44.4%. The gap narrows with rating: 50.6% against 45.4% under 1400, and 46.7% against 46.1% from 2200 up. In 2,378 master games over the board White won 36.5%, Black 26.1%, and 37.4% were drawn.
