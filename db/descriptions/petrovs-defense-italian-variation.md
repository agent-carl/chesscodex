The Italian Variation of the [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Bc4. White leaves the e4-pawn undefended and develops the bishop, often intending to meet 3...Nxe4 with 4.Nc3, the Boden-Kieseritzky Gambit. It is an occasionally seen alternative to the main lines 3.Nxe5 and 3.d4.

### Black's third move

- 3...Nxe4 takes the pawn and allows the gambit.
- 3...Nc6 transposes to the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense).
- 3...d5 is the aggressive alternative.

### The Boden-Kieseritzky Gambit

After 3...Nxe4 4.Nc3 the gambit is not considered wholly sound, since Black has several viable options. Black can accept with 4...Nxc3 5.dxc3 f6, but must play carefully after 6.O-O: 6...Bc5? 7.Nxe5 is disastrous, while 6...d6 and 6...Nc6 are good. Returning the pawn with 4...Nxc3 5.dxc3 c6 6.Nxe5 d5 equalises. Black can also transpose to the Italian Four Knights Game with 4...Nc6, meeting 5.Nxe4 with 5...d5; after 5.O-O Nxc3 6.dxc3 Qe7, Bobby Fischer wrote that "White has no compensation for the Pawn".

### Other fourth moves

After 3...Nxe4 White can also play 4.d3, for example 4...Nf6 5.Nxe5 d5 6.Bb3 Bd6 7.d4 O-O 8.O-O c5, or 4.Nxe5, which after 4...d5 5.Be2 usually leads to positions like the main line of the Petrov but with White a tempo down.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.3% of the 10.1 million games and Black 45.9%. White's lead shrinks as ratings rise, and from 2200 up Black is ahead, 48.1% against 45.4%. In 177 master games over the board Black won 37.3%, White 27.1%, and 35.6% were drawn.
