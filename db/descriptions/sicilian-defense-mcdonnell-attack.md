The McDonnell Attack (1.e4 c5 2.f4) is the immediate form of the Grand Prix Attack against the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). White grabs space on the kingside at once and prepares Nf3 and a kingside attack, without first committing the queen's knight.

### Origins

The name comes from the 14th game of the 1834 match in London between Alexander McDonnell and Louis-Charles Mahé de La Bourdonnais, in which McDonnell played 2.f4; La Bourdonnais answered 2...e6 and won.

### The idea

White gains space on the kingside and usually follows with Nf3 and a bishop to b5 or c4, the latter aiming for a kingside attack. The drawback of playing f4 at once is that Black can strike in the centre immediately with ...d5.

### Main lines

- 2...d5 3.exd5 Nf6!, the [Tal Gambit](/openings/sicilian-defense-mcdonnell-attack-tal-gambit). According to Jeremy Silman and others this is Black's best reply, and it has caused the immediate 2.f4 to decline in popularity.
- 2...d5 3.Nc3, declining the gambit, the so-called [Toilet Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-mcdonnell-attack-toilet-variation), named after its reputed place of invention.
- 2...e6, as La Bourdonnais played, a less common option.
- For these reasons players nowadays usually reach the Grand Prix by playing 2.Nc3 first and then 3.f4, the [Grand Prix Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-grand-prix-attack) of the [Closed Sicilian](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- A direct plan of kingside expansion.
- Avoids the Open Sicilian main lines.

**Weaknesses**

- 2...d5 hits the centre at once; the Tal Gambit is considered Black's best answer.
- Players now usually prefer the move order 2.Nc3 and 3.f4.

### Who should play it

The McDonnell Attack suits aggressive White players who want a kingside attack against the Sicilian and are ready to meet 2...d5; others reach the same plans more safely through 2.Nc3 and 3.f4.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does slightly better: White and Black are level under 1400 (48.3% against 48.6%) and at 1400–1799 (48.5% against 48.2%), while Black leads 49.1% against 46.7% at 1800–2199 and 48.4% against 45.6% from 2200 up. There are only about 2,700 master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
