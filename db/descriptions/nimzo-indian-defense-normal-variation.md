The Normal Variation is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3 O-O, Black's most flexible and most frequent answer to the [Rubinstein System](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-rubinstein-system) of the [Nimzo-Indian Defense](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense). Its main line is 5.Bd3 d5 6.Nf3 c5 7.O-O: White has completed kingside development, and Black has claimed a share of the centre.

### Black's seventh move

- 7...dxc4 8.Bxc4 Nbd7, the Parma Variation, named after Bruno Parma. White usually plays 9.Qe2 to clear d1 for a rook and support the advance of the d-pawn.
- 7...dxc4 8.Bxc4 cxd4 9.exd4 b6, the Karpov Variation, one of Black's most reliable defences. White has an isolated d-pawn; Black plans ...Bxc3 at some point and then ...Bb7, ...Nbd7, ...Rc8 and ...Qc7 to restrain White's c- and d-pawns.
- 7...dxc4 8.Bxc4 Nc6 9.a3 Ba5, the Larsen Variation. Black waits for White to play dxc5 before taking on c3, and otherwise plays ...Bb6 against the d-pawn.
- 7...Nc6 8.a3 Bxc3 9.bxc3 dxc4 10.Bxc4 Qc7, the main variation, enormously popular in the 1950s. Black equalises in all lines, but White has a huge choice of eleventh moves. Black plays a quick ...e5 against d4, while White tries to free the bishops by advancing the centre pawns.
- 7...Nc6 8.a3 Bxc3 9.bxc3 Qc7, the Khasin Variation, named after Abram Khasin.
- 7...Nbd7, the Averbakh Variation, has gone out of fashion since the 1960s because of Svetozar Gligorić's gambit 8.cxd5 exd5 9.a3 Ba5 10.b4 cxb4 11.Nb5!

### Lines with Ne2

Because the main line has held up well for Black, since the 1980s White has often put the king's knight on e2 instead of f3, so that it can recapture on c3 and avoid doubled pawns. The [Reshevsky Variation](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-reshevsky-variation), 5.Ne2, was a speciality of Samuel Reshevsky; its main line is 5...d5 6.a3 Be7 7.cxd5. In the Modern Variation, 5.Bd3 d5 6.Ne2, White develops the bishop first so the knight does not block it.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.2% of the 737,000 games and Black 45.4%. The margin is small both under 1400, 49.9% against 46.9%, and from 2200 up, 48.0% against 44.1%. In 22,646 master games over the board 48.0% were drawn, White won 27.5% and Black 24.5%.
