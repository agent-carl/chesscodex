The Fianchetto Variation of the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense) is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nf3 Bg7 4.g3, usually followed by 4...O-O 5.Bg2 d6 6.O-O. White develops the light-squared bishop on g2. It is one of the most popular lines at grandmaster level, and Viktor Korchnoi was once its best-known practitioner.

### The idea

The variation is played on completely different lines from other King's Indian systems. With the bishop on g2 White's kingside is better defended than usual, so Black's normal kingside attack can hardly succeed. Black's main plans aim at the centre with ...e5, or at the weakness on c4, the Achilles heel of the fianchetto lines.

### Main lines

- [6...Nbd7](/openings/kings-indian-defense-fianchetto-variation-classical-variation) prepares ...e5. In the Gallagher Variation play goes 7.Nc3 e5 8.e4 exd4 9.Nxd4 Re8 10.h3 a6, preparing ...Rb8, ...c5 and ...b5. On move eight Black can also play 8...c6 or 8...a6.
- [6...Nc6](/openings/kings-indian-defense-fianchetto-variation-panno-variation) 7.Nc3 a6 8.d5 Na5 puts the knight on the rim, against the old rule, to press against c4. Hundreds of master games have continued 9.Nd2 c5 10.Qc2 Rb8 11.b3 b5 12.Bb2 bxc4 13.bxc4 Bh6 14.f4 e5!

### A trap in the Panno line

In the line above, 14.e3 instead of 14.f4 runs into 14...Bf5, a trap that counts Mark Taimanov among its victims: White must now lose material, as there is no good interposition.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.1% of the 1.2 million games and Black 44.3%. The gap is similar at both ends of the rating range: 50.5% against 45.6% under 1400 and 47.9% against 43.5% from 2200 up. In 18,221 master games over the board White won 35.7%, Black 20.9%, and 43.5% were drawn.
