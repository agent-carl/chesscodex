The Scandinavian Variation of the [Alekhine Defense](/openings/alekhine-defense) is 1.e4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5. White declines to chase the knight with 2.e5 and defends e4 instead, and Black answers by striking at the centre with the d-pawn. 2.Nc3 is by far White's most common alternative to 2.e5; the author Cox saw it in over half his games with the Alekhine.

### 3.exd5

After 3.exd5 Nxd5 4.Bc4, the replies 4...Nb6 and 4...Nxc3 are considered roughly equal, while 4...e6 is solid but blocks in the light-squared bishop. 4.g3 has been played by the Danish correspondence player Ove Ekebjaerg.

### 3.e5

More combative is 3.e5, when Black can choose among 3...d4, 3...Nfd7, 3...Ne4 and even 3...Ng8. 3...Nfd7 transposes to the [Steinitz Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-steinitz-variation) of the French Defense after 4.d4 e6, though 4.e6 is a sharp alternative. After 3...d4 play can go 4.Nce2 Ng4, or 4.exf6 dxc3 5.fxg7 cxd2+, which leads to quick castling for White.

### Reputation

Most grandmasters play 2.e5 against the Alekhine, but Jonny Hector regularly plays 2.Nc3 and has scored well against 2...d5. His ideas have left White with a theoretical edge, so textbook authors on the Alekhine, including Davies, Cox and Taylor, have encouraged 2...e5 instead, which can transpose to the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game) or the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game).

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 50.6% of the 4.5 million games and White 43.5%. Under 1400 the order is reversed, 48.6% for White against 46.5%, and from 2200 up the results are close, 46.7% for Black against 45.2%. In 2,054 master games over the board 38.8% were drawn, White won 32.5% and Black 28.8%.
