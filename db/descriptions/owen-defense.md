The Owen Defense (1.e4 b6) is an offbeat reply to [1.e4](/openings/kings-pawn-game). Black fianchettoes the queen's bishop to b7, where it presses on e4 and joins the fight for the centre from a distance, instead of occupying it with pawns.

### Origins

It is named after the Rev. John Owen, a 19th-century vicar and strong amateur player who used 1...b6 regularly, including two games against Paul Morphy in 1858 (he won one and lost one). Earlier examples appear in the 17th-century writings of Gioachino Greco. Isidor Gunsberg played it with success in 1889, Bent Larsen and Michael Basman revived it in the 1970s, and more recently Tony Miles and Pavel Blatný have used it, and Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura have played it in online blitz.

### The idea

Black lets White build a big pawn centre with d4, then attacks it with the bishop on b7, ...e6 and ...c5. The price is space: White can take the centre with pawns and gain a spatial advantage, and Black must know how to hit back before that centre becomes a crushing bind.

### Main lines

After 1.e4 b6 2.d4 Bb7:

- 3.Bd3 e6 4.Nf3 c5 5.c3: historically the main line, with White supporting e4 with the bishop.
- 3.Nc3 e6 4.Nf3 Bb4 5.Bd3 Nf6: Black pins the knight and increases the pressure on e4.
- 3.Nf3, the [Smith Gambit](/openings/owen-defense-smith-gambit), leaving e4 to Black's bishop.

Other tries include 2...Ba6, the [Guatemala Defense](/openings/owen-defense-guatemala-defense), and the sharp [Matovinsky Gambit](/openings/owen-defense-matovinsky-gambit).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Rarely played, so opponents are often unprepared.
- Flexible: Black can choose between ...e6 and ...c5 set-ups and a double fianchetto.
- Less theory than the main defences to 1.e4.

**Weaknesses**

- It has a dubious reputation in opening theory: White gets the centre and more space for free.
- According to grandmaster Christian Bauer, Black may suffer against a well-prepared opponent.

### Who should play it

The Owen suits players who like to counterattack a pawn centre and want an offbeat weapon, especially in faster games. It is a risky choice against strong, well-prepared opponents.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.8–51% and Black 43.5–46.5%: Black does best under 1800 (46.2–46.5%) and worst from 2200 up (43.5%). There are only about 1,900 master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
