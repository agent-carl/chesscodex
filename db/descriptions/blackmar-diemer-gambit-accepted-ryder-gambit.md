The Ryder Gambit is 1.d4 d5 2.e4 dxe4 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.f3 exf3 5.Qxf3, a double-pawn sacrifice in the [Blackmar-Diemer Gambit](/openings/blackmar-diemer-gambit-accepted). Instead of recapturing on f3 with the knight, White uses the queen and leaves the d4-pawn unprotected. Emil Josef Diemer devoted most of his late-1950s book on the opening, Vom Ersten Zug An Auf Matt!, to this line and the Halosar Trap.

### 5...Qxd4

Black can take the second pawn. Gary Lane argued in 2000 that White has serious problems proving enough compensation after 5...Qxd4 6.Be3 Qg4 7.Qf2 e5. Black can also decline the pawn with 5...c6 or 5...e6 and hold the position.

### The Halosar Trap

The trap, named after Hermann Halosar, arises after 5...Qxd4 6.Be3 Qb4 7.O-O-O Bg4? 8.Nb5, threatening mate with 9.Nxc7#. The queen cannot take the knight, because 8...Qxb5 9.Bxb5+ is check and gives White's queen time to escape from the bishop. Black lost Diemer–Halosar, Baden-Baden 1934, after 8...Na6 9.Qxb7 Rc8 10.Qxa6. The main line continues 9...Qe4 10.Qxa6 Qxe3+ 11.Kb1 Qc5 12.Nf3, when, as Graham Burgess wrote, White has some advantage but Black has avoided instant loss.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.3% of the 976,818 games and Black 45.4%. The result turns with rating: White wins 54.1% under 1400, but from 2200 up Black is ahead, 51.3% against 43.9%. There are only 4 master games.
