The Rossolimo Variation (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5), called the Nyezhmetdinov-Rossolimo Attack in this database, is a well-respected alternative to the [Open Sicilian](/openings/sicilian-defense-open) with 3.d4. White develops the bishop with a pin and often gives it up for the knight on c6, leaving Black with doubled pawns.

### Origins

The variation is named after Nicolas Rossolimo and is related to the [Moscow Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-moscow-variation) (2...d6 3.Bb5+). Sergei Rublevsky and Tomáš Oral play both lines. Fabiano Caruana is perhaps its biggest proponent at the top level: he played it in games 1, 3 and 5 of his 2018 World Championship match against Magnus Carlsen.

### The idea

White's usual intention is Bxc6, damaging Black's pawn structure. 3.Bb5 is also a common way to avoid the [Sveshnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation), which arises after 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 e5.

### Main lines

- 3...g6, the [Fianchetto Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-nyezhmetdinov-rossolimo-attack-fianchetto-variation), preparing ...Bg7.
- 3...d6, preparing ...Bd7; this hybrid line also arises from the Moscow Variation after 2...d6 3.Bb5+ Nc6.
- 3...e6, preparing ...Nge7.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- White avoids the Sveshnikov and much of the Open Sicilian theory.
- Bxc6 leaves Black with doubled pawns.

**Weaknesses**

- Giving up the bishop hands Black the bishop pair.
- Black has several sound set-ups and is not forced into any one structure.

### Who should play it

The Rossolimo suits White players who want a positional answer to 2...Nc6 and who are comfortable trading bishop for knight.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are close below 1800: 49.6% for White against 46.7% under 1400 and 49.1% against 46.8% at 1400–1799. From 1800 White pulls ahead, 50.8% against 44.1% at 1800–2199 and 51.2% against 41.3% from 2200 up. In about 38,600 master games White won 34.3%, Black 22.3%, and 43.3% were drawn.
