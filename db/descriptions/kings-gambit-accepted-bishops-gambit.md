The Bishop's Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Bc4, a line of the [King's Gambit Accepted](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted). It is the main alternative to 3.Nf3: White develops the bishop first and lets Black check on h4. It is far less common than 3.Nf3, so its theory is less developed, but some grandmasters have argued that it is the better move. The famous Immortal Game started with this line.

### The idea

White's plan is to develop quickly and attack on the kingside along the half-open f-file and with the bishop on c4. Many of Black's defences include ...d5, which hits the bishop.

After 3...Qh4+ White loses the right to castle but answers 4.Kf1, where the king is reasonably safe: the queen cannot check it again, and White gains time by attacking the queen with Nf3. With no knight on f3 yet, White's queen can use the d1–h5 diagonal, and Black's usual plan from the [King's Knight Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kings-knights-gambit), ...g5 and ...g4, is less effective. White often delays the knight or develops it to e2.

### Main lines

Besides 3...Qh4+, Black usually plays 3...Nf6 or 3...d5.

- 3...Qh4+ 4.Kf1, the Classical Defense. It was the main line in the 19th century, a side line for most of the 20th, and is now about as common as 3...Nf6. The most common reply is [4...d6](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit-cozio-variation), the Cozio Variation, after which 5.Nf3 is usual. The aggressive 4...g5, the Lopez Variation, was once popular and is now only occasionally played. 4...Nf6 aims for ...Ng4 and ...Qf2 mate, and White usually attacks the queen at once with 5.Nf3.
- [3...Nf6 4.Nc3](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit-bogoljubow-variation), the Bogoljubov Variation, has been called the modern main line. It most often continues [4...c6](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit-bogoljubow-defense) with the plan ...d5. A main line runs 5.Bb3 d5 6.exd5 cxd5 7.d4: Black has an extra pawn and active pieces, but weak pawns on d5 and f4, while White leads in development.
- [3...d5](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit-bledow-variation), the Bledow Variation: Black gives back the pawn for rapid development. White usually takes with 4.Bxd5, and the main line continues 4...Nf6, the Morphy Variation. Emanuel Lasker considered 3...d5 4.Bxd5 Qh4+ a refutation of the gambit, but according to modern theory it is playable for both sides.

### Who should play it

In many lines White can transpose into positions normally reached with 3.Nf3 but often chooses not to, so the Bishop's Gambit gives a King's Gambit player a second, less explored path.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 54.2% of the 1.7 million games and Black 42.4%. White stays ahead at every rating: 53.8% against 43.4% under 1400, 55.5% against 41.8% between 1400 and 1799, and 51.2% against 43.7% from 2200 up. In 803 master games over the board Black did better: Black won 37.1%, White 26.8%, and 36.1% were drawn.
