The Modern Attack is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.d4, White's main alternative to 3.Nxe5 against [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense). It is also known as the Steinitz Variation: Wilhelm Steinitz recommended it in his Modern Chess Instructor (1889) and played it three times against Harry Nelson Pillsbury at St. Petersburg 1895-96, winning two games and drawing one. It has become more popular in the 21st century, though it is still played less than 3.Nxe5.

### Main lines

Black's most common reply is 3...Nxe4. After White's usual 4.Bd3 the main line goes 4...d5 5.Nxe5.

- 5...Nd7 is the modern move and now predominant, advocated by Vladimir Kramnik and Viswanathan Anand among others. It drew attention in 1980, when Bent Larsen won with it against Anatoly Karpov after a pawn sacrifice. White usually plays 6.Nxd7 Bxd7 7.O-O, and Black chooses between 7...Be7, 7...Qh4 and the most popular 7...Bd6. 6.Nxf7 is a drawing line: after 6...Kxf7 7.Qh5+ White can force a perpetual check.
- 5...Bd6 is the older symmetrical move, far less common now.
- [4...Nc6](/openings/petrovs-defense-modern-attack-murrey-variation), the Murey Variation, is an alternative to 4...d5. The knight on e4 seems to hang, but 5.Bxe4 d5! leaves the bishop without a good square, and 6.Bd3 e4 forks knight and bishop.

The asymmetrical 4.dxe5 is sharper and is not uncommon when White wants to avoid a draw.

### Other third moves for Black

After 3...exd4 the usual line is 4.e5 Ne4 5.Qxd4 d5 6.exd6 Nxd6. 4.Bc4 instead transposes to the [Urusov Gambit](/openings/bishops-opening-urusov-gambit), which is not considered wholly sound. 3...d6 transposes to the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.0% of the 2.9 million games and Black 43.1%. White is ahead both under 1400, 52.4% against 43.6%, and from 2200 up, 49.0% against 42.1%. Masters over the board draw most games: in 6,417 games 58.4% were drawn, White won 30.0% and Black only 11.6%.
