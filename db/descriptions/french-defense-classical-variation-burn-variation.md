The Burn Variation is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 dxe4, a line of the [Classical Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation) of the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) named after Amos Burn. It is Black's most common reply to 4.Bg5 at the top level.

### The idea

Black gives up the centre by taking on e4, much as in the [Rubinstein Variation](/openings/french-defense-rubinstein-variation). The difference is that White's bishop is already on g5, and after the usual exchange on f6 Black gets the bishop pair. That gives Black more dynamic chances than in the Rubinstein, although White's knight is well placed on e4, and the line is more popular than the Rubinstein. It has long been a favourite of Evgeny Bareev.

### Main lines

After White's usual 5.Nxe4:

- 5...Be7 6.Bxf6 Bxf6 7.Nf3 Nd7 (or 7...O-O) is the [main line](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-burn-variation-main-line).
- 5...Be7 6.Bxf6 [gxf6](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-burn-variation-morozevich-line), as played by Alexander Morozevich and Gregory Kaidanov, accepts a poor pawn structure. Black follows up with ...f5 and ...Bf6 and gets active piece play in return.
- 5...Nbd7 6.Nf3 Be7 7.Nxf6+ Bxf6 is another line that resembles the Rubinstein; 6...h6 is also tried.

The other main fourth move for Black is 4...Bb4, the [McCutcheon Variation](/openings/french-defense-mccutcheon-variation).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.3% of the 832,000 games and Black 43.1%. Under 1400 the gap is small, 49.8% against 46.1%; from 2200 up White leads 50.0% to 41.3%. Masters over the board draw about half their games: in 6,463 games 49.5% were drawn, White won 28.5% and Black 22.0%.
