The Dragon Variation (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 g6) is one of the sharpest lines of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). Black fianchettoes the king's bishop on g7, where it bites along the long diagonal, and the game often turns into a race of attacks on opposite wings.

### Origins

The German master Louis Paulsen began to play the modern Dragon around 1880. The Russian master Fyodor Dus-Chotimirsky wrote that he coined the name in 1901, because Black's kingside pawns reminded him of the constellation Draco. The earliest printed use of the name is from 1914. The Dragon featured in the 1974 Candidates final between Anatoly Karpov and Viktor Korchnoi. Garry Kasparov used it as a surprise weapon against Viswanathan Anand in their 1995 world championship match, scoring two wins and a draw. Veselin Topalov, Tony Miles and Eduard Gufeld are among its well-known exponents.

### The idea

The g7-bishop is the heart of Black's position: it presses on d4, on the long diagonal and on White's queenside. Black plays on the half-open c-file, often with ...Rc8, ...Ne5–c4 and sometimes an exchange sacrifice on c3, and pushes the a- and b-pawns against White's king. White usually castles queenside and throws the h- and g-pawns at Black's king, trying to trade the g7-bishop with Be3–h6 and open the h-file.

### Main lines

- [Yugoslav Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation-yugoslav-attack) 6.Be3 Bg7 7.f3 O-O 8.Qd2 Nc6: one of the sharpest of all chess openings. White chooses between the [main line with 9.Bc4](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation-yugoslav-attack-main-line) and [9.O-O-O](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation-yugoslav-attack-modern-line), where Black often answers 9...d5.
- [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation-classical-variation) 6.Be2: the oldest reply, with kingside castling and calmer play.
- The [Accelerated Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-accelerated-dragon), which skips ...d6 to fight for ...d5 in one move, is a separate system.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Clear plans and dynamic counterplay for Black: the c-file, the g7-bishop and the queenside pawns.
- Very sharp positions where Black plays for a win.
- The ideas repeat from game to game, which helps players learn it.

**Weaknesses**

- The Yugoslav Attack is extremely dangerous. Kasparov remarked that White's attack "almost plays itself" there.
- The theory is concrete and forcing, and many lines end in mate if one side is a tempo slow.
- Its popularity at top level declined from the late 1990s.

### Who should play it

The Dragon is for tactical, attacking players who love opposite-side castling and are ready to learn sharp theory. It works well against opponents who play the calm 6.Be2 or who don't know the Yugoslav Attack in detail. It is at its riskiest against well-prepared players who go straight for Be3, f3, Qd2 and h4.

### The numbers

On Lichess the Dragon is one of the few main lines where Black scores better than White at every band: 49–49.5% against 46–47% below 2200, and 46.7% against 46.3% above it. Among masters over the board the picture changes: White wins 39%, Black 23% and 37.5% are drawn, which matches the Dragon's reputation as a risky line at the highest level.
