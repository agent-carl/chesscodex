The Scandinavian Variation is 1.e4 Nc6 2.d4 d5, the line of the [Nimzowitsch Defense](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense) that Aron Nimzowitsch, its originator, usually preferred. Black meets White's two-pawn centre by attacking e4 at once, as in the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense), but with the knight already on c6. The other main answer to 2.d4 is the [Kennedy Variation](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-kennedy-variation), 2...e5.

### White's main choices

- 3.e5 is usually met by 3...Bf5 followed by ...e6, which no longer shuts in the light-squared bishop. Black later attacks White's central pawns with ...f6 and ...c5. 3...f6 is also a decent but more complex reply.
- 3.exd5 Qxd5 4.Nf3 aims to gain time by attacking the queen with Nc3, but Black can put pressure on White's centre with 4...Bg4 or 4...e5.
- 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.d5 Ne5 is the sharpest line; White usually continues 5.Qd4 or 5.Bf4 Ng6 6.Bg3. Black can also answer 3.Nc3 with 3...e6, which leads to a kind of [French Defense](/openings/french-defense).

### The idea

The Nimzowitsch Defense is a hypermodern opening. Black lets White occupy the centre with pawns, then tries to block or restrain them and to undermine them later with well-timed pawn advances or by attacking the pieces that defend them. Garry Kasparov and Raymond Keene wrote that it has never been fully accepted as a dependable opening, though it is sound.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.1% of the 3.2 million games and Black 47.5%. From 2200 up the result turns slightly in Black's favour, 49.5% against 44.0%. In 1,081 master games over the board White won 38.3%, Black 28.7%, and 33.0% were drawn.
