The Max Lange Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nc6, one of Black's two usual replies to the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game); the other is 2...Nf6. The original idea of the Vienna was a delayed King's Gambit with f4, but in modern play White often prefers quieter moves such as 3.Bc4 or 3.g3.

### Main lines

- 3.Bc4 is White's most common move. Black most often answers 3...Nf6, and after 4.d3 the game reaches the [Berlin-Vienna Hybrid](/openings/bishops-opening-vienna-hybrid), a position also reached from the Bishop's Opening.
- [3.f4](/openings/vienna-gambit-with-max-lange-defense) is the Vienna Gambit in its traditional sense: White offers a pawn to gain control of the centre, as in the King's Gambit. After 3...exf4 4.d4 White plays the [Steinitz Gambit](/openings/vienna-gambit-with-max-lange-defense-steinitz-gambit), a favourite of the first World Champion, Wilhelm Steinitz; it allows 4...Qh4+ 5.Ke2, and is rarely seen today.
- [3.g3](/openings/vienna-game-paulsen-variation), the Paulsen Variation, is named after Louis Paulsen, who won three games with it at the Vienna tournament of 1873.
- 3.Nf3 transposes to the [Three Knights Opening](/openings/three-knights-opening).

### A trap in the Symmetrical Variation

After 3.Bc4 Black sometimes plays 3...Bc5, the Symmetrical Variation. White's 4.Qg4 is awkward to meet, and 4...Kf8 and 4...g6 are thought to be the best replies, though neither is appealing. The natural 4...Qf6?? loses to 5.Nd5! Qxf2+ 6.Kd1, when White's king is in no real danger and White threatens Qxg7 and Nxc7+.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.0% of the 13 million games and Black 44.5%. Under 1400 the gap is small, 49.3% against 46.4%; from 2200 up it is 50.9% against 42.6%. In 1,976 master games over the board White won 32.6%, Black 27.9%, and 39.5% were drawn.
