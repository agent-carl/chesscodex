The Jaenisch Counterattack is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.c3 Nf6, one of Black's two usual replies to the [Ponziani Opening](/openings/ponziani-opening); the other is 3...d5. The knight attacks e4. It is considered Black's safest course, and the positions that arise range from highly chaotic to dull and passive.

### Main lines

White's usual answer is 4.d4, in keeping with the idea of 3.c3. Black most often replies 4...Nxe4, with 4...exd4 a frequent alternative.

### After 4...Nxe4 5.d5

The knight on c6 is attacked, and Black has three choices:

- 5...Ne7, the traditional line, often continues 6.Nxe5 Ng6. Here 6...d6? loses material to 7.Bb5+. After 6...Ng6 White plays 7.Qd4, 7.Qf3 or 7.Nxg6; the last line, 7...hxg6 8.Qe2 Qe7 9.Bf4 d6 10.Na3 Rh5 11.O-O-O Rf5, leads to equality according to Modern Chess Openings. Quiet draws are common in this line, though White keeps reasonable winning chances in the endgame.
- 5...Nb8 usually continues 6.Nxe5 Qe7 7.Qd4.
- [5...Bc5](/openings/ponziani-opening-vukovic-gambit), the Vuković Gambit or Fraser Defense, is an extremely sharp line in which Black lets White take the knight. The main line runs 6.dxc6 Bxf2+ 7.Ke2 Bb6 8.Qd5 Nf2 9.Rg1 O-O 10.cxb7 Bxb7 11.Qxb7 Qf6, with Black a knight and bishop down for two pawns but planning ...e4.

### After 4...exd4

4...exd4 gives a position that can also arise in the [Göring Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-goring-gambit) and tends to be more double-edged than 4...Nxe4. White usually plays 5.e5, and Black answers 5...Nd5 or 5...Ne4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.0% of the 3.7 million games and Black 46.7%. The gap stays small at every rating: 48.5% against 47.7% under 1400 and 47.7% against 45.5% from 2200 up. In 909 master games over the board White won 32.7%, Black 28.8%, and 38.5% were drawn.
