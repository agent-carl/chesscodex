The Bronstein-Larsen Variation is 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nf6 5.Nxf6+ gxf6, a line of the [Caro-Kann Defense](/openings/caro-kann-defense). After White exchanges knights on f6, Black recaptures with the g-pawn instead of the e-pawn.

### The idea

Black voluntarily accepts an inferior pawn structure, and in practice has to castle queenside. In return Black gets dynamic compensation, above all the open g-file for a rook. The play is unusually active by the Caro-Kann's standards.

### Reputation

The variation is generally considered somewhat unsound. It takes its name from two players who used it with some success: the world championship challenger David Bronstein and the former world championship candidate Bent Larsen.

The more solid recapture is 5...exf6, the [Tartakower Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-tartakower-variation), which is regarded as sounder. Other lines after 4.Nxe4 are the [Classical Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-classical-variation), 4...Bf5, and the [Karpov Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-karpov-variation), 4...Nd7.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.0% of the 490,000 games and Black 45.6%. White's lead is largest under 1400, 55.4% against 39.8%; between 1800 and 2199 the results are close, 47.5% against 46.4%, and from 2200 up White leads 48.6% to 43.2%. In 1,902 master games over the board White won 41.4%, Black 23.0%, and 35.6% were drawn.
