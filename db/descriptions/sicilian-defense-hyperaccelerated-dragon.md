The Hyperaccelerated Dragon (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 g6) is a [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense) in which Black fianchettoes the king's bishop at once, before committing the knight to c6 or the pawn to d6.

### The idea

2...g6 prepares ...Bg7 and Dragon-type positions. After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 the game transposes to the [Accelerated Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-accelerated-dragon). Black can also avoid the transposition with 3.d4 Bg7, the [Pterodactyl](/openings/sicilian-defense-hyperaccelerated-pterodactyl), when 4.dxc5 Qa5+ is a typical continuation.

### Main lines

- [3.d4](/openings/sicilian-defense-hyperaccelerated-dragon-2), the most common move:
  - 3...cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6, transposing to the Accelerated Dragon; White can also recapture with 4.Qxd4;
  - 3...Bg7, the Pterodactyl, for example 4.dxc5 Qa5+.
- 3.c3, transposing to a line of the [Alapin Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation).
- 3.c4, setting up a Maróczy Bind at once.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Flexible: after 3.d4 Black can still choose between the Accelerated Dragon and the Pterodactyl.

**Weaknesses**

- White can choose the structure, for example with 3.c4 or 3.c3, before Black has developed the knights.
- Black needs to know the Accelerated Dragon and Alapin structures it transposes to.

### Who should play it

The Hyperaccelerated Dragon suits Black players who like Dragon-type fianchetto positions and want to reach the Accelerated Dragon by a different move order.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does better below 2200: 49.0% against 47.7% under 1400, 49.4% against 46.8% at 1400–1799 and 48.4% against 46.3% at 1800–2199. From 2200 up White is slightly ahead, 46.7% against 45.4%. In about 10,900 master games White won 37.1%, Black 24.3%, and 38.6% were drawn.
