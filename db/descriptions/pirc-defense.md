The Pirc Defense (1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6) is a hypermodern reply to 1.e4. Black lets White build a broad pawn centre, fianchettoes the king's bishop to g7 and castles, planning to strike at the centre later with ...e5 or ...c5.

### Origins

The Pirc was occasionally played in the 19th century but was considered an irregular opening. It began to gain popularity only after the Second World War. It is named after the Slovenian grandmaster Vasja Pirc, who played it often from the late 1940s onwards. In Russia and the other former Soviet countries it is often called the Ufimtsev Defence, after the Soviet master Anatoly Ufimtsev. By the 1960s it was regarded as playable, largely thanks to the Canadian grandmaster Duncan Suttles. Later, Jan Timman played the Austrian Attack successfully with both colours, and Zurab Azmaiparashvili had good results with the Pirc as Black. It is a close relative of the [Modern Defense](/openings/modern-defense) (1...g6), where Black delays ...Nf6.

### The idea

Black builds a compact kingside with ...d6, ...Nf6, ...g6, ...Bg7 and ...O-O. White gets more space, but Black's position has no weaknesses, and the g7-bishop presses on d4 and the long diagonal. At the right moment Black hits the centre with ...e5, ...c5 or ...c6 and ...b5. The Pirc is a bet that White's centre will become a target rather than a strength.

### Main lines

- [Austrian Attack](/openings/pirc-defense-austrian-attack) 4.f4: the most aggressive answer, grabbing even more space and preparing e5. Black must react quickly with ...c5 or ...O-O and ...c5.
- [Classical Variation](/openings/pirc-defense-classical-variation) 4.Nf3: calm development with Be2 and O-O, leading to the [Quiet System](/openings/pirc-defense-classical-variation-quiet-system).
- [150 Attack](/openings/pirc-defense-150-attack) with Be3, Qd2 and often Bh6 and h4: a simple, direct plan to trade Black's key bishop and attack on the h-file. The name refers to an English (ECF) rating of 150, about 1825 Elo, suggesting that players of that strength can play it easily and get strong play.
- [Byrne Variation](/openings/pirc-defense-byrne-variation) 4.Bg5 and the [Kholmov System](/openings/pirc-defense-kholmov-system) 4.Bc4: less common tries.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Flexible: Black's setup works against many White systems, so there is less theory to learn.
- Unbalanced positions that give Black real winning chances.
- Punishes opponents who overextend with too many pawn moves.

**Weaknesses**

- White gets a big space advantage. If Black doesn't counterattack in time, Black gets squeezed.
- The Austrian and 150 Attacks are dangerous and require precise knowledge.
- Objectively it is considered slightly less sound than the main defences to 1.e4.

### Who should play it

The Pirc suits counterattacking players who are comfortable defending a cramped position while waiting for the right moment to strike, and who like the King's Indian structures. It is effective against opponents who build a big centre but have no clear plan, and against players who don't know the specific attacking systems. It is least comfortable against aggressive, well-prepared opponents who choose the Austrian Attack or the 150 Attack.

### The numbers

On Lichess the Pirc is balanced at club level: White wins 49–50% and Black 46–47% at every band below 2200, and 49% against 45% above it. In master games over the board, White wins 38%, Black 26% and only 36% are drawn. That is a sharp profile: Black wins more often than in most main defences, but also loses more often.
