The Adams Attack is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6 6.h3, an odd-looking pawn move against the [Najdorf](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation). Weaver Adams introduced it in the middle of the 20th century, Bobby Fischer used it several times, and it was long employed mostly as a surprise weapon.

### 6...e5

If Black continues 6...e5 anyway, White can answer 7.Nde2, followed by g4 and Ng3, and fight for the weak light squares by playing g5. It is therefore recommended that Black stops g4 altogether with 7...h5.

### 6...e6

Black can also choose a Scheveningen set-up with 6...e6. After 7.g4 b5 8.Bg2 Bb7 White has to lose more time defending e4, since ...b4 is a threat. An answer for White was not found until early 2008: after 9.O-O b4 White has the positional sacrifice 10.Nd5, which leaves Black with long-term weaknesses and gives White an open e-file. Since then the Adams Attack has been popular at all levels of play.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.6% of the 687,242 games and Black 44.9%. From 2200 up White's lead is 50.1% against 43.3%. Masters draw most of their games: in 11,255 games over the board 57.2% were drawn, White won 24.4% and Black 18.4%.
