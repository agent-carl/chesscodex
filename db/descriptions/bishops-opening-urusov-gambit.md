The Urusov Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nf6 3.d4 exd4 4.Nf3, a pawn sacrifice in the [Berlin Defense](/openings/bishops-opening-berlin-defense) of the [Bishop's Opening](/openings/bishops-opening). It is named after the Russian prince Sergey Semyonovich Urusov (1827-1897). It can also be reached from [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense) after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.d4 exd4 4.Bc4.

### The idea

White gives up the d-pawn for quick development and attacking chances. On move three the alternative 3...Nxe4 4.dxe5 gives White some advantage.

### Main lines

- 4...Nxe4 accepts the gambit. After 5.Qxd4 Nf6 White continues with Nc3, Bg5, Qh4 and O-O-O, and usually intends to meet ...O-O and ...h6 with the piece sacrifice Bxh6, exposing the king. Black's position is solid with no clear weaknesses, but White has attacking chances and active pieces for the pawn. After 5...Nd6 instead, 6.O-O gives White an overwhelming attack.
- 4...d5 declines the gambit. Play goes 5.exd5 Bb4+ 6.c3 Qe7+ 7.Be2 dxc3, and both 8.bxc3 and 8.Nxc3 give roughly equal chances.
- 4...Nc6 transposes to the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense).

From 4...Bc5 5.O-O Nc6 the game can also reach the [Max Lange Attack](/openings/italian-game-scotch-gambit-max-lange-attack).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.0% of the 781,000 games and Black 42.9%. White's lead is largest between 1400 and 1799, 54.4% against 42.6%, and smallest from 2200 up, 47.5% against 45.3%. Masters over the board draw more than half their games: in 424 games 54.7% were drawn, White won 24.8% and Black 20.5%.
