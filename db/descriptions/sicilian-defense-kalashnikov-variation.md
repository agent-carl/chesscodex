The Kalashnikov Variation is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e5 5.Nb5 d6, a close relative of the [Sveshnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation) of the Sicilian. It is sometimes called the Neo-Sveshnikov. In the late 1980s Black players revived 4...e5 with the plan of meeting 5.Nb5 with 5...d6.

### The idea

The ideas are similar to those of the Sveshnikov: Black accepts a backward pawn on d6 and a weak d5-square, but gains time by chasing White's knight. The difference is that Black has not yet developed the knight to f6 and White has not yet brought the other knight to c3, so both sides have extra options.

### Main lines

- 6.N1c3 a6 7.Na3 b5 8.Nd5 Nge7. Here Black develops the knight to e7 instead of f6, which avoids White's usual plan of Bg5 and Bxf6 to give Black doubled f-pawns.
- Black can also delay the knight and play ...Be7–g5 or a quick ...f5.
- 6.c4, the Maróczy Bind, strengthens White's hold on d5 and stops ...b5, but leaves the d4-square slightly weak.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.2% of the 2.7 million games and Black 44.6%. Between 1400 and 2199 White wins about 50.5% against 44 to 46%, and from 2200 up 47.6% against 44.8%. In 7,634 master games over the board White won 32.3%, Black 25.4%, and 42.3% were drawn.
