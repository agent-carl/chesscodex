The Portuguese Gambit, or Portuguese Variation, is 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Nf6 3.d4 Bg4, a sharp line of the [Modern Scandinavian](/openings/scandinavian-defense-modern-variation). Instead of recapturing the pawn on d5 at once, Black delays it and concentrates on rapid development. It is an important and recently popular alternative to 3...Nxd5.

### The main line

The normal continuation is 4.f3 Bf5 5.Bb5+ Nbd7 6.c4. The resulting sharp play is often similar to that of the [Icelandic Gambit](/openings/scandinavian-defense-icelandic-palme-gambit), 3.c4 e6.

### Black's other third moves

3.d4 is the main line against 2...Nf6. GM John Emms called it the common choice for White, and its idea is to let Black regain the pawn while White develops quickly. The obvious reply is 3...Nxd5, after which White can chase the knight with 4.c4 or play 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.c4. 3...Qxd5 is occasionally seen; it can transpose to the classical 2...Qxd5 lines of the [Scandinavian](/openings/scandinavian-defense) when White attacks the queen with Nc3, but White can avoid this with 4.Nf3 or 4.c4.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 50.9% of the 1.2 million games and White 44.0%. Under 1400 White is slightly ahead, 49.1% against 47.8%, but Black leads in every higher band. In 1,126 master games over the board White won 42.0%, Black 26.1%, and 31.9% were drawn.
