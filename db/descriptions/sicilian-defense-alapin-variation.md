The Alapin Variation (1.e4 c5 2.c3) is one of the most respected ways to avoid the Open [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). Instead of playing Nf3 and d4 and exchanging on d4, White prepares d4 with c3, so that a pawn, not a piece, can recapture and White keeps a strong pawn centre.

### Origins

It is named after the Russian master Semyon Alapin (1856–1923). For many years it was not highly regarded, because 2...d5 was thought to give Black easy equality. It is closely associated with grandmasters such as Evgeny Sveshnikov, Eduardas Rozentalis and Sergei Tiviakov, and today it is considered one of the most solid and respectable Anti-Sicilians. World champions from Anatoly Karpov and Garry Kasparov to Viswanathan Anand have used it; Deep Blue played it against Kasparov in their 1996 match, and Magnus Carlsen played it in a tiebreak of the 2023 FIDE World Cup.

### The idea

White wants a classical centre with pawns on e4 and d4. Black's two main answers both hit e4 before d4 comes:

- 2...d5: after 3.exd5 Qxd5 the queen is safe on d5 because White's knight cannot come to c3 with tempo.
- 2...Nf6: the knight attacks e4, and after 3.e5 Nd5 the game becomes a fight over White's advanced pawn.

### Main lines

- [Barmen Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation-barmen-defense) 2...d5 3.exd5 Qxd5, with the [Central Exchange](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation-barmen-defense-central-exchange) 4.d4 cxd4 5.cxd4 Nc6 6.Nf3 Bg4 and the [Modern Line](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation-barmen-defense-modern-line) 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 Bg4.
- 2...Nf6 3.e5 Nd5, with 4.d4 cxd4 ([Smith-Morra Declined](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation-smith-morra-declined)) or 4.Nf3 Nc6 5.Bc4 Nb6 6.Bb3 ([Stoltz Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation-stoltz-attack)).
- Rarer replies: 2...e6, 2...d6 and 2...e5.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Avoids the huge theory of the Open Sicilian (Najdorf, Dragon, Sveshnikov...).
- Clear plans for White: a pawn centre, and often an isolated d-pawn with active pieces.
- Solid: White takes few risks.

**Weaknesses**

- Black equalises comfortably with accurate play in both main lines.
- 2.c3 takes the c3-square from White's knight, so development is slower than in the Open Sicilian.

### Who should play it

The Alapin suits 1.e4 players who want a sound, low-theory answer to the Sicilian and are happy in positions with an isolated queen's pawn. It is an especially good fit for players who don't want to learn the Open Sicilian main lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess results barely change with rating: White scores 49–49.9% and Black 42.9–46.6%, and draws rise from 3.5% under 1400 to 8.1% from 2200 up. In master games over the board, White wins 28.5%, Black 27.7% and 43.8% are drawn.
