The Evans Gambit (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.b4) is the most famous gambit in the [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game). White offers the b-pawn to divert Black's bishop from c5, then uses the time to play c3 and d4, opening the centre and attacking.

### Origins

The gambit is named after the Welsh sea captain William Davies Evans, the first player known to have played it. The first recorded game is Evans–McDonnell, London 1827. It was a favourite of the Romantic era: Adolf Anderssen used it in his famous "Evergreen Game" against Jean Dufresne, and Paul Morphy and Mikhail Chigorin played it too. Emanuel Lasker dealt it a heavy blow with a defensive idea that returns the pawn, and it was out of favour for much of the 20th century. Garry Kasparov revived it in the 1990s, most famously with a 25-move win against Viswanathan Anand in Riga in 1995.

### The idea

After 4...Bxb4 5.c3, White gains a tempo on the bishop and prepares d4. The aim is to open the centre quickly, attack f7, and keep Black's king in the centre or make kingside castling difficult. White gets open lines and diagonals for the pieces in exchange for the pawn.

### Main lines

- [Accepted](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit-accepted) 4...Bxb4 5.c3:
  - [5...Ba5](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit-main-line), the main line.
  - [5...Be7](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit-anderssen-variation), the Anderssen Variation.
  - [5...Bc5](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit-mcdonnell-defense), regarded as inferior.
  - Lasker's Defence, where Black returns the pawn early with ...Bb6.
- [Declined](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit-declined) 4...Bb6: Black keeps the bishop and avoids the complications.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Fast development and a strong centre. White gets the initiative.
- Many opponents know the Italian Game but not the Evans, so it is a good surprise weapon.
- Modern analysis has found no clear refutation.

**Weaknesses**

- Black can return the pawn (as in Lasker's Defence) or decline with 4...Bb6 and reach a comfortable position.
- If the attack stalls, White is simply a pawn down.
- It only works after 3...Bc5; against the Two Knights Defense (3...Nf6) White needs something else.

### Who should play it

The Evans Gambit suits attacking 1.e4 players who like open positions and initiative, and who want more than the quiet Giuoco Pianissimo. It is effective against players who grab the pawn and then try to keep it at all costs. It is less effective against well-prepared opponents who return the pawn at the right time.

### The numbers

On Lichess White does well at club level: below 1800 White scores 53–54% and Black 43–44%. From 1800 to 2200 it is 52% against 44%. Above 2200 it is level at about 47.5% each. In master games over the board (a small sample of about 1,700 games), White wins 27%, Black 29% and 44% are drawn.
