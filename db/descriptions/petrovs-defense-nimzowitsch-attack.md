The Nimzowitsch Attack of the [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 d6 4.Nf3 Nxe4 5.Nc3. Instead of the classical 5.d4, White offers to trade knights and accepts doubled c-pawns after 5...Nxc3 6.dxc3, in return for rapid development and queenside castling.

### The plan

White can play a quick Be3, Qd2 and O-O-O and go for a kingside attack. White trusts that the doubled c-pawns will help protect the king, and that a strong initiative and attacking chances will outweigh their long-term drawback.

### A trap: 5...Bf5?

Black must avoid 5...Bf5 6.Qe2, which wins a piece because of the pin on the e-file. If 6...Qe7, then 7.Nd5 forces 7...Qd7 because of the threat to c7, and 8.d3 wins the knight. Viswanathan Anand fell for this against Alonso Zapata at Biel 1988 and resigned after only six moves.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.6% of the 960,693 games and Black 41.4%. From 2200 up the results are closer, 47.3% against 43.5%. Masters draw most of their games: in 5,659 games over the board 61.5% were drawn, White won 27.1% and Black 11.5%.
