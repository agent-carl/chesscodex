The Three Knights Variation of the [Nimzo-Indian Defense](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense) is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.Nf3, usually called the Kasparov Variation. White develops the knight to a natural square and waits to see Black's reply. The same position arises from the [Bogo-Indian Defense](/openings/bogo-indian-defense) if White answers 3.Nf3 Bb4+ with 4.Nc3.

### History

Garry Kasparov used 4.Nf3 to great effect against Anatoly Karpov in their 1985 World Championship match: he played it six times, scoring three wins and three draws. Today it is a favourite weapon of GM Alexei Barsov and of former Women's Champion Nona Gaprindashvili.

### Black's replies

- 4...d5 transposes to the [Ragozin Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-ragozin-defense) of the Queen's Gambit Declined.
- 4...b6 5.Bg5 Bb7 transposes to a Nimzo/Queen's Indian hybrid.
- 4...c5 or 4...O-O are the most common moves that stay within Nimzo-Indian territory.

### The main line

After 4...c5, 5.e3 transposes to the [Rubinstein System](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-rubinstein-system), but the main move is 5.g3, a position that also arises from the Fianchetto Variation. 5.g3 cxd4 6.Nxd4 O-O 7.Bg2 d5 8.cxd5 Nxd5 can be considered the main line. Black has dissolved White's centre, but the bishop on g2 presses on Black's queenside, and White can add to it with 9.Qb3.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.3% of the 2.3 million games and Black 46.5%; from 1800 up the results are nearly level. In 8,075 master games over the board 45.1% were drawn, White won 31.7% and Black 23.1%.
