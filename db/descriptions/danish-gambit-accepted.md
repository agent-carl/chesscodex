The Danish Gambit Accepted arises when Black takes the first pawn in the [Danish Gambit](/openings/danish-gambit): 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.c3 dxc3. White now chooses between a one-pawn gambit with 4.Nxc3 and the double gambit 4.Bc4, which offers a second pawn.

### The idea

After 4.Bc4 cxb2 5.Bxb2 White is two pawns down but has both bishops aimed at Black's kingside: the c4-bishop at f7 and the b2-bishop down the long diagonal at g7. White often follows up with Qb3, adding pressure on b7 and f7, and Black's bishops can find it hard to develop. One of Black's most reliable defences gives a pawn back to finish development.

### Main lines

- 4.Bc4, Lindehn's continuation, named after the Swedish player Hans Lindehn. Black can decline the second pawn with 4...d6, when 5.Nxc3 can transpose to the [Göring Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-goring-gambit). After 4...cxb2 5.Bxb2:
  - 5...d5, the [Schlechter Defense](/openings/danish-gambit-accepted-schlechter-defense). Carl Schlechter recommended returning a pawn to gain time. After 6.Bxd5 Nf6 7.Bxf7+ Kxf7 8.Qxd8 Bb4+ 9.Qd2 Bxd2+ 10.Nxd2 Re8 (Nyholm–Tartakower, Baden 1914) Black has regained the queen. Most theorists rate this endgame as equal, while some think Black's queenside majority gives Black the advantage. The Danish became much less popular after this defence was introduced.
  - 5...Bb4+, the [Copenhagen Defense](/openings/danish-gambit-accepted-copenhagen-defense), answered by 6.Kf1 or 6.Nc3.
  - 5...Nf6, the [Classical Defense](/openings/danish-gambit-accepted-classical-defense); 5...Qe7, the [Chigorin Defense](/openings/danish-gambit-accepted-chigorin-defense); and 5...d6, met by 6.Qb3.
- 4.Nxc3, recommended by Alexander Alekhine. White gives up only one pawn, and the game often transposes to the Göring Gambit. This move order lets White meet an early ...Bb4 by developing the king's knight to e2 instead of f3, so that Black cannot damage White's queenside pawns, as in Alekhine–Pomar, a clock simultaneous in Madrid in 1943.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- After 5.Bxb2 White has a big lead in development and two bishops aimed at Black's king.
- With 4.Nxc3 White keeps the investment to one pawn.

**Weaknesses**

- Against the Schlechter Defense White gets positions that are not what a gambit player wants.
- It is rarely played in top-level chess today.

### Who should play it

The accepted Danish suits White players who are happy to give material for fast development and open lines. Black players who meet it should know the Schlechter Defense.

### The numbers

White scores well on Lichess at every level: 54.7% against 42.3% for Black under 1400, 56.3% against 40.6% at 1400–1799, 55.8% against 40.5% at 1800–2199, and 50.7% against 44.0% from 2200 up. The masters database has only 57 games, too few to draw conclusions from.
