The Berlin Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nf6, Black's most common and most active reply to the [Bishop's Opening](/openings/bishops-opening). The knight attacks e4 and forces White to decide at once how to defend the pawn.

### The idea

The usual answer is 3.d3. Black then has to watch for a later f2-f4 from White, a delayed King's Gambit. Because many lines transpose, White sometimes uses the Bishop's Opening as a way to reach the Giuoco Piano without allowing Petrov's Defense: 3.d3 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bc5 is the quiet Giuoco Pianissimo.

### Main lines

After 3.d3:

- [3...c6](/openings/bishops-opening-paulsen-defense), the Paulsen Defense, is the most well-regarded reply. Black aims to build a strong centre with ...d5 and gain a tempo on the bishop; play usually goes 4.Nf3 d5 5.Bb3, and then most often 5...Bd6. It is the most common line that cannot be reached from the Italian Game or the Vienna.
- 3...Nc6 transposes to the [Modern Bishop's Opening](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-modern-bishops-opening) after 4.Nf3, or reaches the [Vienna Hybrid](/openings/bishops-opening-vienna-hybrid) after 4.Nc3.
- 3...Bc5 is also common and often transposes to the Giuoco Piano after a later ...Nc6.

White can also avoid d3. The most common alternative is 3.Nc3, a position also commonly reached from the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game-stanley-variation). 3.d4, the [Ponziani Gambit](/openings/bishops-opening-ponziani-gambit), usually aims for the [Urusov Gambit](/openings/bishops-opening-urusov-gambit) after 3...exd4 4.Nf3. 3.Nf3 usually intends the [Boden-Kieseritzky Gambit](/openings/bishops-opening-boden-kieseritzky-gambit), 3...Nxe4 4.Nc3.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.5% of the 18 million games and Black 45.3%. The gap is similar at every level: 49.9% against 46.4% under 1400 and 48.4% against 44.8% from 2200 up. In 7,755 master games over the board 44.6% were drawn, White won 33.3% and Black 22.1%.
