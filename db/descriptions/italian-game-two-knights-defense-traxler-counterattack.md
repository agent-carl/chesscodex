The Traxler Counterattack, also known as the Wilkes-Barre Variation, is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 Bc5. In the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense) White has chosen the aggressive [4.Ng5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-knight-attack), attacking f7 with knight and bishop, and Black ignores the threat and counterattacks f2 instead. Its ECO code is C57.

### Origins

The variation was first seen in the game J. Reinisch–Karel Traxler, Hostouň 1890. Frank Marshall later named it after Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania, claiming to be the first to analyse and publish it. So today 4...Bc5 is known as the Traxler Variation and, in the United States and the United Kingdom only, as the Wilkes-Barre Variation.

In the original game White answered the sacrifice badly and Black won with a famous mating attack, giving up the queen and both knights to chase the white king to the edge of the board.

### Main lines

- [5.Nxf7](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack-knight-sacrifice-line) is the natural move: the knight, defended by the bishop, forks the black queen and rook. Black answers 5...Bxf2+, drawing out the white king and bringing the queen into play with gain of time. After 6.Kxf2 Nxe4+ the best moves are 7.Kg1 and [7.Ke3](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack-king-march-line), as any square where the king can be checked can lead to a decisive attack, and Black usually follows with ...Qh4. The best answer to 5...Bxf2+ is 6.Kf1: the king can no longer be checked, Black has to move the queen with 6...Qe7, and White takes the rook. After 7.Nxh8 d5 8.exd5 Nd4 Black still has a strong attack, but White keeps the advantage with 9.d6.
- [5.Bxf7+](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack-bishop-sacrifice-line) is preferred by most modern masters: it wins a pawn and avoids the complications. After 5...Ke7 White should move the bishop again, and the main line continues 6.Bd5, with 6.Bb3 and 6.b4 also possible. White is considered to have a slight edge but is behind in development, and Black's rook can become active on the open f-file.

### A beginner's trap

After 5.Nxf7 Bxf2+ 6.Kxf2 Nxe4+ 7.Ke3 Qh4, taking the rook with 8.Nxh8 is a common beginner's mistake: mate in four follows with 8...Qf4+ 9.Ke2 Qf2+ 10.Kd3 Nb4+ 11.Kxe4 Qf4#.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins slightly more often: 49.5% against 47.4% for White in 1.3 million games. Under 1400 White is ahead, 51.6% against 45.8%. From 1800 up Black wins more: 50.0% against 46.6% between 1800 and 2199, and 48.3% against 46.7% from 2200 up. There are only 130 master games over the board, in which White won 43.8% and Black 21.5%.
