The Richter-Rauzer Variation, also called the Richter–Rauzer Attack, is White's most common reply to the [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-classical-variation) of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense): 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 Nc6 6.Bg5. Its ECO codes are B60–B69.

### Origins

The move 6.Bg5 was Kurt Richter's invention. After 6...e6, Vsevolod Rauzer introduced the modern plan of Qd2 and O-O-O in the 1930s.

The Classical Variation itself can arise from two move orders: 2...d6 followed by 5...Nc6, as on this page, or 2...Nc6 followed by 5...d6.

### The idea

6.Bg5 threatens to double Black's pawns with Bxf6, and it forestalls the [Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation), because 6...g6 is now unplayable.

White's pressure on the d6-pawn often makes Black answer Bxf6 with ...gxf6 rather than recapture with a piece, such as the queen, that also has to defend the d-pawn. This weakens Black's kingside pawn structure, but in return Black gets the two bishops and a central pawn majority.

### Main lines

- 6...e6 ([this position](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-2)) 7.Qd2 is Rauzer's plan, the [Traditional Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-traditional-variation) in this database, with long castling to follow. 7...Be7 is the [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-classical-variation) of the Richter-Rauzer, and 8.O-O-O O-O 9.f4 Nxd4 the [Rauzer Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-rauzer-attack).
- 7.Qd3, favoured by Paul Keres, is an alternative for those who want to avoid the main lines ([6...e6 7.Qd3](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-3)).
- Other seventh moves in this database include 7.Bb5, the [Vitolins Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-vitolins-variation), and 7.Nxc6, the [Exchange Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-exchange-variation).
- 6...Bd7 is the [Modern Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-richter-rauzer-variation-modern-variation) in this database.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**For White**

- A clear plan: Qd2, long castling and pressure on d6.
- Bxf6 can double Black's pawns, and the Dragon set-up with ...g6 is ruled out.

**For Black**

- After ...gxf6 Black's kingside pawns are weakened, but Black has the two bishops and a central pawn majority.

### Who should play it

The Richter-Rauzer suits White players who want to meet the Classical Sicilian with a direct plan: Qd2, long castling and pressure on Black's d-pawn.

### The numbers

In 935,582 rated Lichess games between players rated 1600 and 2500, White won 49.1%, Black 45.4%, and 5.5% were drawn. White is ahead in every rating band: 51.4% against 45.1% under 1400, 50.0% against 46.0% at 1400–1799, 50.0% against 45.1% at 1800–2199 and 47.6% against 45.4% from 2200 up. In about 25,000 master games White won 36.2%, Black 26.1%, and 37.7% were drawn.
