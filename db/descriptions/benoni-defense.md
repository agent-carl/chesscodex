The Benoni Defense (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 c5) is the most common way to reach the Benoni family of openings. Black attacks White's d4-pawn at once; White usually answers 3.d5, gaining space.

### Origins

The name comes from Aaron Reinganum's 1825 book *Ben-Oni oder die Vertheidigungen gegen die Gambitzüge im Schache*, which analysed defences to the King's and Queen's Gambits and the then unknown 1.d4 c5. Ben-Oni is a Hebrew name meaning "son of my sorrow"; Reinganum meant it as a nickname for his writings, not as the name of an opening. The original form, 1.d4 c5, is now called the [Old Benoni](/openings/benoni-defense-old-benoni).

### The idea

After 3.d5 Black chooses the structure: ...e6 leads to the Modern Benoni, ...b5 sacrifices a pawn for a queenside attack, and ...e5 closes the centre.

### Main lines

- 3.d5, the main move:
  - 3...e6, the [Modern Benoni](/openings/benoni-defense-modern-variation), the most common choice; after 4.Nc3 exd5 5.cxd5 d6 6.e4 the game reaches the [King's Pawn Line](/openings/benoni-defense-kings-pawn-line);
  - 3...b5, the [Benko Gambit](/openings/benko-gambit), a pawn sacrifice for a queenside attack;
  - 3...e5, the [Czech Benoni](/openings/benoni-defense-czech-benoni-defense), also named after Karel Hromádka: more solid than the Modern Benoni but more passive, with much manoeuvring and breaks such as ...b5 or ...f5;
  - 3...d6 ([Hromadka System](/openings/benoni-defense-hromadka-system)) or 3...g6, usually transposing to main lines or to the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense).
- 3.Nf3, the [Anti-Benoni](/openings/english-opening-symmetrical-variation-anti-benoni-variation), avoiding the Benoni and often reaching a Symmetrical English.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- An immediate challenge to White's centre.
- Black chooses between several distinct structures: the Modern Benoni, the Benko and the Czech Benoni.

**Weaknesses**

- After 3.d5 White gains space in the centre.
- White can sidestep the Benoni altogether with 3.Nf3.

### Who should play it

2...c5 suits Black players who want an unbalanced fight against 1.d4 and are happy with Benoni structures, whether sharp (Modern Benoni, Benko) or solid (Czech Benoni).

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does very well between 1400 and 2200: 51.1% against 45.0% at 1400–1799 and 50.0% against 44.6% at 1800–2199. Under 1400 the results are level (48.5% against 48.3%), and from 2200 up White leads 47.4% against 45.3%. In about 37,700 master games White won 40.4%, Black 26.1%, and 33.5% were drawn.
