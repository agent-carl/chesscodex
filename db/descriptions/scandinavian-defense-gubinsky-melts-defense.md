The Gubinsky-Melts Defense is 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qd6, a line of the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense) in which the queen retreats to d6. It is also called the Pytel Variation, after Krzysztof and Bożena Pytel. Michael Melts covered it thoroughly in a book in 2001.

### Reputation

At first sight the queen on d6 looks exposed to a later Nb5 or Bf4, and for many years the move was poorly regarded for that reason. Numerous grandmaster games have since shown 3...Qd6 to be quite playable, and it has been played many times in high-level chess since the mid-1990s. It has grown in popularity over time.

### How White plays against it

White players have found an effective set-up with d4, Nf3, g3, Bg2 and O-O, followed later by Ne5, which gives White a strong, active position.

The other main queen moves on move three are [3...Qa5](/openings/scandinavian-defense-main-line), the main line, and [3...Qd8](/openings/scandinavian-defense-valencian-variation), the Valencian Variation.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are level overall: White wins 47.3% of the 4.5 million games and Black 47.2%. They change sharply with rating. Under 1400 White leads 53.4% to 42.7%, while between 1800 and 2199 Black is ahead, 47.4% against 46.8%, and from 2200 up White leads narrowly, 46.3% against 45.8%. In 5,698 master games over the board White won 36.0%, Black 24.4%, and 39.6% were drawn.
