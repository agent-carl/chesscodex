The Exchange Variation of the [Alekhine Defense](/openings/alekhine-defense) is 1.e4 Nf6 2.e5 Nd5 3.d4 d6 4.c4 Nb6 5.exd6. It is less ambitious than the [Four Pawns Attack](/openings/alekhine-defense-four-pawns-attack), 5.f4: White trades pawns and accepts a more modest space advantage.

### Black's recapture

Black's main decision is how to take back on d6.

- 5...exd6 is the solid choice and leads to a fairly strategic position. It resembles the Exchange French, but Black has ways to unbalance it: ...Nc6 against almost any White set-up, so that a bishop on d3 can be hit by ...Nb4, then castling and ...Be7-f6, and after enough preparation the push ...d5. Black usually develops the bishop by ...Be7 and ...Bf6, because Bg5 can be annoying against a ...g6 and ...Bg7 set-up.
- 5...cxd6 is more ambitious and gives Black more pawns in the centre. Black usually aims at White's pawn on d4, and possibly c4: a fianchetto with ...g6 and ...Bg7, ...Bg4 to remove a knight on f3 that defends d4, and the knights on b6 and c6 bearing down on c4 and d4.
- 5...Qxd6 is possible, since the fork 6.c5 can be met by 6...Qe6+, but it is considered inferior because Black sooner or later has to deal with that threat.

### The Voronezh Variation

A popular answer to 5...cxd6 is the [Voronezh Variation](/openings/alekhine-defense-exchange-variation-voronezh-variation), 6.Nc3 g6 7.Be3 Bg7 8.Rc1 O-O 9.b3, named after the Russian city where Grigory Sanakoev and others pioneered it. White delays kingside development so that Black has fewer targets, for example no knight on f3 to hit with ...Bg4. The main line is 9...e5 10.dxe5 dxe5 11.Qxd8 Rxd8 12.c5 N6d7, a forced retreat because 12...Nd5?? loses the knight to the pin 13.Rd1. John Emms recommended the Voronezh and Nigel Davies called it a big problem for Black, which led many players to choose 5...exd6 instead.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 48.3% of the 2.5 million games and White 46.2%. Under 1400 White leads 53.8% to 43.2%, but between 1800 and 2199 Black is ahead, 48.9% against 45.7%. From 2200 up the results are almost level, 46.5% for White against 46.0%. In 3,915 master games over the board White won 38.8%, Black 25.3%, and 35.9% were drawn.
