The Main Line of the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense) (1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qa5) is the most popular way to continue after the queen recapture: attacked by 3.Nc3, the queen goes to a5.

### The idea

3...Qa5 is considered the main line and remains the most popular of Black's queen moves. Black follows with ...c6, ...Nf6 and ...Bf5, putting the light-squared bishop outside the pawn chain before playing ...e6. The resulting structure, with pawns on c6 and e6, resembles the [Caro-Kann Defense](/openings/caro-kann-defense), which is why many Caro-Kann players have taken up this line. Both sides can castle on either side.

### Main lines

- 4.d4, the usual move. The main line runs 4...c6 5.Nf3 Nf6 6.Bc4 Bf5 7.Bd2 e6, often reached by other move orders, when White chooses between the aggressive 8.Qe2 and the quiet 8.0-0. 4.d4 Nf6 is the [Mieses Variation](/openings/scandinavian-defense-main-line-mieses-variation) in this database.
- 4.Nf3 and 4.Bc4 often transpose to the same positions.
- 4.g3, a fianchetto set-up aimed at Black's queenside: the main line is 4...Nf6 5.Bg2 c6 6.Nf3, intending 0-0, Rb1 and b4–b5. Anand, Baadur Jobava, Gyula Sax and Francisco Vallejo Pons have played it.
- 4.b4!?, the [Leonhardt Gambit](/openings/scandinavian-defense-main-line-leonhardt-gambit), also called the Kotrč–Mieses Gambit. Paul Saladin Leonhardt introduced it to master play against Jacques Mieses in 1907, and Lasker, Capablanca and Keres have played it. With correct play White should not have enough compensation, but that can be hard to prove over the board.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- A solid Caro-Kann-like structure with Black's bishop developed actively.
- Flexible castling for both sides.

**Weaknesses**

- The early queen moves cost Black time, and White develops with gain of tempo.

### Who should play it

The Main Line suits Black players who want a solid, structurally sound answer to 1.e4 and are comfortable with Caro-Kann-type positions.

### The numbers

On Lichess White leads under 1400, 50.4% against 46.2%, but Black is ahead at 1400–1799 (48.4% against 47.7%) and at 1800–2199 (48.0% against 47.0%). From 2200 up White leads again, 47.5% against 45.6%. In about 7,800 master games White won 38.6%, Black 23.8%, and 37.6% were drawn.
