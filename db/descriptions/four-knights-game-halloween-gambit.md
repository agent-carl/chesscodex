The Halloween Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Nxe5: in the normally quiet [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game) White gives up a whole knight for a single pawn. It is also known as the Müller–Schulze Gambit or the Leipzig Gambit.

### Origins

The theoretician Oskar Cordel reported in 1888 that club players in Leipzig used the opening with dangerous effect, although he did not believe it was sound. Their name for it, "Gambit Müller und Schulze", does not refer to real players: it is a joking German equivalent of "Smith and Jones". The modern name comes from the German player Steffen Jakob, who explained that many players are shocked, as if by a Halloween mask, when they expect a boring Four Knights and are faced with Nxe5.

### The idea

White wants to take the centre with pawns and drive back Black's knights. After 4...Nxe5 White usually plays 5.d4, and Black's attacked knight retreats to g6 or c6.

### Main lines

- 5...Ng6 6.e5, chasing the f6-knight. After 6...Ng8 7.Bc4, former World Champion Max Euwe recommended 7...d5 8.Bxd5 c6, claiming a decisive advantage for Black, while Larry Kaufman prefers 8...N8e7. Kaufman calls 5...Ng6 good but unnecessarily risky. According to Eric Schiller, 6...Bb4 is more logical than holding on to the extra piece: after 7.exf6 Qxf6 Black has the better game.
- 5...Nc6 6.d5, chasing the knight again. After 6...Ne5 7.f4 Ng6 8.e5 Ng8 9.d6 cxd6 10.exd6, White's attack is very dangerous and likely to win in practical play: the threat of Nb5–c7 is hard to meet. With precise defence Black may still keep the extra material, but White has reached the aims of the opening.
- 5...Nc6 6.d5 Bb4, Piński's move. Kaufman wrote in 2004 that 7.dxc6 Nxe4 8.Qd4 Qe7, which he credits to the Polish IM Jan Piński, refutes the gambit. In 2020 he added that in one critical line White has no compensation for his poor pawn structure.

### Who should play it

The Halloween Gambit is for players who want to shock a Four Knights opponent. Its strength is practical: an unprepared Black player can be overrun quickly, while against Piński's 6...Bb4 theory gives Black the advantage.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.6% of the 680,000 games and Black 43.8%, despite the piece. The split stays similar from 1400 to 2199, around 52.5% against 44%. Under 1400 it is 50.7% against 45.6%, and from 2200 up 49.0% against 45.3%. There are too few master games over the board, 16, to say anything.
