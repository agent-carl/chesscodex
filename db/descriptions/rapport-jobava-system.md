The Rapport-Jobava System (1.d4 d5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.Bf4), also known as the Jobava London, is an aggressive relative of the [London System](/openings/queens-pawn-game-london-system). White develops the queen's knight to c3 instead of d2. That one change brings ideas such as Nb5 and a quicker e4, and often leads to long castling and a kingside pawn storm.

### Origins

The moves 2.Nc3 and 3.Bf4 were played long before the system had a name. James Mason played the line as early as 1882, but it was not treated as an independent opening. In the early 2000s the Georgian grandmaster Baadur Jobava began to use it often in rapid and blitz, and he and the Hungarian grandmaster Richard Rapport developed its ideas further. Both played aggressive versions with early pawn storms and long castling, which is why the system carries their names.

### The idea

With Nc3 and Bf4, White puts pressure on c7 and prepares Nb5, while keeping the option of e3, Qd2, O-O-O and f3 with g4 and h4. In many lines White castles queenside and attacks Black's king directly. The knight on c3 also supports a quick e4, so the game is sharper than in the ordinary London.

### Main lines

- 3...e6 and ...Bd6: Black challenges the f4-bishop and keeps a solid centre.
- 3...c5: Black strikes at the centre at once. Lines with Nb5 and e3 become sharp.
- 3...Bf5, a natural developing move, and 3...a6, which takes the b5-square from White's knight.
- The system can also start with 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Bf4.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Aggressive and flexible, with less theory than the main lines after 2.c4.
- Many opponents prepared for the normal London are surprised by it.
- Clear attacking plans with long castling and kingside pawns.

**Weaknesses**

- The c-pawn is blocked by the knight, so White has less central control than in the Queen's Gambit.
- Long castling and pawn storms are risky if Black counterattacks first.
- Accurate play gives Black a fine game.

### Who should play it

The Jobava London suits attacking players who like the London setup but want sharper, more dynamic positions. It is effective against opponents who play automatic London-style replies. It is less effective against well-prepared players who hit the centre with ...c5 and ...e6 and counterattack on the queenside.

### The numbers

On Lichess the Jobava London scores well at club level. Below 1400 White scores 53% and Black 43%. From 1400 to 2200 it is 50–51.5% against 44.5–45%. Above 2200 it is close to 50% against 43%. In master games over the board (about 4,000 games), White wins 30%, Black 28% and 42% are drawn.
