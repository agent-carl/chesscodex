In the Exchange Variation of the [Caro-Kann Defense](/openings/caro-kann-defense) (1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.exd5 cxd5) White exchanges in the centre on move three. The game can stay quiet, with a structure resembling a [Queen's Gambit Declined](/openings/queens-gambit-declined) with colours reversed, or turn sharp if White plays 4.c4, the Panov Attack.

### Origins

The quiet main line 4.Bd3 Nc6 5.c3 Nf6 6.Bf4 Bg4 7.Qb3 was tried by Bobby Fischer and is considered to give White a slightly better game. The sharper 4.c4 is named after Vasily Panov and world champion Mikhail Botvinnik.

### The idea

With 4.Bd3 White develops and stops Black's bishop from coming to f5. The pawn structure resembles a Carlsbad structure with colours reversed, so many strategic ideas are those of the [Queen's Gambit Declined Exchange Variation](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-exchange-variation).

The Panov Attack usually leads to positions with an isolated queen's pawn on d4: White gets rapid development, a grip on e5 and chances on the kingside in return for the long-term weakness of the isolated pawn.

### Main lines

- [3...cxd5](/openings/caro-kann-defense-exchange-variation-2), the usual recapture, then:
  - 4.Bd3 Nc6 5.c3 Nf6 6.Bf4, the [Rubinstein Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-exchange-variation-rubinstein-variation), the main line, with 6...Bg4 7.Qb3;
  - 4.c4, the [Panov Attack](/openings/caro-kann-defense-panov-attack). Its main line is 4...Nf6 5.Nc3 e6 6.Nf3, when Black chooses between 6...Bb4, often transposing to the [Nimzo-Indian Defense](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense), and 6...Be7;
  - [4.Nf3 Nc6](/openings/caro-kann-defense-exchange-variation-4) and [4.Bf4](/openings/caro-kann-defense-exchange-variation-3) are other ways to develop.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- White chooses the character of the game: a calm Carlsbad-type middlegame or a sharp isolated-pawn position.
- The 4.Bd3 main line is considered to give White a slightly better game.

**Weaknesses**

- In the Panov the isolated d-pawn is a long-term structural weakness.

### Who should play it

The Exchange suits White players who like clear, structural middlegames, while the Panov Attack is a good choice for those comfortable with isolated-queen's-pawn positions.
