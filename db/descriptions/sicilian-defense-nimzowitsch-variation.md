The Nimzowitsch Variation, also called the Nimzowitsch-Rubinstein Variation, is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nf6, a less common second move in the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). The knight attacks e4 at once, and the play that follows bears some similarity to the [Alekhine Defense](/openings/alekhine-defense).

### Main lines

White's strongest reply is to chase the knight with [3.e5](/openings/sicilian-defense-nimzowitsch-variation-advance-variation) Nd5 4.Nc3. Black then has two choices:

- [4...Nxc3](/openings/sicilian-defense-nimzowitsch-variation-exchange-variation) 5.dxc3.
- 4...e6, the main line: 5.Nxd5 exd5 6.d4 Nc6 7.dxc5 Bxc5 8.Qxd5 Qb6 9.Bc4! Bxf2+ 10.Ke2 O-O 11.Rf1 Bc5 12.Ng5 Nd4+ 13.Kd1, with sharp play that favours White. Instead of 8...Qb6, Black also plays 8...d6 9.exd6 Qb6.

### A trap after 4...Nxc3

After 4...Nxc3 5.dxc3 the move 5...b6?, which Nimzowitsch himself played and recommended, loses to 6.e6! f6 7.Ne5!

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 48.8% of the 1.5 million games and White 46.0%. The rating split is sharp: under 1400 White leads 53.0% to 43.3%, while between 1800 and 2199 Black is ahead 50.2% to 44.7%, and from 2200 up 48.2% to 43.9%. In 3,570 master games over the board White won 37.3%, Black 24.8%, and 37.9% were drawn.
