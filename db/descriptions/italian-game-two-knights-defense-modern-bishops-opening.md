The Modern Bishop's Opening (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.d3), also called the Closed Variation of the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense), is White's most common answer to 3...Nf6. Instead of attacking f7 with 4.Ng5 or opening the centre with 4.d4, White calmly defends e4.

### Origins

The name comes from the fact that the position is often reached from the [Bishop's Opening](/openings/bishops-opening) by 2.Bc4 Nf6 3.d3 Nc6 4.Nf3. The move became popular in the 1980s, and John Nunn among others has played it.

### The idea

With d3 White avoids the tactical battles of the other Two Knights lines and heads for a positional game. If White follows with c3 and brings the bishop back to c2 via b3, the position takes on some features of the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez).

### Main lines

- 4...Bc5, the most common reply, transposing to the [Giuoco Pianissimo](/openings/italian-game-giuoco-pianissimo-normal).
- 4...Be7, a position also often reached from the [Hungarian Defense](/openings/italian-game-hungarian-defense) when White does not play d4.
- 4...h6, intending ...d6. Black should not play 4...d6 at once because of 5.Ng5, when 5...d5 transposes to the Knight Attack with White having the extra move d3; Black often ends up playing ...g5 or ...g6.
- 4...d5, much less common, a dynamic but risky line.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- A solid, positional set-up without forcing tactical lines.
- With c3 and Bb3–c2 the position can take on features of the Ruy Lopez.

**Weaknesses**

- White gives up the attempt to attack f7 directly or to open the centre early.
- Black has several good replies, such as 4...Bc5, 4...Be7 and 4...h6.

### Who should play it

4.d3 suits White players who like the Italian Game but prefer slow manoeuvring to the sharp lines after 4.Ng5 or 4.d4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White leads below 2200: 51.8% against 44.4% under 1400, 51.3% against 44.7% at 1400–1799 and 49.3% against 45.7% at 1800–2199. From 2200 up it is 47.4% against 45.6%. In about 17,800 master games White won 32.0%, Black 23.6%, and 44.4% were drawn.
