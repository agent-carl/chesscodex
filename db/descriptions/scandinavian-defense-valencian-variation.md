The Valencian Variation is 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qd8, a line of the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense) in which the queen goes all the way back to its starting square instead of [3...Qa5](/openings/scandinavian-defense-main-line), the main line.

### History

The retreat 3...Qd8 appears in Scachs d'amor, a Catalan poem of the 15th century. With that in mind, Sergi Núñez de Arenas and Francisco Rubio proposed the name Valencian Variation in a 2023 article for the Spanish magazine Peón de Rey.

Before the 20th century 3...Qd8 was often considered the main line, and Howard Staunton called it best in his Chess-Player's Handbook. It was gradually superseded by 3...Qa5. In the 1960s it had something of a revival after David Bronstein played it against Andrija Fuderer in 1959, although Bronstein lost that game.

### Main lines

- 4.d4 Nf6 is the older line, the one Bronstein played. After [5.Nf3 Bg4](/openings/scandinavian-defense-valencian-variation-main-line) the game reaches this site's main line of the variation.
- 4.d4 g6, with a later ...Ng8-h6, is a fianchetto system explored by Karl Robatsch and other grandmasters. Its reputation suffered after Bobby Fischer beat Robatsch at the 1962 Olympiad, a game later published in My 60 Memorable Games.

Fischer also beat William Addison in this line in 1970. The variation as a whole remains playable, though it is considered somewhat passive. It is played particularly by the IMs John Bartholomew and Daniel Lowinger and the GMs David Garcia Ilundain and Nikola Djukić.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.1% of the 17 million games and Black 44.5%. White's lead is largest under 1400, 52.9% against 42.7%; from 2200 up it is 49.3% against 42.4%. In 1,437 master games over the board White won 39.2%, Black 22.3%, and 38.4% were drawn.
