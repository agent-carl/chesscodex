The Botvinnik Variation of the [Semi-Slav Defense](/openings/semi-slav-defense) is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 e6 5.Bg5 dxc4 6.e4. It is probably one of the most complicated of all chess openings, with theory stretching past move thirty in some lines. Black takes on c4 at once, White takes the centre with 6.e4, and Black defends the extra pawn with 6...b5.

### History

Mikhail Botvinnik introduced it in the 1945 USSR vs. USA radio match against Arnold Denker, though Klaus Junge is credited as the actual inventor. Alex Yermolinsky has an excellent record with White, and Alexei Shirov has been Black's chief proponent.

### The main line

The main line runs 6...b5 7.e5 h6 8.Bh4 g5 9.Nxg5 hxg5 10.Bxg5 Nbd7. White will regain the piece and come out a pawn up, but Black soon completes development and gets great dynamic compensation. White fianchettoes the king's bishop and castles kingside; Black plays ...c5 and ...Qb6, castles queenside, and can attack in the centre or on either flank.

The main continuation is 11.g3 Bb7 12.Bg2 Qb6 13.exf6 O-O-O 14.O-O c5 15.d5 b4, a key position. White now chooses between 16.Na4, the most challenging move, and 16.Rb1, which stops Black from taking the knight on c3. These positions are highly dynamic and favour the better-prepared player first of all, although objectively White may have a slight advantage.

### The numbers

On Lichess the line is balanced: White and Black each win 47.6% of the 249,742 games. White does much better under 1400, 57.3% against 39.3%. Masters draw almost half the time: in 3,937 games over the board 48.4% were drawn, White won 33.2% and Black 18.4%.
