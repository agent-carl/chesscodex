The Cunningham Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 Be7, a reply to the [King's Knight's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kings-knights-gambit). The bishop threatens a check on h4 that can permanently stop White from castling, and if White has not yet developed the king's bishop, Ke2 would be forced, hemming the bishop in.

### 4.Nc3

A sample line is 4.Nc3 Bh4+ 5.Ke2 d5 6.Nxd5 Nf6 7.Nxf6+ Qxf6 8.d4 Bg4 9.Qd2. White has strong central control with pawns on d4 and e4, while Black relies on the discomfort of White's king for compensation.

### 4.Bc4

To avoid Ke2, White's most popular reply is 4.Bc4. The same line is often reached from the [Bishop's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit).

- 4...Bh4+ forces 5.Kf1, unless White chooses the wild [Bertin Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-cunningham-defense-bertin-gambit), also called the Three Pawns' Gambit: 5.g3 fxg3 6.O-O gxh2+ 7.Kh1, played in the 19th century.
- 4...Nf6 5.e5 Ng4, known as the Modern Cunningham, is common in modern practice: Black simply develops. 5...Ne4, the Euwe Variation, is underexplored but seemingly playable and has a number of trappy ideas.

### The numbers

On Lichess the line is balanced: White wins 48.4% of the 2.6 million games and Black 48.3%. Black does better under 1400, 53.1% against 44.6%. In 170 master games over the board Black won 38.8%, White 30.6%, and 30.6% were drawn.
