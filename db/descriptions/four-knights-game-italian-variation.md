The Italian Variation of the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game) (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Nc3) defends the e4-pawn with the knight after 3...Nf6. The same position arises from the Four Knights with 4.Bc4, which was popular in the 1880s.

### The idea

The trouble with 4.Nc3 is the fork trick: Black can take on e4 anyway with 4...Nxe4!, and after 5.Nxe4 d5 regains the piece. Opening theory regards the line as inferior for White; Pinski calls it inferior and International Master Larry Evans an outright mistake. Even so, Nigel Short used it successfully against Antoaneta Stefanova in 2003.

### Main lines

- 4...Nxe4 5.Nxe4 d5, the main line. It continues 6.Bd3 dxe4 7.Bxe4 Bd6; 6...Nb4 is also playable. 6.Bxd5? and 6.Bb5?! are weak.
- 4...Nxe4 5.Bxf7+, the [Noa Gambit](/openings/four-knights-game-italian-variation-noa-gambit), superficially attractive but it gives Black the bishop pair and the centre: after 5...Kxf7 6.Nxe4 d5 White's attack peters out.
- 4...Nxe4 5.0-0, closely related to the Boden–Kieseritzky Gambit, which White often has in mind with 4.Nc3. The gambit is not well regarded by theory, but it can offer good practical chances, especially in blitz.
- 4...Bc5, avoiding the complications and keeping the symmetry; it transposes to the quiet [Giuoco Pianissimo](/openings/italian-game-giuoco-pianissimo). A better move order for White to reach that position is 3...Bc5 4.Nc3 Nf6.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Natural development.
- The Boden–Kieseritzky ideas can give White good practical chances, especially in blitz.

**Weaknesses**

- The fork trick 4...Nxe4! wins back the piece and frees Black's game.
- Regarded by theory as inferior for White.

### Who should play it

Because theory regards 4.Nc3 as inferior, it is mainly a route for White into the Boden–Kieseritzky Gambit. Black players should know the fork trick.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black's results improve sharply with rating: White leads 49.8% against 45.8% under 1400 and 48.2% against 47.6% at 1400–1799, but Black is clearly ahead from 1800 up, 49.5% against 45.4% at 1800–2199 and 51.8% against 41.4% from 2200 up. There are only a few hundred master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
