The Advance Variation of the [Winawer](/openings/french-defense-winawer-variation) is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e5, White's normal answer to the pin on c3. White clarifies the centre for the moment, gains space and hopes to show that Black's bishop is misplaced on b4. The Winawer, named after Szymon Winawer and pioneered by Aron Nimzowitsch and Mikhail Botvinnik, is one of the main systems of the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense).

### The main line

Play usually continues 4...c5 5.a3 Bxc3+ 6.bxc3 Ne7. White now has doubled pawns on the queenside, which are the basis of Black's counterplay, but they also strengthen White's centre and open the b-file. White has more space on the kingside, where Black is weaker than usual after giving up the dark-squared bishop, and together with the bishop pair this gives White attacking chances, though the long-term features of the pawn structure favour Black. Instead of 6...Ne7 Black can play 6...Qc7, 6...Qa5, a recently popular choice, or occasionally 6...Nc6.

### White's seventh move

- 7.Qg4 is the most common move and invites great tactical complications, since Black's bishop no longer defends g7. Black can give up two kingside pawns with 7...Qc7 8.Qxg7 Rg8 9.Qxh7 cxd4 and destroy White's centre in return, the [Poisoned Pawn Variation](/openings/french-defense-winawer-variation-poisoned-pawn-variation), or play 7...O-O, or the more recent 7...Kf8. Judit Polgár is an expert on 7.Qg4, and Garry Kasparov has also played it.
- 7.Nf3 and 7.a4 are good positional alternatives. 7.a4 prepares Ba3 and stops Black from attacking c2 with ...Qa5–a4 or ...Bd7–a4; Vasily Smyslov and Bobby Fischer both used it with success.
- 7.h4 is a more aggressive try, aiming to push the pawn to h6 or to bring the rook into play via h3.

Against the quieter lines Black can often attack with ...O-O-O, usually combined with ...c4 to close the queenside and ...f6 to open the kingside.

### Other fifth moves

After 4...c5 5.a3 Black can keep the pin with 5...Ba5, sometimes called the Armenian Variation because players from that country, most notably Rafael Vaganian, have enriched its theory. White usually answers 6.b4 cxb4 and then 7.Qg4 or 7.Nb5. White can also deviate on move 5 with 5.Qg4, 5.dxc5, 5.Nf3 or 5.Bd2. On move 4, 4...Ne7 usually transposes to the main line, while 4...b6 and 4...Qd7 are considered better for White.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.1% of the 2.8 million games and Black 45.7%. Under 1400 White's lead is larger, 53.6% against 42.8%. In 24,645 master games over the board White won 38.8%, Black 23.3%, and 37.9% were drawn.
