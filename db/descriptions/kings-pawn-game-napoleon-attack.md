The Napoleon Attack, or Napoleon Opening, is 1.e4 e5 2.Qf3. Like the [Wayward Queen Attack](/openings/kings-pawn-game-wayward-queen-attack), 2.Qh5, it brings the queen out early in the hope of Scholar's Mate, for example 2...Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.Qxf7#, but Black can easily avoid the attack.

### Origins

The opening is named after Napoleon Bonaparte, who loved chess but is said to have been a mediocre player. The name came into use after mid-19th-century publications reported that he played this opening in an 1809 game he lost to the Turk, a fake chess automaton operated at the time by the master and theoretician Johann Allgaier.

### The idea

The Napoleon is a weak opening. It develops the queen too early, where Black can attack her with gain of time, and it takes away the best square of White's king's knight, f3. Unlike 2.Qh5, 2.Qf3 does nothing to hinder Black's development, so Black can develop normally.

### The numbers

On Lichess the two sides are almost even: White wins 48.1% of the 2.1 million games and Black 47.5%. The split stays close at every rating up to 2199, for example 48.0% against 47.6% between 1800 and 2199. From 2200 up White wins 50.9% against 43.2%. There are too few master games over the board, 16, to say anything.
