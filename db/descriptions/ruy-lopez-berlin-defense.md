The Berlin Defense (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6) is the most solid answer to the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez). Black counterattacks the e4-pawn instead of playing 3...a6, and in the main line heads for a queenless endgame nicknamed the "Berlin Wall". It is known as one of the most reliable drawing weapons in chess.

### Origins

The Berlin Defense was first analysed in depth in the 19th century, and it takes its name from the Berlin players who examined its variations. Emanuel Lasker used it often throughout his career. Its modern fame comes from the 2000 Classical World Championship: Vladimir Kramnik used it in games 1, 3, 9 and 13 against Garry Kasparov, all drawn, and won the title. Magnus Carlsen and Viswanathan Anand both played it in their 2013 World Championship match.

### The idea

In the main line, 4.O-O Nxe4 5.d4 Nd6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.dxe5 Nf5 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8, the queens come off at move eight. Black has doubled c-pawns and the king in the centre, and can no longer castle. In return Black has the bishop pair. White has a healthy kingside pawn majority. The general view is that Black can hold this endgame with accurate play.

### Main lines

- [Berlin Wall endgame](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense-lhermet-variation-berlin-wall-defense) after 4.O-O Nxe4 5.d4 Nd6: the main line described above.
- After [4.O-O Nxe4](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense-rio-gambit-accepted), Black can also avoid the early queen trade with 5.d4 Be7, the Rio de Janeiro Variation.
- [4.O-O Bc5](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense-beverwijk-variation): a sharper, tactically rich line.
- [Anti-Berlin](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense-anti-berlin-variation) 4.d3: White protects e4 and avoids the endgame, heading for a slow manoeuvring game.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very solid: one of the best ways for Black to neutralise 1.e4 at the top level.
- The endgame is well understood and hard for White to win.
- It forced a change in top-level chess: many White players moved to the Anti-Berlin or the Italian Game.

**Weaknesses**

- Very drawish, so Black's winning chances are small against a careful opponent.
- The endgame requires real understanding: at club level, a misplaced king or weak pawns can lose.
- The Anti-Berlin 4.d3 leads to long manoeuvring games that Black must also know.

### Who should play it

The Berlin suits solid, endgame-minded players who want a reliable defence against the Ruy Lopez, and players who need a safe result with Black. It is effective against aggressive 1.e4 players who want a middlegame attack, because the queens disappear early. It is less suitable for players who need to win with Black, or who don't enjoy endgames.

### The numbers

The Berlin's numbers show the gap between club and master chess. On Lichess below 2200, White scores 51.5–52.5% and Black 43–43.5%. Above 2200 it is 48% against 43%. Among masters over the board it is the "Berlin Wall": 65% of games are drawn, White wins 21% and Black 14%.
