The Adler Variation of the [Budapest Gambit](/openings/indian-defense-budapest-defense) is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 Ng4 4.Nf3. With this natural move White is ready to return the e5-pawn in order to develop quickly. The first known Budapest Gambit game was a casual game between Mór Adler and Géza Maróczy in Budapest on 5 March 1896.

### White's plans

White aims to control the d5-square and the long a1–h8 diagonal, and to expand on the queenside with c4–c5 or on the kingside with f2–f4–f5–f6, as appropriate.

### The main line: 4...Bc5

Black's main move is 4...Bc5, attacking f2 and forcing 5.e3, which blocks in White's bishop on c1. After 5...Nc6 White will not have enough pieces to hold the e5-pawn in the long run. The minor line 4...Nc6 delays the bishop's development so that it can go to the long diagonal instead.

An important decision for White is whether to play a2–a3, which prepares b2–b4. After 5.e3 Nc6 6.a3 Black typically answers 6...a5, and in the 1980s it was found that this suits Black, because the rook on a8 can then join the attack along the sixth rank. 6.a3 has gone from being the main continuation to being considered a mistake. Without a3, a typical line is 6.Be2 O-O 7.O-O Re8 8.Nc3 Ngxe5 9.Nxe5 Nxe5: Black has regained the pawn, White has more space in the centre, and Black can attack the kingside with rook lifts.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are level: White wins 47.4% of the 2.1 million games and Black 47.3%. Black is ahead below 1800, White from 2200 up (48.8% against 43.9%). In 942 master games over the board White won 40.6%, Black 24.4%, and 35.0% were drawn.
