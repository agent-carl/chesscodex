The Abbazia Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 d5 4.exd5 Nf6, the most common continuation of the [Modern Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-modern-defense) to the [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit). The name comes from a tournament held in Abbazia in 1912 in which every game had to be a King's Gambit Accepted, and it is also used for the whole Modern Defense, 3...d5.

### The idea

Black does not try to keep the extra pawn. The plan is to gain piece play and fight for the initiative, much as in the [Falkbeer Countergambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit). The position before 4...Nf6 can in fact arise from the Falkbeer by transposition: 2...d5 3.exd5 exf4 4.Nf3.

Several publications have recommended this line as an easy way for Black to equalise, and it was once seen as a critical test of the gambit. White's extra central pawn and active pieces are now considered to give White a slight advantage.

### Main lines

With 4...Nf6 Black attacks the pawn on d5.

- 5.Bb5+ is White's usual counterattack. The continuation 5...c6 6.dxc6 bxc6 7.Bc4 Nd5 is known as the Botvinnik Variation.
- Otherwise White defends the pawn with 5.c4, 5.Bc4 or 5.Nc3.

Instead of 4...Nf6 Black sometimes plays 4...Qxd5, which resembles the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense), or 4...Bd6 or 4...c6.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 49.7% of the 944,000 games and White 45.5%. Under 1400 White is ahead, 50.4% against 46.9%, but between 1800 and 2199 Black leads 50.2% to 45.0%, and from 2200 up 47.8% to 45.2%. The 639 master games over the board split almost evenly: White won 28.2%, Black 29.1%, and 42.7% were drawn.
