The Fajarowicz Variation is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 Ne4, the less common branch of the [Budapest Gambit](/openings/indian-defense-budapest-defense). Black makes no immediate effort to regain the gambit pawn and relies on active piece play and tactical tricks, such as ...Bb4+ at an awkward moment for White, ...Qf6 hitting b2 and f2, and pressure on d3.

### History

The variation is said to have come from the chess circles of Leipzig. The first important game was H. Steiner–Fajarowicz at the Wiesbaden tournament of 1928.

### 4.a3

4.a3 stops both the bishop check and the knight jump to b4, and prepares Qc2 to undermine Black's knight. Lalic and de Firmian both consider it White's best move, and de Firmian assesses it as leading to a large advantage for White. Lalic considers 4...b6 the best reply, so that Qc2 can be met by ...Bb7. White cannot win a piece with 5.Qd5, because of the trick 5...Nc5 6.Qxa8 Bb7 7.Qxa7 Nc6, trapping the queen.

### 4.Nf3

4.Nf3 develops a piece and covers d2. After 4...Bb4+ 5.Nbd2 Nc6 6.a3 Black can easily get confused by the move order: 6...Nxd2 7.axb4 Nxf3+ 8.gxf3 Nxe5 9.Rg1 Qe7 10.Ra3 gives White a strong initiative.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are almost level: White wins 47.2% of the 590,575 games and Black 47.0%. Masters have done much better with White: in 430 games over the board White won 52.3%, Black 15.8%, and 31.9% were drawn.
