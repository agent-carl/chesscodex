The Bongcloud Attack is 1.e4 e5 2.Ke2, a joke opening known from internet chess humour. It breaks every normal principle of opening play: White gives up castling, blocks both the queen and the light-squared bishop, exposes the king, and neither develops a piece nor fights for the centre. The name is also used for other openings in which a player moves the king on move two.

### Origins

The name is thought to come either from a Chess.com user "Lenny_Bongcloud", who played it with little success, or from a bong, implying that one would have to be intoxicated to see the opening as a serious strategy. Andrew Fabbro's joke manual Winning with the Bongcloud spread it further.

### At the top level

Hikaru Nakamura has played it in online blitz. He streamed himself playing it on a new Chess.com account and reached a 3000 rating. In the 2018 Speed Chess Championship he played it three times against Levon Aronian, winning one game and losing two, and in 2019 he beat Vladimir Dobrov and Wesley So with it. In September 2020 he won with it against Jeffery Xiong in the St. Louis Rapid and Blitz, played on Lichess.

On 15 March 2021 Magnus Carlsen opened with the Bongcloud against Nakamura at the Magnus Carlsen Invitational, and Nakamura copied it with 2...Ke7, the "Double Bongcloud". Both players had already qualified for the knockout stage, and they drew by repetition, shuffling their kings back and forth.

The first use in a FIDE-rated game between top grandmasters came at the Chess.com Global Championship finals in November 2022, an in-person rapid event. Trailing 3–0 against Nakamura, Jan-Krzysztof Duda played 1.e3 and 2.Ke2, and lost.

### Why play it

There is no chess reason. The only argument is psychological: after Carlsen beat Wesley So in a 2020 blitz game that opened 1.f3 and 2.Kf2, another version of the Bongcloud, So said that losing after such an opening had a crushing effect.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 57.7% of the 49,000 games and White 37.0%, with 5.2% draws.
