The Falkbeer Countergambit (1.e4 e5 2.f4 d5) declines the [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit) by striking back in the centre at once. It is named after the 19th-century Austrian master Ernst Falkbeer.

### The idea

Black ignores the f4-pawn and counterattacks e4. White usually answers 3.exd5, since 3.fxe5? Qh4+ 4.g3 Qxe4+ wins White's rook. Black then either gives up a pawn for quick and easy development or transposes to other King's Gambit lines.

### Main lines

- 3.exd5, the [usual reply](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-accepted):
  - 3...e4, the traditional line ([Staunton Line](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-staunton-line) in this database), sacrificing a pawn for development. It was once considered good for Black and scored well, but White gets some advantage with 4.d3! ([Charousek Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-charousek-gambit)), and the line fell out of favour after the 1930s;
  - 3...exf4, transposing to the [Modern Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-modern-defense) of the King's Gambit Accepted;
  - 3...c6, the more modern [Nimzowitsch-Marshall Countergambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-nimzowitsch-marshall-countergambit), aiming for early piece activity; White gets the pawn back with a better structure and endgame prospects. The main line is 4.Nc3 exf4 5.Nf3 Bd6 6.d4 Ne7 7.dxc6 Nbxc6; 4.Qe2 is another common answer.
- 3.Nf3, the [Blackburne Attack](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-blackburne-attack), typically 3...dxe4 4.Nxe5.
- 3.Nc3, the [Milner-Barry Variation](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit-milner-barry-variation).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- An active, principled answer to the King's Gambit.
- In the traditional 3...e4 line Black gets quick and easy development for the pawn.

**Weaknesses**

- The traditional 3...e4 is answered well by 4.d3, which gives White some advantage.
- In the 3...c6 lines White keeps a better pawn structure.

### Who should play it

The Falkbeer suits Black players who meet the King's Gambit with a direct central counter and prefer active piece play to defending an extra pawn.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results move in Black's favour as ratings rise: White leads 49.7% against 47.1% under 1400, but Black is ahead 48.7% against 48.1% at 1400–1799, 49.6% against 46.3% at 1800–2199 and 47.7% against 46.3% from 2200 up. There are fewer than a thousand master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
