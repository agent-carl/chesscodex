The Modern Defense (1.e4 g6) is a flexible hypermodern reply to 1.e4. Black fianchettoes the king's bishop at once and lets White build the "ideal" centre with pawns on d4 and e4, planning to attack and undermine it later instead of occupying it.

### Origins

The opening is also known as the Robatsch Defense, after the Austrian master Karl Robatsch. In 1965 the tenth edition of *Modern Chess Openings* grouped it with the Pirc as the "Pirc–Robatsch Defense". The 1972 book *The Modern Defence* by Raymond Keene and George Botterill helped popularise it. The Canadian grandmaster Duncan Suttles was one of its leading exponents, and British grandmasters Nigel Davies and Colin McNab, Judit Polgár and Magnus Carlsen have played it. Carlsen calls one variant the "Norwegian Rat".

### The idea

Black plays ...g6, ...Bg7 and ...d6, and delays ...Nf6. That is the main difference from the [Pirc Defense](/openings/pirc-defense). Because the knight stays on g8, Black keeps extra options such as ...c6, ...b5, ...a6 or ...Nd7 and ...e5, and White cannot build the same attacking setups as against the Pirc. The downside is that White also gets extra options, such as c2–c3 to blunt the g7-bishop, or c4 to gain even more space.

### Main lines

- [Standard line](/openings/modern-defense-standard-defense) 2.d4 Bg7 3.Nc3 d6, with continuations such as [4.f4](/openings/modern-defense-pseudo-austrian-attack) (for example 4...c6 5.Nf3 Bg4) and [4.Nf3](/openings/modern-defense-two-knights-variation).
- [Averbakh System](/openings/modern-defense-averbakh-system) with an early c4, where the game often resembles a King's Indian.
- [Three Pawns Attack](/openings/modern-defense-three-pawns-attack) 3.f4: White grabs as much space as possible.
- The "Monkey's Bum", an early Bc4 and Qf3 line discovered by IM Nigel Povah in the 1970s, and the Norwegian Defense 2...Nf6 3.e5 Nh5.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very flexible, with less forcing theory than most defences to 1.e4.
- It provokes White into aggressive setups that can become overextended.
- Rich, unbalanced middlegames with chances for both sides.

**Weaknesses**

- White gets a big centre and plenty of space. If Black's counterattack is late, Black is squeezed.
- White's extra options (c3, c4, early f4 or Be3 and Qd2) require Black to know several structures.
- It is played less often than the main defences at the top level.

### Who should play it

The Modern suits creative, counterattacking players who like to keep options open and are comfortable in cramped positions. It is effective against players who overextend or play on autopilot. It is less comfortable against patient opponents who build a solid centre and wait.

### The numbers

On Lichess the Modern is balanced: White scores 48.5–49% and Black 46–47.5% at every band below 2200, and 47% against 46% above it. The master numbers are striking: over the board, White wins 36%, Black 31%, and only 33% are drawn. It is one of the most decisive defences to 1.e4 on this site, with one of the best winning percentages for Black.
