The King's Indian Attack is a system for White built with Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2 and e4. It is the same setup Black uses in the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense), but with an extra move. On this site the name covers 1.Nf3 d5 2.g3, but the system can be reached from many move orders, including against the French and the Sicilian.

### Origins

The King's Indian Attack is named after the King's Indian Defence, whose setup White adopts. It emerged in the 1950s, when grandmasters who appreciated Black's King's Indian system began to use it with the extra move. Players who used it include Bobby Fischer early in his career (with games from 1957 and 1967), Mikhail Botvinnik, Vasily Smyslov, Tigran Petrosian and Leonid Stein. Today it is relatively uncommon at grandmaster level, but the Egyptian grandmaster Bassem Amin plays it often, and it is popular at club level.

### The idea

White plays the same moves in almost every game: Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2 and e4. The main plan, especially against setups with ...e6, is the push e4–e5, gaining a central bind and kingside space and giving practical attacking chances against Black's castled king. Unlike in the French Defence, White's e5-pawn is supported by pieces rather than by a d4-pawn.

### Typical Black setups

- **With ...e6 and ...c5** (as in the French or Sicilian): the classic KIA battleground. White plays e5, Re1, h4 and Nf1–h2–g4 or similar, while Black expands on the queenside with ...b5–b4.
- **With ...d5 and ...c6 or ...Bg4**: Black builds a solid centre, and White must choose between e4 and a quieter game.
- **With ...g6**: a symmetrical King's Indian setup where the game is more strategic.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- One setup against almost everything, with very little theory to learn.
- Clear attacking plans with e5 and a kingside pawn storm.
- A safe king position and a flexible middlegame.

**Weaknesses**

- Objectively modest: Black equalises easily with sensible play.
- Black's queenside counterplay can arrive before White's attack.
- Its predictability allows well-prepared opponents to choose the most unpleasant setup.

### Who should play it

The KIA suits players who want a universal system with White, prefer understanding to memorising, and like kingside attacks in closed positions. It is effective against French- and Sicilian-type setups with ...e6. It is less effective against opponents who build a solid ...d5 and ...c6 centre and aim for quiet equality.

### The numbers

On Lichess, White scores about 50% and Black 45–46% below 1800. Between 1800 and 2200 White does better, 53% against 41%, and above 2200 it is 50.5% against 41%. In master games over the board, White wins 34%, Black 22% and 44% are drawn.
