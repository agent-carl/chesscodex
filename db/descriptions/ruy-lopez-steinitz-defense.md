The Steinitz Defense (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 d6), also called the Old Steinitz Defence, is a solid but passive and cramped way to meet the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez). Black supports the e5-pawn with ...d6 instead of asking the bishop a question with 3...a6.

### Origins

It was the favourite defence of the first world champion, Wilhelm Steinitz, and was often played by Emanuel Lasker and José Raúl Capablanca, and occasionally by Vasily Smyslov. It largely fell out of use after the First World War, as its passivity led players to look for more active ways of defending the Ruy Lopez.

### The idea

Black keeps a firm point on e5, but the pin on the c6-knight makes this hard to maintain. White's most direct approach is 4.d4, challenging e5 at once; 4.c3 and 4.O-O are also good. After 4.d4 Bd7, breaking the pin and parrying the threat of 5.Bxc6 and 6.dxe5, White plays 5.Nc3, protecting e4 and renewing the threat. After 5...Nf6 6.O-O Be7 7.Re1 Black has to give up the centre with 7...exd4, since 7...O-O? walks into the Tarrasch Trap.

### Main lines

- 4.d4 Bd7 5.Nc3 Nf6, the main line described above; 6.Bxc6 is the [Nimzowitsch Attack](/openings/ruy-lopez-steinitz-defense-nimzowitsch-attack).
- 4.d4 exd4 5.O-O, the [Center Gambit](/openings/ruy-lopez-steinitz-defense-center-gambit).
- 4.d4 Bd7 5.c4, the [Semi-Duras Variation](/openings/ruy-lopez-steinitz-defense-semi-duras-variation).
- The [Modern Steinitz Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-morphy-defense-modern-steinitz-defense) (3...a6 4.Ba4 d6) gives Black a freer position and is more popular.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Solid, with a firm hold on e5 for as long as Black can keep it.
- It was the choice of defensive world champions such as Steinitz, Lasker and Capablanca.

**Weaknesses**

- Passive and cramped; Black usually has to give up the centre with ...exd4.
- Tactical traps, such as the Tarrasch Trap, punish natural moves.

### Who should play it

The Steinitz Defense suits patient, defensive players who are happy with a cramped but solid position; most Black players prefer the Modern Steinitz with 3...a6 4.Ba4 d6.
