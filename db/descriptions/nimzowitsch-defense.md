The Nimzowitsch Defense (1.e4 Nc6) is named after Aron Nimzowitsch. It is a hypermodern reply: Black develops a knight at once and keeps the choice between ...d5 and ...e5, so the character of the game depends on Black's second move.

### The idea

1...Nc6 blocks the c-pawn, so Black gives up the usual ...c5 or ...c6 plans and instead strikes the centre with ...d5 or ...e5 next move. Garry Kasparov and Raymond Keene wrote that it has never been fully accepted as a dependable opening, yet is sound and offers a great deal of unexplored territory.

### Main lines

After 2.d4, Black has two main answers:

- [Scandinavian Variation](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-scandinavian-variation) 2...d5, Nimzowitsch's own choice:
  - [3.e5](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-scandinavian-variation-advance-variation), usually met by ...Bf5;
  - [3.exd5 Qxd5](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-scandinavian-variation-exchange-variation), followed by 4.Nf3;
  - 3.Nc3 dxe4.
- [Kennedy Variation](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-kennedy-variation) 2...e5, favoured by Tony Miles:
  - 3.Nf3 transposes to the [Scotch Game](/openings/scotch-game);
  - [3.d5](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-kennedy-variation-linksspringer-variation) Nce7 gains space;
  - 3.dxe5 Nxe5 leads to open play.

The most common reply in databases is [2.Nf3](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-declined-variation), when Black can choose 2...d6 ([Williams Variation](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-williams-variation)), 2...e6 ([Franco-Nimzowitsch](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-franco-nimzowitsch-variation)), 2...e5 with a normal open game, or the sharp Colorado Gambit 2...f5.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Rare, so opponents are often unprepared.
- Flexible: Black can steer towards many different structures.
- Less theory than the main defences to 1.e4.

**Weaknesses**

- The knight on c6 blocks the c-pawn, which limits Black's usual counterplay.
- White can gain space with d4 and d5 or e5.
- It has never been fully accepted as a dependable opening.

### Who should play it

The Nimzowitsch suits players who like unusual positions and want to take the opponent out of their preparation early. Because it can transpose into open games after 2...e5, it is also a practical choice for players who already know those positions.

### The numbers

On Lichess White does better at lower levels (51.8% against 44.1% under 1400), but from 1800 up it is nearly even: 47.6% against 48% at 1800–2199, and 47.3% against 46.3% from 2200 up. In master games over the board, White wins 38.3%, Black 28.5% and 33.2% are drawn.
