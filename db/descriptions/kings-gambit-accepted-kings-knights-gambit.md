The King's Knight's Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3, the most popular way for White to continue after Black accepts the [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit). The knight develops and stops the check 3...Qh4+. The main alternative on move three is 3.Bc4, the [Bishop's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit).

### The idea

Black has two main approaches. One is to hold on to the extra pawn with ...g5, sometimes prepared by ...h6. The other is to return the pawn with an early ...d5 to speed up development. The two strategies can also be combined.

### Main lines

- 3...g5 is the Classical Variation. Black defends f4 and threatens to drive the knight away with ...g4. After 4.h4 g4 5.Ne5 White plays the [Kieseritzky Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kieseritzky-gambit), which modern writers consider the main line. 5.Ng5 instead is the [Allgaier Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-allgaier-gambit), a knight sacrifice that modern theory considers unsound. 4.Bc4 aims for the Muzio Gambit after 4...g4 5.O-O, while the safer 4...Bg7 usually leads to the [Hanstein Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-hanstein-gambit). The [Quaade Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-quaade-gambit), 4.Nc3, was recommended by John Shaw as a less explored alternative.
- [3...d6](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-fischer-defense), the Fischer Defense, was advocated by Bobby Fischer after Boris Spassky beat him in a Kieseritzky Gambit at Mar del Plata in 1960.
- [3...h6](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-becker-defense), the Becker Defense, builds the pawn chain h6, g5, f4 while avoiding the Kieseritzky.
- [3...d5](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-modern-defense), the Modern Defense, gives the pawn back for piece play. Its main line is the [Abbazia Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-abbazia-defense), 4.exd5 Nf6.
- [3...Be7](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-cunningham-defense), the Cunningham Defense, and [3...Nf6](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-schallopp-defense), the Schallopp Defense, are the other named replies.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.8% of the 19.3 million games and Black 44.7%. The balance shifts with rating: White leads 55.1% to 42.0% under 1400, while from 2200 up Black is ahead, 48.6% against 46.2%. In 2,381 master games over the board White won 26.3%, Black 35.4%, and 38.3% were drawn.
