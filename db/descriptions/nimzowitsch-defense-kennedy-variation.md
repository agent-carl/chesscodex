The Kennedy Variation is 1.e4 Nc6 2.d4 e5, one of Black's two main answers to 2.d4 in the [Nimzowitsch Defense](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense). Instead of challenging the centre with ...d5, as Aron Nimzowitsch himself usually did (the [Scandinavian Variation](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense-scandinavian-variation)), Black strikes at d4 with the e-pawn. It is a solid line, favoured by the late British grandmaster Tony Miles.

### The idea

The Nimzowitsch Defense is a hypermodern opening: Black invites White to occupy the centre with pawns, then tries to restrain those pawns and later undermine them.

### White's third move

- 3.Nf3 transposes to the [Scotch Game](/openings/scotch-game), so Black has to be ready for it.
- 3.d5 Nce7 gains space. Black intends ...Ng6, a manoeuvre known as the Black Knight's Tango. The retreat 3...Nb8 is considered inferior, though perhaps not as bad as it looks. In practice 3.d5 gives White only a slight plus score.
- 3.dxe5 Nxe5 lets White seek a quiet advantage with 4.Nf3, or play the more aggressive but potentially weakening 4.f4.

### The numbers

On Lichess the line has been played in 6.9 million games: White wins 48.6% and Black 47.5%. Under 1400 White does much better, 54.3% against 41.7%, while from 2200 up the results are level, 47.1% against 47.2%. In 649 master games over the board White won 38.1%, Black 30.8%, and 31.1% were drawn.
