The Three Knights Opening (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3) brings out a third knight. Black's most common reply, 3...Nf6, leads to the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game); the name Three Knights Game is used when Black breaks the symmetry with a different third move to avoid the Four Knights, which is often considered drawish.

### Origins

An early example is Rosenthal–Steinitz, 1873, reached by the move order 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nc6 3.Nf3: Wilhelm Steinitz answered 3...g6, the defence that bears his name, and won with Black in 38 moves. Today the Three Knights Game is almost never seen at master level, as Black players look for more active tries, even within the Four Knights.

### The idea

White develops naturally and keeps the option of d4. If Black plays a passive move such as 3...d6 or 3...Be7, White answers 4.d4 and Black gets a cramped position resembling the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense). After 3...Bc5, White can remove Black's grip on e5 with 4.Nxe5! Nxe5 5.d4 Bd6 6.dxe5 Bxe5, a line that tournament practice seems to show favours White.

### Main lines

- 3...Nf6, the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game).
- [3...Bb4](/openings/three-knights-opening-2), usually met by 4.Nd5; 4...Nf6 is the [Schlechter Variation](/openings/three-knights-opening-schlechter-variation).
- 3...g6, the [Steinitz Defense](/openings/three-knights-opening-steinitz-defense), typically 4.d4 exd4 5.Nd5 ([Steinitz-Rosenthal Variation](/openings/three-knights-opening-steinitz-rosenthal-variation)).
- 3...f5!?, the [Winawer Defense](/openings/three-knights-opening-winawer-defense), also called the Gothic Defense; 4.Bb5 transposes into the [Schliemann Defense](/openings/ruy-lopez-schliemann-defense) of the Ruy Lopez.
- With the black knight on f6 instead of c6, the similar position is [Petrov's Three Knights Game](/openings/petrovs-defense-three-knights-game), which also usually leads to the Four Knights.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Simple, natural development.
- Passive replies by Black are met by 4.d4, leaving Black cramped.

**Weaknesses**

- Black can simply reply 3...Nf6 and steer the game into the Four Knights.
- Strong players rarely meet it, so there is little modern master practice to learn from.

### Who should play it

The Three Knights suits players who like quiet, classical development and want to avoid the heavily analysed [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) and [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game).

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are close below 1800: 48.0% for White against 47.4% under 1400, and 48.1% against 47.4% at 1400–1799. From 1800 White pulls ahead, 50.0% against 44.8% at 1800–2199 and 50.1% against 41.4% from 2200 up, where 8.5% of games are drawn. In about 14,600 master games 48.0% were drawn; White won 27.5% and Black 24.5%.
