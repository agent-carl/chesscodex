The Anglo-Indian Defense (1.c4 Nf6) is the most common reply to the [English Opening](/openings/english-opening). Black develops a knight and keeps every option open, usually hoping to transpose to an Indian defence once White plays d4.

### The idea

1...Nf6 waits for White to commit. If White avoids d4, Black often switches to a Symmetrical English with a later ...c5, or to a Reversed Sicilian with ...e5. The many transpositions make move orders important for both sides: Black cannot always reach the defence they want, and White can steer away from some of them.

### Main lines

- 2.Nc3, the [Queen's Knight Variation](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-queens-knight-variation):
  - 2...e6, the [Hedgehog System](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-hedgehog-system) in this database. 3.e4, the Mikėnas–Carls Variation, is an aggressive answer that stops Black from reaching a Queen's Gambit Declined; the most common continuation is 3...d5 4.e5, the Flohr Variation, usually followed by 4...d4 5.exf6 dxc3 6.bxc3 Qxf6;
  - 2...g6, where 3.d4 d5 gives a [Grünfeld Defense](/openings/grunfeld-defense), but 3.e4 rules the Grünfeld out and more or less forces Black into a [King's Indian](/openings/kings-indian-defense) with 3...d6;
  - 2...d5, where White can avoid a Grünfeld with 3.cxd5 Nxd5 4.g3, a line Mikhail Botvinnik played several times in his 1958 world championship match against Vasily Smyslov;
  - 2...c5 and 2...e5, transposing to the Symmetrical English and the Reversed Sicilian.
- 2.Nf3, the [King's Knight Variation](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-kings-knight-variation), with set-ups such as 2...g6 ([King's Indian Formation](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-kings-indian-formation)), 2...d5 ([Scandinavian Defense](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-scandinavian-defense)) and 2...d6 ([Old Indian Formation](/openings/english-opening-anglo-indian-defense-old-indian-formation)). White can also aim for a [Réti Opening](/openings/reti-opening) with g3 and Bg2.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Maximum flexibility: Black can head for an Indian defence, a Symmetrical English or a Reversed Sicilian.
- The most common reply to 1.c4, with natural development.

**Weaknesses**

- White can use move orders to avoid Black's preferred defence, such as 3.e4 against the Grünfeld or the Queen's Gambit Declined.
- Black needs to know several structures.

### Who should play it

The Anglo-Indian suits Black players with an Indian repertoire against 1.d4 who want to keep it against 1.c4, and who are comfortable switching to English structures when White does not play d4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White scores about 49% at every level: 48.9% against 47.3% under 1400, 49.1% against 46.7% at 1400–1799, 49.3% against 45.1% at 1800–2199 and 49.6% against 42.5% from 2200 up. In about 55,200 master games White won 35.4%, Black 21.2%, and 43.4% were drawn.
