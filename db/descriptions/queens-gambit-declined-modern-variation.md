The Modern Variation is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5, the traditional line of the [Queen's Gambit Declined](/openings/queens-gambit-declined). White pins the knight on f6 to the queen. 4.Bg5 was the most common fourth move in the 20th century, although the [Exchange Variation](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-exchange-variation), 4.cxd5, has gradually become more common.

### Main lines

- 4...Be7 is Black's most common reply, typically followed by 5.e3 O-O 6.Nf3. The same position often arises from the [Three Knights Variation](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-three-knights-variation), 4.Nf3 Be7 5.Bg5, though that move order gives White the extra option of the Harrwitz Attack, 5.Bf4. After 6.Nf3, 6...Nbd7 is the [Orthodox Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-orthodox-defense), while 6...h6 is the Neo-Orthodox Variation, which leads to the Tartakower Variation (7.Bh4 b6), the Lasker Variation (7.Bh4 Ne4) and the Anti-Tartakower Variation (7.Bxf6).
- 4...Nbd7 sets up the Elephant Trap. If White avoids it, 5.e3 c6 6.Nf3 allows 6...Qa5, the [Cambridge Springs Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-cambridge-springs-defense).

### Lines worth knowing

The Lasker Variation is a solid line that often leads to the exchange of two pairs of minor pieces. Viswanathan Anand chose it in the final game of the 2010 world championship match to beat Veselin Topalov and keep his title. The Anti-Tartakower was tested extensively in the Kasparov-Karpov matches of the 1980s, and Black has had no problems in it even at the highest level.

An early 4...h6 is weak because of 5.Bxf6: after 5...gxf6 6.cxd5 exd5 Black has doubled isolated pawns, and 5...Qxf6 6.cxd5 exd5 7.Nxd5 loses a pawn.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.4% of the 9.4 million games and Black 43.9%. White's lead shrinks a little with rating, from 52.3% against 43.9% under 1400 to 49.1% against 42.6% from 2200 up. Masters over the board draw half their games: in 7,914 games 50.3% were drawn, White won 32.4% and Black 17.4%.
