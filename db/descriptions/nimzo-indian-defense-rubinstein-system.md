The Rubinstein System is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3, White's most common way of meeting the [Nimzo-Indian Defense](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense). It is named after Akiba Rubinstein. White simply continues development before choosing a plan, and Svetozar Gligorić and Lajos Portisch did much for its theory and practice at the top level.

### Main lines

Black has three main replies, 4...O-O, 4...c5 and 4...b6, and sometimes plays 4...d5 or 4...Nc6, the Taimanov Variation.

- 4...O-O is the most flexible and most common. The main line runs 5.Bd3 d5 6.Nf3 c5 7.O-O: White has finished developing the kingside and Black has claimed a share of the centre. Important continuations are the Parma Variation 7...dxc4 8.Bxc4 Nbd7, the Karpov Variation 7...dxc4 8.Bxc4 cxd4 9.exd4 b6, one of Black's most reliable defences, and the main variation 7...Nc6 8.a3 Bxc3 9.bxc3 dxc4 10.Bxc4 Qc7, in which Black equalises in all lines but must know White's many eleventh moves.
- [4...c5](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-rubinstein-system-2) puts pressure on d4. In the Hübner Variation, 5.Bd3 Nc6 6.Nf3 Bxc3+ 7.bxc3 d6, Black gives up the bishop at once to blockade the dark squares with ...d6 and ...e5. Bobby Fischer used it with great effect in game 5 of his 1972 match against Boris Spassky. With 5.Ne2, the Rubinstein Variation, White avoids doubled pawns.
- [4...b6](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-st-petersburg-variation), favoured by Nimzowitsch, fianchettoes the light-squared bishop to control e4. White usually answers 5.Ne2 or 5.Bd3.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main line of the Rubinstein has held up very well for Black. Since the 1980s White has therefore often looked elsewhere, for example by developing the knight to e2 rather than f3, so that it can recapture on c3 and avoid doubled pawns. Two such lines after 4...O-O are the [Reshevsky Variation](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense-reshevsky-variation), 5.Ne2, and the Modern Variation, 5.Bd3 d5 6.Ne2.

### Who plays it

Besides Gligorić and Portisch on the white side, many of its lines carry the names of the players who shaped them as Black: Bruno Parma, Anatoly Karpov, Bent Larsen and Robert Hübner.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.3% of the 1.8 million games and Black 44.8%. Under 1800 White wins about 51% against about 45.5%, and from 2200 up 48.9% against 43.4%, with 7.7% draws. In 36,178 master games over the board 44.9% were drawn, White won 29.7% and Black 25.4%.
