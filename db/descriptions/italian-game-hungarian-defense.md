The Hungarian Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Be7, a quiet answer to the [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game). With 3...Be7 Black avoids the complications of the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense) (3...Nf6) and the [Giuoco Piano](/openings/italian-game-giuoco-piano) (3...Bc5). White keeps more space and freer development, so Black must be ready to defend a cramped position.

### Origins

The variation takes its name from a correspondence game between Paris and Pest, Hungary, played from 1842 to 1845, but it was first analysed by Cozio in the 18th century.

### Main lines

White's best reply is 4.d4, aiming for an advantage in the centre. 4.d3 Nf6 transposes to the Two Knights Defense, and less well regarded moves include 4.c3 Nf6, Steinitz's choice, and 4.O-O Nf6 5.Nc3 d6 6.d4 Bg4. After 4.d4 transpositions to the [Scotch Game](/openings/scotch-game) or the [Philidor Defense](/openings/philidor-defense) are common.

- 4...exd4 5.Nxd4 transposes into a Scotch Game in which White has more space. 5.c3 is weaker: after 5...dxc3 6.Qd5 Black resigned in Midjord–Scharf, Nice Olympiad 1974, but Black can instead play 5...Na5, as Chigorin recommended. 5...Nf6 6.e5 Ne4 is the [Tartakower Variation](/openings/italian-game-hungarian-defense-tartakower-variation), where Unzicker considered White better after 7.Bd5 Nc5 8.cxd4 Ne6 9.Bb3.
- 4...d6 holds the centre, and White has several plans, each enough for a slight advantage. White can simplify with 5.dxe5 dxe5 6.Qxd8+ Bxd8 7.Nc3 into a slightly better queenless middlegame. Here 5...Nxe5? loses a pawn to 6.Nxe5 dxe5 7.Qh5, hitting e5 and f7. White can also close the centre with 5.d5 Nb8, followed by Bd3 and c4, with positions like the [Old Indian Defense](/openings/old-indian-defense). Or White keeps the tension with 5.Nc3 for active piece play.

### Who plays it

The Hungarian is rarely seen in modern play. Grandmasters with strong defensive skills have played it on occasion, including Samuel Reshevsky, Vlastimil Hort and the former world champions Tigran Petrosian and Vasily Smyslov. Harding and Botterill wrote that it "can only be played for a draw" and that White should have an edge in most lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins more often than White below 1800: Black wins 49.0% against 47.3% under 1400, and 48.1% against 47.7% between 1400 and 1799. From 1800 up White is ahead: 48.7% against 45.9% between 1800 and 2199, and 49.6% against 42.8% from 2200 up. Over all 5.5 million games White wins 48.4% and Black 46.5%. In 1,043 master games over the board White won 41.6%, Black 21.5%, and 36.9% were drawn.
