The McCutcheon Variation is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Bb4: in the Classical [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) Black ignores White's threat of e4–e5 and counterattacks by pinning the c3-knight. Its ECO code is C12.

### Origins

The variation is named after John Lindsay McCutcheon of Philadelphia (1857–1905), who brought it to public attention by beating World Champion Wilhelm Steinitz with it in a simultaneous exhibition in Manhattan in 1885. It was most popular from 1905 to 1925, then disappeared from view until it was revived in 1990.

### Main lines

- [5.e5](/openings/french-defense-mccutcheon-variation-advance-variation) h6 6.Bd2 Bxc3 7.bxc3 Ne4 8.Qg4 is the main line. Black then chooses between 8...g6, which weakens the dark squares on the kingside but keeps the option of castling queenside, and 8...Kf8. The line 6.Bd2 Bxc3 is named the [Lasker Variation](/openings/french-defense-mccutcheon-variation-lasker-variation) on this site.
- White can also carry out the threat after 5.e5 h6 with 6.exf6 hxg5 7.fxg7 Rg8.

### The idea

Instead of defending the knight on f6 with ...Be7, as in the Classical Variation, Black puts pressure on e4 by pinning the knight on c3. In the main line Black gives up the bishop for the knight to double White's c-pawns, and accepts some weakness on the kingside in return.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins slightly more often: 47.2% against 46.7% for White in 510,000 games. Lower ratings favour White, 54.8% against 41.4% under 1400. Between 1800 and 2199 Black leads, 48.2% against 46.2%, and from 2200 up White is slightly ahead again, 47.3% against 45.2%. In 4,145 master games over the board White won 32.6%, Black 24.2%, and 43.2% were drawn.
