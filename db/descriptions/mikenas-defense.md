The Mikenas Defense is 1.d4 Nc6: Black answers the queen's pawn with the queen's knight. It is also known as the Bogoljubov–Mikenas Defense, the Queen's Knight Defense, the Nimzowitsch Queen Pawn Defense and the Lundin Defense, and is classified as A40 unless it transposes into another opening.

### Origins

Hypermodern players such as Aron Nimzowitsch and Efim Bogoljubov tried 1...Nc6 against 1.d4, but it has never become very popular. The move is committal: it blocks Black's c-pawn, and usually Black waits to play ...Nc6 until White's set-up is clear.

### The idea

Black is ready to play ...e5 on the next move, so White cannot ignore the knight. Most games with 1.d4 Nc6 transpose into other openings, and Black has to be ready for all of them.

### Main lines

- 2.e4 gives the [Nimzowitsch Defense](/openings/nimzowitsch-defense).
- 2.c4 d5 is the [Chigorin Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-chigorin-defense) of the Queen's Gambit. After 2.c4 e5 3.d5 the knight retreats: [3...Nce7](/openings/mikenas-defense-lithuanian-variation) is the Lithuanian Variation.
- 2.Nf3 is the practical choice for White, looking for a position with a theoretical advantage. Black then has two main replies. 2...d6 can lead to the [Pirc Defense](/openings/pirc-defense) after ...g6 and e4, or to the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense) if White plays an early c4. 2...e6 can transpose to the [Nimzo-Indian](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense), the [Bogo-Indian](/openings/bogo-indian-defense) or the Chigorin if White plays c4 at once.
- 2.d5 is the line that belongs to this opening alone: it chases the knight away, usually to e5. The position resembles an [Alekhine Defense](/openings/alekhine-defense) on the other side of the board. In an opening book by Sid Pickard this line was called the "Bozo-Indian", from "Nimzo" and "Bogo", although it is not an Indian opening. The retreat [2...Nb8](/openings/montevideo-defense) has its own name on this site, the Montevideo Defense.

### Who should play it

After 1.d4 Nc6 Black has to be ready for many different continuations. Since most games transpose, a player who uses it needs to be at home in the Nimzowitsch, the Chigorin and the Indian set-ups alike.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.4% of the 4.1 million games and Black 46.0%. Lower down, White does better: under 1400 White wins 53.3% against 41.9%, and between 1400 and 1799 55.0% against 41.1%. Between 1800 and 2199 Black is slightly ahead, 47.9% against 47.6%, and from 2200 up White wins 47.1% and Black 46.1%. In 1,680 master games over the board White won 43.8%, Black 26.1%, and 30.1% were drawn.
