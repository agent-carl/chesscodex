The Orthodox Variation of the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense) (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Be2) is White's natural development with a big pawn centre. Black usually answers [6...e5](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-2), reaching the Classical Variation of the King's Indian.

### Main lines

After 6...e5:

- 7.O-O Nc6 ([Aronin-Taimanov Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-aronin-taimanov-defense) in this database) 8.d5 Ne7, the main line or Mar del Plata Variation. White usually attacks on the queenside, preparing the break c4–c5, while Black moves the f6-knight, usually to d7, and starts a kingside pawn storm with ...f5–f4 and ...g5. 9.b4, the [Bayonet Attack](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-bayonet-attack), was introduced by Viktor Korchnoi in the 1970s; it used to put top players off this line, and it has recently been revived by Teimour Radjabov. 9.Ne1 ([Classical System](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-classical-system)) and 9.Nd2 are among White's other choices.
- 7.O-O Nbd7, the old main line, still playable but less common than 7...Nc6. 7.O-O exd4 8.Nxd4 was made popular by Igor Glek in the mid-1990s. 7.O-O Na6 ([Glek Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-glek-defense) in this database) prepares to bring the knight to c5 after White's d5.
- 7.d5, the [Petrosian Variation](/openings/kings-indian-defense-petrosian-variation), named after Tigran Petrosian, who often played it in the 1960s; Vladimir Kramnik played it extensively in the 1990s. After 7...a5 ([Stein Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense-petrosian-variation-stein-defense) in this database) White pins the knight with 8.Bg5, making ...f5 harder to achieve.
- 7.Be3, the [Gligorić System](/openings/kings-indian-defense-orthodox-variation-gligoric-taimanov-system), after Svetozar Gligorić. White avoids the theory after 7.O-O Nc6 and keeps the central tension for the moment. Black most often replies 7...Ng4 8.Bg5 f6 9.Bh4 Nc6.
- 7.dxe5 dxe5 8.Qxd8 Rxd8, the [Exchange Variation](/openings/kings-indian-defense-exchange-variation), a queenless middlegame in which White plays for a small, safe advantage. It is often chosen by White players hoping for an early draw, but chances are balanced and there is still a lot of play left.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- White develops naturally, keeps a big centre and has a clear queenside plan.
- Black gets rich, double-edged positions with kingside attacking chances, especially in the Mar del Plata.

**Weaknesses**

- The Bayonet Attack once put top players off the main line with 7...Nc6.
- White's quiet option, the Exchange Variation, leads to balanced chances.

### Who should play it

The Orthodox Variation suits White players who want the classical approach against the King's Indian, and Black players who enjoy races on opposite wings.

### The numbers

On Lichess White is ahead at every level: 50.4% against 45.9% for Black under 1400, 50.0% against 45.7% at 1400–1799, 50.3% against 44.1% at 1800–2199, and 50.0% against 42.6% from 2200 up. In about 54,500 master games White won 36.6%, Black 22.8%, and 40.5% were drawn.
