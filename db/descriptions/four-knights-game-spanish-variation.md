The Spanish Variation of the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game) (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bb5) is, together with the [Scotch Variation](/openings/four-knights-game-scotch-variation) (4.d4), one of the two main continuations of the Four Knights. White develops the bishop as in the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez), with the knights already on c3 and f6.

### Origins

The Four Knights was a popular choice in the 19th century but had fallen out of favour by the First World War, as ambitious players turned to the Ruy Lopez. It saw a renaissance in the 1990s and is again played even by grandmasters. The Double Spanish line is one of the most classical in chess, with 19th-century games still influencing its assessment, and Nigel Short, Michael Adams and Alexei Shirov have played it.

### The idea

The bishop on b5 attacks the c6-knight, which defends e5. The game is often quiet and positional, and the Four Knights has a drawish reputation, though some lines are sharp.

### Main lines

- 4...Bb4, the [Double Spanish](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-double-spanish), Black's most common move: the Ruy Lopez with mirrored moves added. Most games continue 5.O-O O-O, then 6.d3 d6, the [Symmetrical Variation](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-symmetrical-variation), where the main line is 7.Bg5 Bxc3 8.bxc3 Qe7, Metger's unpin. Black must not mirror with 7...Bg4?, which loses a piece after 8.Bxf6 Qxf6 9.Nd5 Qd8 10.Bxc6 bxc6 11.Nxb4.
- 4...Nd4, the [Rubinstein Variation](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-rubinstein-variation), named after Akiba Rubinstein, an unbalancing counter-gambit. 5.Ba4 is White's most common reply, 5.Bc4 a more modern one, and 5.Nxd4 a very drawish line that discourages many ambitious Black players.
- 4...Bc5, the [Classical Variation](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-classical-variation), perfectly playable, as in the famous game Paulsen–Morphy from the First American Chess Congress of 1857.
- 4...a6 5.Bxc6, the [Ranken Variation](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-ranken-variation); 5.Ba4 transposes to the Ruy Lopez.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Solid, natural development with Ruy Lopez-type pressure on e5.
- A good way for White to avoid the theory of [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense) via 2...Nf6 3.Nc3 Nc6.

**Weaknesses**

- Drawish reputation, especially in the symmetrical lines and after 4...Nd4 5.Nxd4.
- Black has several sound replies, including the active Rubinstein Variation.

### Who should play it

The Spanish Four Knights suits White players who like classical, positional chess and want a solid alternative to the main Ruy Lopez.

### The numbers

On Lichess White scores about 50% at every level: 50.1% against 44.9% under 1400, 49.9% against 45.3% at 1400–1799, 50.4% against 43.9% at 1800–2199 and 49.4% against 42.2% from 2200 up. In about 7,600 master games 50.1% were drawn; White won 28.3% and Black 21.6%.
