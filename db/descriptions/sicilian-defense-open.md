The Open Sicilian with 2...Nc6 (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4) is one of the main battlegrounds of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). White opens the centre with d4; after 3...cxd4 4.Nxd4 Black has traded a wing pawn for a centre pawn and gets a half-open c-file in return.

### The idea

2...Nc6 is a natural developing move that also prepares ...Nf6. Like 2...d6, it stops White from answering ...Nf6 with e5. After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Black chooses the structure: the most common move is 4...Nf6, while 4...e6, 4...g6 and 4...e5 lead to quite different systems.

### Main lines

- 4...Nf6 5.Nc3, when Black chooses between:
  - 5...d6, transposing to the [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-classical-variation);
  - 5...e5, the Sveshnikov Variation, called the [Lasker-Pelikan Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation) before Evgeny Sveshnikov and Gennadi Timoshchenko developed it in the 1970s. Black accepts a backward d-pawn and a hole on d5 in return for time and central space; it is now extremely popular at every level, and players such as Magnus Carlsen, Vladimir Kramnik and Veselin Topalov have used it;
  - 5...e6, transposing to the [Four Knights Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-four-knights-variation).
- 4...e6, transposing to the [Taimanov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation).
- 4...g6, the [Accelerated Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-accelerated-dragon).
- 4...e5 5.Nb5, with two options:
  - 5...a6, the [Löwenthal Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lowenthal-variation), played by La Bourdonnais in his 1834 matches against McDonnell and named after Johann Löwenthal; it declined once it was found that White keeps the advantage in several lines;
  - 5...d6, the [Kalashnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-kalashnikov-variation), a close relative of the Sveshnikov that Black players revived in the late 1980s.
- Less common fourth moves include 4...Qc7, which may transpose to the Taimanov, 4...Qb6 (the Grivas Variation) and 4...d6.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Black gets an unbalanced game with a half-open c-file and many systems to choose from.
- The Sveshnikov in particular is established as a first-rate defence.

**Weaknesses**

- Lines such as the Sveshnikov and Kalashnikov leave Black with a backward d-pawn and a weak d5-square.
- Some older lines, such as the Löwenthal, are known to leave White an advantage.

### Who should play it

The Open Sicilian with 2...Nc6 suits Black players who want a fighting game against 1.e4 and are ready to study the system they choose after 4.Nxd4. White players who want to avoid the Sveshnikov often play the [Rossolimo Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-nyezhmetdinov-rossolimo-attack) (3.Bb5) instead of 3.d4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.7% against Black's 46.3%. Under 1400 White leads 51.0% against 45.3%; the gap narrows with rating, to 49.5% against 46.5% at 1400–1799, 48.6% against 46.3% at 1800–2199 and 47.1% against 45.2% from 2200 up. In about 78,200 master games 45.4% were drawn; White won 30.2% and Black 24.4%.
