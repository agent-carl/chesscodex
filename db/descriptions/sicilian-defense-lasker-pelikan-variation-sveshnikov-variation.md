This page covers the main line of the Sveshnikov Variation of the Sicilian Defense: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 e5 6.Ndb5 d6 7.Bg5 a6 8.Na3 b5. The whole line up to 8...b5 is also called the Chelyabinsk Variation. With 5...e5 Black drives the knight from d4 and accepts a backward d-pawn and a weak d5-square in return for a foothold in the centre, time gained on White's knight and dynamic play. The general overview is on the [Lasker-Pelikan Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation) page.

### History

Before the 1970s the variation saw only occasional play: Emanuel Lasker played it once in his world championship match against Carl Schlechter, and Jorge Pelikan played it a few times in the 1950s. Evgeny Sveshnikov's treatment in the 1970s revitalised it, and today it is extremely popular among grandmasters and amateurs alike. Magnus Carlsen, Vladimir Kramnik and Veselin Topalov are among the top players who have used it.

### The moves

6.Ndb5, threatening Nd6+, is the critical move; all other sixth moves are thought to give Black easy equality. 6...d6 stops Nd6+, 7.Bg5 prepares to remove the knight on f6, which guards d5, and 7...a6 drives White's knight back to a3. 8...b5 was Sveshnikov's innovation: it controls c4 and threatens ...b4, forking White's knights. Before it, Black played 8...Be6, the Bird Variation, which let the knight come back to life with 9.Nc4.

### 9.Bxf6 and 9.Nd5

White usually meets the threat of ...b4 with 9.Bxf6 or 9.Nd5.

- After 9.Bxf6 Black must recapture 9...gxf6, since 9...Qxf6 10.Nd5 Qd8 11.c4 favours White. After 10.Nd5 White's strong knight on d5 and Black's broken kingside are balanced by Black's bishop pair and White's offside knight on a3. Black can play 10...f5, or 10...Bg7 followed by ...Ne7 to trade off the knight on d5, the Novosibirsk Variation.
- 9.Nd5 tends to lead to quieter play, often 9...Be7 10.Bxf6 Bxf6 11.c3, after which the knight on a3 comes back into play via c2 and e3. A recent development is 11.c4 instead, which often leads to positions where White presses for a win at no risk.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.8% of the 1 million games and Black 44.0%. From 2200 up White's lead is 49.4% against 42.0%. Masters draw most of their games: in 24,487 games over the board 58.7% were drawn, White won 24.1% and Black 17.2%.
