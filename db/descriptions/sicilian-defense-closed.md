The Closed Sicilian (1.e4 c5 2.Nc3) is White's second most common answer to the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense), after 2.Nf3. Instead of opening the centre with an early d4, White usually builds up slowly with g3, Bg2 and d3, or with f4.

### Move order

2.Nc3 keeps White's options open. Black's replies are much the same as against 2.Nf3: most often 2...Nc6, but also 2...e6 and 2...d6. White can still play 3.Nf3 and reach a position from the 2.Nf3 lines, and many other lines transpose to the [Open Sicilian](/openings/sicilian-defense-open), the [Moscow Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-moscow-variation) or the [Rossolimo Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-nyezhmetdinov-rossolimo-attack). 2...e5 transposes into a [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game).

### The idea

The main line runs 2...Nc6 3.g3 g6 4.Bg2 Bg7 5.d3 d6. White then chooses between 6.Be3 followed by Qd2 and possibly long castling, and 6.f4 followed by Nf3 and short castling.

### Main lines

- 2...Nc6, the [Traditional](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-traditional) move, Black's most common choice:
  - 3.g3, the [Fianchetto Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-fianchetto-variation), leading to the [main line](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-6) above, then [6.Be3](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-8) or [6.f4](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-7);
  - 3.f4, the [Grand Prix Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-grand-prix-attack). This is how the Grand Prix is usually reached nowadays, rather than with 2.f4 at once. The modern main line is 3...g6 4.Nf3 Bg7, when White plays the positional 5.Bb5 or the more aggressive 5.Bc4;
  - 3.Nge2, the [Chameleon Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-chameleon-variation): White keeps the choice between a Closed Sicilian with g3 and an Open Sicilian with d4.
- [2...e6](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-2), for example 3.g3 d5, the [Korchnoi Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense-closed-korchnoi-defense).
- 2...d6 is also common.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- White avoids the heavily analysed Open Sicilian main lines.
- Flexible: White can still transpose to other Sicilian systems.

**Weaknesses**

- Black can often steer the game into a familiar Sicilian line.
- Lines with an early Nge2 are not very challenging for Black. After 2...Nc6 3.Nge2 g6 4.d4 the game reaches an [Accelerated Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-accelerated-dragon) where White can no longer set up the Maróczy Bind with c4.

### Who should play it

The Closed Sicilian suits White players who prefer slow manoeuvring and kingside play to memorising long forcing lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does slightly better below 1800: 49.4% against 46.9% under 1400 and 48.5% against 47.6% at 1400–1799. From 1800 up White is ahead: 49.7% against 45.5% at 1800–2199 and 49.4% against 43.9% from 2200 up. In about 45,600 master games White won 31.3%, Black 28.9%, and 39.8% were drawn.
