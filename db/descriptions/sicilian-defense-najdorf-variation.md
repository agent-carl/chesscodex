The Najdorf Variation (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6) is the most famous line of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense). It is often called the "Cadillac" or "Rolls-Royce" of chess openings, and it is one of the most popular, most respected and most deeply studied openings in chess.

### Origins

The move 5...a6 appeared in the 1920s, and the Czech master Karel Opočenský claimed credit for it. The variation is named after the Polish-Argentine grandmaster Miguel Najdorf, who played it as early as 1937 and deepened its analysis in the 1950s. Bobby Fischer called it "one of the greatest creations in chess theory" and made it his main weapon, and Garry Kasparov relied on it heavily too. Today it is played by elite players such as Maxime Vachier-Lagrave.

### The idea

The quiet-looking 5...a6 takes the b5-square away from White's knights and bishop. It prepares ...e5 or ...e6 and queenside expansion with ...b5, ...Bb7 and knights on d7, c5 or c4. Because Black keeps many options open, White has to commit first. The result is often opposite-side castling, with both players attacking the enemy king at the same time.

### Main lines

- [English Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-english-attack) 6.Be3, with f3, Qd2, g4 and long castling: the modern main line at top level.
- 6.Bg5, the classical main line, usually followed by 7.f4. It includes the famous Poisoned Pawn, where Black grabs the b2-pawn with the queen, and the [traditional 8.Qf3 lines](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-main-line).
- [Opočenský Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-opocensky-variation) 6.Be2: a quieter, positional approach with kingside castling, which Anatoly Karpov used often.
- [6.Bc4](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-lipnitsky-attack), the Fischer–Sozin idea aimed at f7, which has been less popular since the 1980s.
- [Adams Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-adams-attack) 6.h3, preparing g4, and the [Amsterdam Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-amsterdam-variation) 6.f4.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Sound at the highest level, and it has been tested in world championship matches for decades.
- Flexible: Black can choose between ...e5 and ...e6 structures depending on White's setup.
- Rich winning chances for Black, in sharp and unbalanced positions.

**Weaknesses**

- An enormous amount of theory. White's attacks are fast and concrete, and one inaccuracy can be decisive.
- Black must also be ready for all the Anti-Sicilians White can play before move five.
- Black's king often stays in the centre for a while.

### Who should play it

The Najdorf suits ambitious players who enjoy sharp, double-edged positions and are willing to study and update their theory. It is especially strong against players who attack by pattern without knowing the specific lines. It is least comfortable against well-prepared opponents who choose the English Attack or a sharp 6.Bg5 line.

### The numbers

On Lichess, Black wins slightly more often than White below 1800: 49% against 47%. Between 1800 and 2200 it is even, at 47.5% each. Above 2200, White edges ahead 49% to 45%. In master games over the board, 51% of games are drawn, White wins 27% and Black 22%.
