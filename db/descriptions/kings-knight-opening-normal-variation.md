The Normal Variation of the [King's Knight Opening](/openings/kings-knight-opening) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6, Black's usual reply to 2.Nf3. The knight defends the e5-pawn and develops at the same time. From here White's third move decides which of the classical open games is played.

### White's third move

- 3.Bb5, the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) or Spanish Game, is White's most frequent move at the top level. Black's most common answer is [3...a6](/openings/ruy-lopez-morphy-defense), which forces White to choose between exchanging on c6 and retreating with 4.Ba4. Also common is [3...Nf6](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense), the Berlin Defense, and the sharpest try is [3...f5](/openings/ruy-lopez-schliemann-defense), the Schliemann Defense.
- 3.Bc4, the [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game), is the second most popular move. It usually leads to the [Giuoco Piano](/openings/italian-game-giuoco-piano), 3...Bc5, or the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense), 3...Nf6. In the Giuoco Piano White can play c3 and d4, the classical approach; 4.b4, the [Evans Gambit](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit); or c3 and d3, the modern approach.
- 3.d4, the [Scotch Game](/openings/scotch-game), was popular in the 19th century and is still the third most common move. After 3...exd4 White either recaptures with 4.Nxd4, with a roughly equal game, or plays the [Scotch Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-scotch-gambit), 4.Bc4.
- 3.Nc3, the [Three Knights Opening](/openings/three-knights-opening), is usually met by 3...Nf6, the [Four Knights Game](/openings/four-knights-game). It is generally considered less challenging for Black than the three moves above.
- 3.c3, the [Ponziani Opening](/openings/ponziani-opening), has never been widely popular but is sometimes used as a surprise weapon.

### Rare third moves

The [Konstantinopolsky Opening](/openings/kings-knight-opening-konstantinopolsky), 3.g3, and the Inverted Hungarian, 3.Be2, are rarely played and give White no advantage.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.7% of the 229 million games and Black 44.6%. The results barely change with rating: 50.8% against 45.1% under 1400 and 48.2% against 44.3% from 2200 up. In 249,218 master games over the board 50.7% were drawn, White won 28.9% and Black 20.4%.
