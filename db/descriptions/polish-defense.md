The Polish Defense is 1.d4 b5: Black plays ...b5 at once, by analogy with the [Polish Opening](/openings/polish-opening), 1.b4. Black aims at the c4-square, but against 2.e4, which threatens Bxb5, the move is generally considered dubious. The name is also used for later ...b5 lines such as 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 b5, sometimes called the Polish Defense Deferred. The ECO code is A40.

### Origins

The original line was played by Alexander Wagner, a Polish player and opening analyst, against Kuhn in the 1913 Swiss Correspondence Championship. Wagner published an analysis of it in Deutsches Wochenschach in 1914, when he was living in Stanislau (now Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine).

Modern Chess Openings changed its verdict over time. MCO-9 (1957) said the Polish "fails because it neglects the centre", MCO-10 (1965) softened that to "neglects the centre, but is not refuted", and MCO-14 (1999) notes that the lines where Black waits and plays ...b5 on move two are much safer than 1...b5.

### Relation to the St. George

The Polish is closely related to the [St. George Defense](/openings/st-george-defense), 1.e4 a6 with ...b5, into which it often transposes. Boris Spassky played 1.d4 b5 against Tigran Petrosian in the decisive 22nd game of their 1966 World Championship match. Behind by a point with at most three games left, Spassky turned down a chance to draw and played for a win. Petrosian won the game and kept his title.

### The Spassky Variation

Against the Réti or the King's Indian Attack, ...b5 is fully respectable. The line 1.Nf3 Nf6 2.g3 b5, sometimes called the Spassky Variation after the same 1966 match, has been played successfully by Mikhail Tal, Anatoly Karpov and Magnus Carlsen among others. It prepares to fianchetto the queen's bishop and stops White from playing c4. Play typically continues 3.Bg2 Bb7 4.O-O e6, and 5.d4 transposes to 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 e6 3.g3 b5.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.3% of the 1.1 million games and Black 46.3%. Lower down White does much better: 55.1% against 39.9% under 1400. The gap closes as the rating rises, and from 2200 up the sides are level, 46.9% against 46.6%. In 666 master games over the board White won 48.0%, Black 23.9%, and 28.1% were drawn.
