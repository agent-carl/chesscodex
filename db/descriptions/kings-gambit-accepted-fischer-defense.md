The Fischer Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 d6, a reply to the [King's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit) made famous by Bobby Fischer. The move 3...d6 keeps the white knight out of e5 and lets Black hold the extra pawn with ...g5 later. White almost always answers 4.d4 or 4.Bc4. Its ECO code is C34.

### Origins

The move was known before, but it became a major variation only after Fischer advocated it in a 1961 article in the first issue of the American Chess Quarterly. In 1960 at Mar del Plata Fischer had lost to Boris Spassky, who played the [Kieseritzky Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kieseritzky-gambit) against him, and he left in tears and set to work on a new defence.

His article, "A Bust to the King's Gambit", claimed: "In my opinion the King's Gambit is busted. It loses by force." It ended with the famous line: "Of course White can always play differently, in which case he merely loses differently." Fischer never tested his analysis in a tournament game, as he never faced the gambit again. Later he played the King's Gambit himself and won all three of his tournament games with it, but with the [Bishop's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-bishops-gambit), 3.Bc4, not the 3.Nf3 line he had analysed.

### The idea

Fischer called 3...d6 "a high-class waiting move". It lets Black support the pawn with ...g5 while avoiding the Kieseritzky Gambit, 3...g5 4.h4 g4 5.Ne5, where the knight lands on e5.

### Main lines

- 4.d4 is the most common reply. It threatens to win back the pawn with Bxf4, so Black plays 4...g5, the only accurate move. After 5.h4 g4 the knight must move: Fischer analysed 6.Ng5 f6 7.Nh3 gxh3 8.Qh5+ Kd7 9.Bxf4 Qe8 10.Qf3 Kd8 as winning easily for Black. The more common 6.Ng1 has done well for White after 6...Bh6 7.Nc3 c6 8.Nge2 Qf6 9.g3, and Nigel Short beat Vladimir Akopian in this line in Madrid 1997. A more modern idea is 5.Nc3, planning g3 to undermine Black's kingside pawns.
- 4.Bc4 has become slightly better regarded than 4.d4. The usual answer is 4...h6, Fischer's recommendation. Together, ...d6 and ...h6 keep the knight on f3 out of both e5 and g5. The modern line 5.h4 Nf6 6.Nc3 stops ...g5 and is well regarded for White. After 5.d4 g5 the game Spassky–Karpov, Hamburg 1982, a one-hour game for West German television, was won by Spassky on time in an ending of queen and knight against queen.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.6% of the 4 million games and Black 43.7%. Below 1800 White wins about 54.5%, but the gap closes with rating: 51.2% against 44.9% between 1800 and 2199, and from 2200 up Black is ahead, 48.1% against 47.2%. In 451 master games over the board Black won 40.8%, White 25.5%, and 33.7% were drawn.
