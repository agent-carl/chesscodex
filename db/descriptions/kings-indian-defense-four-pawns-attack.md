The Four Pawns Attack is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6 5.f4: against the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense) White puts four pawns side by side on c4, d4, e4 and f4. It is White's most aggressive method against the King's Indian and was often seen in the 1920s.

### The idea

With the fifth move White builds a massive centre, but at the cost of falling behind in development. If Black manages to open the position, White may find the centre overextended. Black's main answer is ...c5.

### Main lines

After 5...O-O 6.Nf3 Black has two main choices.

- 6...c5, the main line. After [7.d5](/openings/kings-indian-defense-four-pawns-attack-dynamic-attack) e6 8.Be2 exd5 9.cxd5 Black has three established moves: 9...Bg4 has been a solid line, 9...Re8 can be justified with solid play, and 9...b5 leads to sharp, dangerous play.
- 6...Na6, the Modern Variation, prepares ...Nc5 with counterplay. It has done well when White answers with neutral moves such as 7.Bd3, while 7.e5 is the most aggressive plan against it.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.1% of the 2.4 million games and Black 45.2%. The split hardly changes with rating: 51.0% against 45.8% under 1400, and 49.8% against 43.7% from 2200 up. In 3,929 master games over the board White won 38.8%, Black 26.8%, and 34.4% were drawn.
