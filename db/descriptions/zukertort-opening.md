1.Nf3, the Zukertort Opening, is named after Johannes Zukertort. It is the third most popular first move, behind [1.e4](/openings/kings-pawn-game) and [1.d4](/openings/queens-pawn-game). It is sometimes called the Réti Opening, although most sources keep that name for 1.Nf3 d5 2.c4, the [Réti Opening](/openings/reti-opening) proper.

### The idea

1.Nf3 develops a piece, controls d4 and e5 and stops ...e5, without committing a central pawn. Aron Nimzowitsch called it certainly the most solid move, while 1.e4 and 1.d4 commit White. Edmar Mednis called it a "perfect and flexible opening".

That flexibility is the point: White waits to see Black's set-up and only then chooses a structure. Many games transpose into openings that usually begin with 1.d4 or 1.c4:

- the [Queen's Gambit](/openings/queens-gambit) and the [Catalan Opening](/openings/catalan-opening) after d4 and c4;
- the [English Opening](/openings/english-opening) after c4 without d4.

### Independent lines

- The [Réti Opening](/openings/reti-opening): 1...d5 2.c4, attacking Black's centre from the side.
- The [King's Indian Attack](/openings/kings-indian-attack): g3, Bg2, d3 and e4, a set-up White can play against almost anything.
- The [Nimzo-Larsen Attack](/openings/nimzo-larsen-attack) ideas with b3 and Bb2.

### Black's main answers

In order of popularity: 1...Nf6, 1...d5, 1...c5 (the [Sicilian Invitation](/openings/zukertort-opening-sicilian-invitation)), 1...g6 ([Kingside Fianchetto](/openings/zukertort-opening-kingside-fianchetto)), 1...e6 and 1...f5. Most of them keep Black's options open as well, so the real opening is often decided only on move two or three.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Very flexible, and hard for the opponent to prepare against.
- Avoids many sharp lines after 1.e4 and some after 1.d4.
- Lets White steer into familiar structures by transposition.

**Weaknesses**

- Black can also choose freely, for example the symmetrical 1...Nf6 or 1...d5 set-ups.
- Transpositions need knowledge of several openings, not just one.

### Who should play it

1.Nf3 suits positional players who know the typical structures of the 1.d4 and 1.c4 openings, and those who like to keep the opponent guessing about which opening is coming.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.2–51.2% and Black 42–46.3%; Black does best under 1400 (46.3%). Draws rise from 4.2–4.4% below 1800 to 8.3% from 2200 up. In master games over the board, White wins 33.3%, Black 22.2% and 44.5% are drawn.
