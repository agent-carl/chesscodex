The Rousseau Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 f5: Black meets the [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game) by attacking White's e-pawn with the f-pawn. It is named after the French master Eugène Rousseau and is also called the Ponziani Countergambit, after Domenico Lorenzo Ponziani.

### The idea

The move ...f5 weakens Black's own kingside, where the bishop on c4 is aiming. White's key themes are to attack Black's kingside and to stop Black from simplifying the position. Trading off White's light-squared bishop is particularly suspect.

### Main lines

- 4.d4 gives White a clear advantage. After 4...fxe4 5.Nxe5 d5 6.Bb5 Ne7 7.O-O a6 8.Bxc6+ bxc6 9.f3 Bf5 10.Nc3 White is better, according to the Bilguer Handbuch. After 4...d6 5.dxe5 dxe5 6.Qxd8+ Nxd8 7.Nxe5 fxe4 8.Bd2 Bd6 9.Bc3 White is also better: this was played in a consultation game in London in 1857, with Morphy and Mongredien on the black side. 4...Nf6 5.dxe5 Nxe4 6.O-O Bc5 7.Nc3 Nxc3 8.bxc3 leads to the same verdict.
- 4.d3 declines the gambit: White supports the e-pawn and waits to take on f5. The position resembles a King's Gambit Declined with colours reversed, with White's bishop aiming at Black's weakened kingside. Black will have trouble castling short, and Ng5 is a likely threat. White's position is better but still needs careful play. 4...Bc5 here transposes to the Lucchini Gambit.
- 4.exf5 is inferior. White still has a good game, but the position is less clear. Black usually plays 4...e4, which White may meet with 5.Nd4 Nf6 6.Nxc6. Here 5...Nxd4 runs into trouble after 6.Qh5+.

### Who should play it

Theory gives White the better game after both 4.d4 and 4.d3, so a White player who knows these lines has little to fear. In online play, though, Black does well with it, as the numbers below show.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does better than White: in 2.5 million games Black wins 50.5% and White 46.2%. Black stays ahead up to 2199, winning 50.9% under 1400 and 49.9% between 1800 and 2199. From 2200 up the picture flips: White wins 48.0% and Black 47.3%. There are too few master games over the board, 15, to say anything.
