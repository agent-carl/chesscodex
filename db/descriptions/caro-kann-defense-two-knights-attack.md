The Two Knights Attack against the [Caro-Kann Defense](/openings/caro-kann-defense) (1.e4 c6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Nf3) can also arise from 2.Nf3 d5 3.Nc3. White develops both knights quickly and keeps options open for the d-pawn. Bobby Fischer played it in his youth, but it has since declined in popularity.

### Main lines

- 3...Bg4, the [Mindeno Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-two-knights-attack-mindeno-variation), Black's logical and probably best reply. After 4.h3:
  - 4...Bxf3 5.Qxf3, the [Exchange Line](/openings/caro-kann-defense-two-knights-attack-mindeno-variation-exchange-line), the positional continuation, when Black can choose between 5...Nf6 and 5...e6.
  - 4...Bh5, the [Retreat Line](/openings/caro-kann-defense-two-knights-attack-mindeno-variation-retreat-line), is playable, but Black must be careful. In Noteboom–Mindeno, 1927, Black lost quickly after 5.exd5 cxd5 6.g4 Bg6 7.Ne5 a6?, where 7...Nc6 was necessary.
- 3...dxe4 4.Nxe4 hides a trap for Black players who continue as in the [Classical Variation](/openings/caro-kann-defense-classical-variation): 4...Bf5 5.Ng3 Bg6 6.h4 h6 7.Ne5 Bh7 8.Qh5 g6 9.Bc4 e6 (9...gxh5 10.Bxf7#), and after 10.Qf3 or 10.Qe2 White has a huge advantage. Lasker–Radsheer, 1908, and Alekhine–Bruce, 1938, both ended quickly after 10.Qe2 and 11.Nxf7. Black should prefer 5...Bg4, and 4...Nd7 is also playable. After 3...dxe4, 4.Ng5 is the [Hector Gambit](/openings/caro-kann-defense-hector-gambit).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Fast development, with White's d-pawn still free to go to d3 or d4.
- Traps for opponents who play it like a Classical Caro-Kann.

**Weaknesses**

- 3...Bg4 gives Black a logical and sound answer.
- It has declined in popularity since Fischer played it.

### Who should play it

The Two Knights Attack suits White players who want to leave the main Caro-Kann theory behind and play a flexible, developing system.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black is slightly ahead under 1400, 48.9% against 47.1%, and White leads from there on: 48.8% against 47.2% at 1400–1799, 49.7% against 44.7% at 1800–2199, and 48.2% against 43.6% from 2200 up. In about 9,100 master games White won 32.1%, Black 27.0%, and 40.9% were drawn.
