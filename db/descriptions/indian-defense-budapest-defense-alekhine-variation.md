The Alekhine Variation of the [Budapest Gambit](/openings/indian-defense-budapest-defense) is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 Ng4 4.e4. White does not try to keep the extra pawn and concentrates on building a strong pawn centre and a space advantage. Alexander Alekhine showed its strength in his wins against Ilya Rabinovich at Baden-Baden 1925 and Adolf Seitz at Hastings 1925–26. Alekhine wrote that it is considered with good reason to be White's best system against the Budapest Gambit: White hands the pawn back but gains control of d5, though over the next few moves White has to play with extreme precision.

### The main line

After 4.e4 the main line is 4...Nxe5 5.f4, and Black must decide where the knight goes. The retreat to the queenside, 5...Nec6, is considered best: there it is safer than on g6 and helps control the central dark squares. 5...Ng6 is probably playable. A disputed point is whether the typical manoeuvre ...Bb4xc3 helps Black, by giving White doubled pawns, or White, by strengthening the centre.

### Réti's 4...h5

Taylor considers 4...Nxe5 inferior and recommends a rarely played idea of Richard Réti, 4...h5. He gives the main line as 4...h5 5.h3 Nxe5 6.Be3 Bb4+, with good play for Black.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.3% of the 295,231 games and Black 42.8%, and the gap barely changes from one rating band to the next. In 231 master games over the board White won 46.8%, Black 24.2%, and 29.0% were drawn.
