The French Variation of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense) is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6. With 2...e6 Black gives priority to developing the dark-squared bishop.

### Main lines after 3.d4

After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Black has three main moves:

- [4...Nc6](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation), the Taimanov Variation.
- [4...a6](/openings/sicilian-defense-kan-variation), the Kan Variation.
- 4...Nf6, after which 5.Nc3 is played; 5.e5 runs into 5...Qa5+. Black can then transpose to the [Scheveningen Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-scheveningen-variation) with 5...d6, play 5...Nc6, the [Four Knights Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-four-knights-variation), or play 5...Bb4, the [Pin Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-pin-variation). The Pin Variation is considered theoretically suspect, though if White is unprepared the tactics can be hard to calculate at the board; 6.e5! is White's most testing reply.

### Without 3.d4

White does not have to open the position.

- 3.d3 aims for a King's Indian Attack set-up with g3 and Bg2. Bobby Fischer used it to crush Oscar Panno in a famous game at Buenos Aires 1970.
- 3.c3 transposes to the [Alapin Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation) after 3...Nf6, or to the French Defense after 3...d5 4.e5 Nc6 5.d4, though 4...d4 is stronger.
- 3.c4 transposes to the Symmetrical English.
- 3.Nc3 is sometimes played as a waiting move but has little independent significance, and [3.b3](/openings/sicilian-defense-french-variation-westerinen-attack) is a rare try that Heikki Westerinen played in the 1970s.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 48.5% of the 40 million games and White 46.6%. Black is ahead up to 2199, 49.2% against 47.4% under 1400; from 2200 up White edges ahead, 47.0% against 46.0%. In 115,430 master games over the board White won 34.4%, Black 28.3%, and 37.4% were drawn.
