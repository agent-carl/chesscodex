The Marshall Gambit is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 c6 4.e4, a gambit against the "triangle setup" of the [Semi-Slav Defense](/openings/semi-slav-defense). Playing 3...e6 before ...Nf6 opens up new options for both sides on the fourth move, and one of White's is to strike in the centre at once with 4.e4.

### The main line

Play continues 4...dxe4 5.Nxe4 Bb4+. The main line is considered to be 6.Bd2 Qxd4 7.Bxb4 Qxe4+, and White then blocks the check with the knight or the bishop. 4...Bb4 instead of 4...dxe4 is sometimes played but should favour White.

### 6.Nc3

White can also answer 5...Bb4+ with 6.Nc3, as Magnus Carlsen did against Viswanathan Anand in their 2013 World Championship match. Play then continues 6...c5 7.a3 Ba5.

### Avoiding the Noteboom

After 4.Nf3 Black has the extra option of 4...dxc4, the [Noteboom Variation](/openings/semi-slav-defense-noteboom-variation). To avoid it, White must play either 4.e4 or 4.e3, which usually transposes to the [Meran](/openings/semi-slav-defense-meran-variation) but deprives White of the lines with Bg5.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 55.0% of the 1.1 million games and Black 40.2%, and White's lead holds in every rating band. In 2,452 master games over the board 44.2% were drawn, White won 34.4% and Black 21.3%.
