The Old Sicilian is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6, one of Black's three main second moves in the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense), alongside 2...d6 and 2...e6. It is a natural developing move that also prepares ...Nf6: as after 2...d6, Black stops White from answering ...Nf6 with e5.

### Main lines after 3.d4

After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Black's most common move is 4...Nf6. Other important moves are 4...e6, transposing to the [Taimanov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-taimanov-variation); 4...g6, the [Accelerated Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-accelerated-dragon); and 4...e5, which leads to the [Kalashnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-kalashnikov-variation) after 5.Nb5 d6.

After 4...Nf6 White usually replies 5.Nc3, and Black chooses between:

- 5...d6, transposing to the [Classical Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-classical-variation);
- 5...e5, the Sveshnikov or [Lasker-Pelikan Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation);
- 5...e6, transposing to the [Four Knights Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-four-knights-variation).

Other moves include 5...g6, aiming for the Dragon while avoiding the Maróczy Bind, and the provocative 5...h5, known as the "Dirty Harry Sicilian".

### Without 3.d4

- 3.Bb5 is the [Rossolimo Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-nyezhmetdinov-rossolimo-attack).
- 3.Nc3 is a common transpositional device: White can play d4 or Bb5 next depending on Black's reply. Black sometimes answers 3...e5 to avoid both, and then 4.Bc4 is considered White's best move.
- 3.c3 tends to transpose to the [Alapin Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-alapin-variation).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.5% of the 87 million games and Black 46.6%. The gap is small under 1400, 48.9% against 47.5%, and grows from 2200 up to 48.4% against 44.1%. In 134,924 master games over the board 44.4% were drawn, White won 31.8% and Black 23.8%.
