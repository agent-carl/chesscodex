The Löwenthal Variation (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e5) is one of Black's sharper answers in the [Open Sicilian](/openings/sicilian-defense-open). Black kicks the knight at once with ...e5, accepting a hole on d5 in return for time. After 4...e5 White usually plays 5.Nb5, when Black has two main choices.

### Origins

The variation was played by Louis-Charles Mahé de La Bourdonnais in his 1834 matches against Alexander McDonnell. It takes its name from Johann Löwenthal, one of the strongest players of the mid-19th century, and has had bursts of popularity since, for example in the 1940s.

### The idea

After 4...e5 5.Nb5 the knight eyes d6. In the Löwenthal proper, 5...a6, Black lets the knight in: 6.Nd6+ Bxd6 7.Qxd6 Qf6 (7...Qe7 is also possible). Black gives up the bishop pair to get a lead in development. White most often retreats the queen to d1; exchanging queens at once is uncommon.

### Main lines

- 5.Nb5 a6, the Löwenthal Variation proper, with 6.Nd6+ Bxd6 7.Qxd6 Qf6. It declined once it was established that White keeps the advantage in several lines.
- 5.Nb5 d6, the [Kalashnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-kalashnikov-variation), which Black players revived in the late 1980s and which is now much more popular. Its ideas are close to the [Sveshnikov Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-lasker-pelikan-variation): Black accepts a backward d-pawn and a weak d5-square in return for time spent chasing the knight.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Black gains time against White's knight and gets a lead in development.

**Weaknesses**

- In the 5...a6 line Black gives up the bishop pair, and White keeps the advantage in several lines.
- The d5-square and, in the Kalashnikov, the d6-pawn become long-term weaknesses.

### Who should play it

4...e5 suits Black players who like dynamic, gambit-like Sicilian positions; today most of them choose 5...d6, the Kalashnikov, rather than 5...a6.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are close at every level: 48.5% for White against 47.8% under 1400, 48.6% against 47.2% at 1400–1799, 48.6% against 46.1% at 1800–2199 and 46.5% against 45.5% from 2200 up. In about 9,300 master games White won 33.0%, Black 24.9%, and 42.2% were drawn.
