The Old Variation is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.e3, a quiet way for White to meet the [Queen's Gambit Accepted](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted). The apparently modest 3.e3 prepares to win back the pawn at once with Bxc4, and strong players, including Anatoly Karpov, have often used it.

### Main lines

- 3...e5 strikes back at the centre. Its early discovery gave 3.e3 a harmless reputation for a long time. A typical continuation is 4.Bxc4 exd4 5.exd4, with an isolated queen's pawn for White. The open positions that follow have not proved easy for Black to handle in practice, but the author Rizzitano still recommends 3...e5 against 3.e3.
- 3...e6 is a simpler choice that many players make, transposing back to the main lines of the Queen's Gambit Accepted.

### A trap from 1604

An old trap catches Black trying to keep the extra pawn. Alessandro Salvio pointed out in 1604 that after 3...b5? 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5?? the long diagonal is fatally weakened: 6.Qf3 Nc6 7.Qxc6+ wins a piece. 3...Be6 does hold on to the pawn, but White gets good compensation after 4.Ne2.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 55.7% of the 5.7 million games and Black 39.5%. White does well below 2200, for example 56.1% against 40.0% under 1400, but from 2200 up the margin is much smaller, 47.1% against 43.6%. In 5,015 master games over the board White won 32.6%, Black 18.7%, and 48.7% were drawn.
