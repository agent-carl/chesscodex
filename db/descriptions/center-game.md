The Center Game (1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.Qxd4) opens the centre on move two. White recaptures with the queen, accepting that it will be chased by 3...Nc6, in return for quick, aggressive development.

### Origins

The opening dates from 1590 or earlier, and Philipp Stamma analysed it in 1745. In the late 19th century Siegbert Tarrasch, Mikhail Chigorin and Wilfried Paulsen played it regularly, but it was mostly abandoned after 1900 because no advantage could be shown for White. Jacques Mieses, Sultan Khan, Savielly Tartakower and Rudolf Spielmann were the last strong players to adopt it until Alexander Shabalov revived it in the 1990s, followed by Alexei Shirov, Michael Adams, Judit Polgár and Alexander Morozevich; more recently Ian Nepomniachtchi and Arjun Erigaisi have played it too.

### The idea

After 3.Qxd4 Nc6 White's queen usually goes to e3, the [Paulsen Attack](/openings/center-game-paulsen-attack-variation), from where it hinders Black's freeing ...d5. In the main line White castles long and often gets an attractive kingside attack, and the opening is considered well suited to faster time controls.

### Main lines

- [Normal Variation](/openings/center-game-normal-variation) 3.Qxd4 Nc6 4.Qe3 Nf6 ([Berger Variation](/openings/center-game-berger-variation)), where the main line is 5.Nc3 Bb4 6.Bd2 O-O 7.O-O-O Re8. 5.Bd2 is considered safer for White.
- 4.Qc4, the rarer [Hall Variation](/openings/center-game-hall-variation).
- Gambits instead of 3.Qxd4:
  - 3.c3, the [Danish Gambit](/openings/danish-gambit);
  - 3.Bc4, the [von der Lasa Gambit](/openings/center-game-von-der-lasa-gambit), linked to the Scotch Gambit;
  - 3.f4, the [Halasz-McDonnell Gambit](/openings/center-game-halasz-mcdonnell-gambit).

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Fast, aggressive play and chances for a kingside attack.
- Many opponents have not prepared against it.
- The queen on e3 makes ...d5 hard for Black.

**Weaknesses**

- The early queen move costs time; Black develops with gain of tempo.
- Theory has never shown an advantage for White, so a prepared opponent equalises.

### Who should play it

The Center Game suits attacking players who like opposite-side castling and fast games, and who want to avoid the heavily analysed [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) and [Italian Game](/openings/italian-game).

### The numbers

On Lichess White does well above 1400: 52.2% against 44.2% at 1400–1799 and 53.3% against 42.6% at 1800–2199. Under 1400 it is 50.5% against 45.4%, and from 2200 up 49.7% against 44.2%. There are only about 1,900 master games, too few to draw firm conclusions.
