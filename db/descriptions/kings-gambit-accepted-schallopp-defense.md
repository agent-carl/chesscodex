The Schallopp Defense is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 Nf6, a reply to the [King's Knight's Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kings-knights-gambit). Black usually intends to defend the extra pawn on f4 with ...Nh5. It is not Black's most popular option, but it has received more interest in the 21st century, particularly in 2020, when Ding Liren used it to beat Magnus Carlsen in the online Magnus Carlsen Invitational.

### 4.e5 Nh5

The most common continuation is 4.e5 Nh5, often followed by 5.d4. The knight on h5 is undefended, so Black must be careful: for example 5.d4 d6 6.Qe2 Be7? 7.exd6 Qxd6 8.Qb5+ wins it. The correct move is 6...d5, with equality.

### 4.Nc3

Also common is 4.Nc3, which defends e4 and lets Black's knight stay on f6. Black most often answers 4...d5, and White usually continues 5.e5, met by 5...Nh5 or 5...Ne4, or 5.exd5, which transposes to the [Abbazia Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-abbazia-defense).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.6% of the 1.6 million games and Black 42.8%. The result turns with rating: White wins 57.1% between 1400 and 1799, but from 2200 up Black is ahead, 49.9% against 44.6%. In 205 master games over the board 42.4% were drawn, Black won 32.2% and White 25.4%.
