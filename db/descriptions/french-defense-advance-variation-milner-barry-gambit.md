The Milner-Barry Gambit is a sharp line of the [Advance Variation](/openings/french-defense-advance-variation) of the French: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.Bd3. White develops the bishop instead of protecting d4, and offers the d-pawn for quick development and an attack.

### Origins

The gambit is named after Sir Stuart Milner-Barry (1906–1995), a British chess player and writer who represented England at the Chess Olympiads of 1937 and 1939. He won the first British Boys' Championship in 1923 and was later the chess correspondent of The Times. During the Second World War he worked as a codebreaker at Bletchley Park, from autumn 1943 heading Hut 6, which decrypted German Army and Air Force Enigma messages. Four opening variations carry his name.

### Main lines

The main line continues [6...cxd4 7.cxd4 Bd7 8.O-O](/openings/french-defense-advance-variation-milner-barry-gambit-main-line) Nxd4 9.Nxd4 Qxd4 10.Nc3. Black is a pawn up.

- If Black takes a second pawn with 10...Qxe5, White gets an attack with 11.Re1 Qb8 12.Nxd5, or 11...Qd6 12.Nb5.
- On move seven Black must not grab the pawn at once: 7...Nxd4 8.Nxd4 Qxd4 loses the queen to 9.Bb5+, a discovered attack on the queen from the queen on d1.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.3% of the 2.2 million games and Black 45.5%. The split hardly moves with rating: 50.1% against 47.1% under 1400, and 49.7% against 44.6% from 2200 up. In 779 master games over the board White won 40.1%, Black 32.2%, and 27.7% were drawn.
