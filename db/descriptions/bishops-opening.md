The Bishop's Opening (1.e4 e5 2.Bc4) is one of the oldest openings in chess. White develops the king's bishop at once, aiming at f7, and keeps the knight and the f-pawn flexible. Its main modern appeal is flexibility: it can transpose into the Italian Game, the Vienna Game or even the King's Gambit, while avoiding [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense).

### Origins

The Bishop's Opening was examined by Lucena and Ruy López, and later developed by Philidor, whose work gave it long-lasting popularity. It fell out of favour after improvements for Black were found in the mid-19th century. Bent Larsen revived it, playing it often after first using it at the 1964 Interzonal. Garry Kasparov used it occasionally as a surprise, John Nunn has used it to avoid the Petrov, and Peter Leko played it against Vladimir Kramnik at the 2007 World Championship tournament.

### The idea

2.Bc4 attacks f7 and stops Black from playing ...d5 easily. White keeps the options of Nf3, Nc3, d3 and even f2–f4, so the opening can be steered into different structures. A common plan is a quiet setup with d3, Nf3, c3 and O-O, similar to the modern Italian Game. The difference is that Black doesn't get the chance to play the Petrov with 2...Nf6 against 2.Nf3.

### Main lines

- [Berlin Defense](/openings/bishops-opening-berlin-defense) 2...Nf6: the most common reply, usually met by 3.d3, with the [Paulsen Defense](/openings/bishops-opening-paulsen-defense) 3...c6 and the [Vienna Hybrid](/openings/bishops-opening-vienna-hybrid) 3...Nc6 4.Nc3 as main branches.
- [Urusov Gambit](/openings/bishops-opening-urusov-gambit) 3.d4 exd4 4.Nf3: a sharp pawn sacrifice for quick development.
- Classical Variation [2...Bc5](/openings/bishops-opening-boi-variation): Black mirrors White's bishop.
- 2...c6, the [Philidor Counterattack](/openings/bishops-opening-philidor-counterattack), preparing ...d5.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Flexible, with many transpositions: White chooses the type of game.
- Avoids the Petrov, which many 1.e4 players find annoying.
- Simple development and clear ideas, good for learning open games.

**Weaknesses**

- According to Nick de Firmian, it leads to equality with best play by both sides.
- Black has simple and solid answers, such as 2...Nf6 and ...d5.
- The early bishop move gives Black some targets, such as ...d5 or ...Na5 at the right moment.

### Who should play it

The Bishop's Opening suits flexible 1.e4 players who want to avoid the Petrov and prefer quiet, manoeuvring Italian-style positions, or who like to mix in the Vienna and the King's Gambit. It is effective against opponents who rely on fixed replies to 2.Nf3. It gives the least against well-prepared players who answer 2...Nf6 and aim for a quick ...d5.

### The numbers

On Lichess it is played very often at club level. Below 1800 White wins 51% and Black 45%. From 1800 up it narrows to 49–50% against 45–46%. In master games over the board, White wins 34%, Black 22% and 43% are drawn.
