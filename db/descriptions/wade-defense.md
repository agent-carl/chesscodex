The Wade Defense is 1.d4 d6 2.Nf3 Bg4: Black pins the knight on f3 at once. It can also arise from 1.Nf3 d6 2.d4 Bg4. The ECO code is A41.

### Origins

The opening is named after the British International Master Bob Wade (1921–2008), originally from New Zealand, who played it for over 30 years. It was played even earlier, in 1938, by Rudolf Spielmann, and in the 1960s by Leonid Stein and Lubomir Kavalek among others.

Several grandmasters have played it often, including Julian Hodgson, Michael Adams, Vlastimil Jansa and Tony Miles. Jouni Yrjölä and Jussi Tella, in their book An Explosive Chess Opening Repertoire for Black, call Hodgson "the towering figure of the line", who popularised it with many dynamic performances, and they name the opening the "Hodgson Variation" in his honour. They also credit Miles, Adams, Hickl and Jansa with developing its theory.

### The idea

In the two games below Black followed up with ...Nd7 and then ...c5 or ...e5, and in one of them gave up the bishop for the knight on f3 at once.

### Main lines

Two games show typical play after 3.c4 Nd7.

- Topalov–Adams, Dortmund 1996: 3.c4 Nd7 4.e4 Bxf3 5.Qxf3 g6 6.Nc3 Bg7 7.Qd1 c5 8.d5 Bxc3+ 9.bxc3. Black gave up both bishops for knights, castled queenside and won.
- Vera–Gulko, Lucerne 1993: 3.c4 Nd7 4.Nc3 e5 5.e3 c6 6.h3 Bh5 7.Be2 Be7 8.O-O f5, and Black went on to win.

Related lines on this site are 1.Nf3 d6 2.e4 Bg4, the [Zukertort Opening: Wade Defense](/openings/zukertort-opening-wade-defense), and 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 d6, the [Wade-Tartakower Defense](/openings/indian-defense-wade-tartakower-defense).

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.6% of the 440,000 games and Black 44.1%. Below 2200 White is ahead by 6 to 9 points: 51.3% against 44.1% under 1400 and 52.1% against 43.4% between 1400 and 1799. From 2200 up it is close, 46.5% against 44.9%. In 4,889 master games over the board White won 36.4%, Black 26.1%, and 37.5% were drawn.
