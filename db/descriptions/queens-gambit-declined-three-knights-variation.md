The Three Knights Variation of the [Queen's Gambit Declined](/openings/queens-gambit-declined) is reached here by 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 d5 4.Nc3, the move order by which it is most often played. With 3.Nf3 White avoids the [Nimzo-Indian Defense](/openings/nimzo-indian-defense), and with ...Nf6 first Black avoids the [Exchange Variation](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-exchange-variation) of the QGD. The same position arises from 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Nf3.

### Black's fourth move

- 4...Be7 is the classical choice. 5.Bg5 transposes to the traditional lines of the QGD, but White can avoid them, most notably with 5.Bf4, the Harrwitz Attack. With the bishop on f4 instead of g5, Black has fewer chances to exchange pieces with ...Ne4, as in the Lasker Defense, or to win the bishop pair. Peter Leko used it to beat Vladimir Kramnik in their 2004 World Championship match.
- 4...Bb4 is the [Ragozin Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-ragozin-defense).
- 4...c6 is the [Semi-Slav Defense](/openings/semi-slav-defense).
- 4...c5 is the [Semi-Tarrasch Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-semi-tarrasch-defense).
- 4...dxc4 is the Vienna Variation, whose main line continues 5.e4 Bb4 6.Bg5.

### The Semi-Tarrasch endgame

An important line of the Semi-Tarrasch is the Endgame Line: 4...c5 5.cxd5 cxd4 6.Qxd4 exd5 7.e4 dxe4 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8. The queens come off much as in the Berlin endgame, and the position is drawish; Wesley So, Anish Giri and Magnus Carlsen have used it when aiming for a draw.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.1% of the 15.7 million games and Black 43.1%, and the gap is about the same in every rating band. Masters draw most of their games: in 78,777 games over the board 57.1% were drawn, White won 26.2% and Black 16.7%.
