The Classical Variation of the [French Defense](/openings/french-defense) (1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nf6) is one of the major systems of the French. Black develops the knight and attacks e4, inviting White either to advance with e5 or to pin the knight with Bg5. The position was considered so normal that nobody's name was attached to it.

### The idea

White's most common replies are 4.e5, the Steinitz Variation, and 4.Bg5, which pins the knight and threatens 5.e5. Black meets that threat with 4...Be7, the classical answer, with 4...dxe4, the Burn Variation, or with the counterattack 4...Bb4, the McCutcheon Variation.

### Main lines

- 4.Bg5:
  - 4...Be7 5.e5 Nfd7 6.Bxe7 Qxe7, the [old main line](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-normal-variation) of the French, usually 7.f4. The gambit 6.h4, the [Alekhine-Chatard Attack](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack), devised by Adolf Albin and taken seriously after Alekhine–Fahrni, Mannheim 1914, now rivals 6.Bxe7;
  - 4...dxe4, the [Burn Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-burn-variation), named after Amos Burn and now the most common reply at top level. After 5.Nxe4 Be7 6.Bxf6 Bxf6 7.Nf3 the position resembles the Rubinstein Variation, but Black has the bishop pair; Evgeny Bareev has long favoured it;
  - 4...Bb4, the [McCutcheon Variation](/openings/french-defense-mccutcheon-variation), named after John Lindsay McCutcheon, who used it to beat Steinitz in a simultaneous exhibition in 1885. The main line runs 5.e5 h6 6.Bd2 Bxc3 7.bxc3 Ne4 8.Qg4.
- 4.e5 Nfd7, the [Steinitz Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-steinitz-variation), now about as popular as 4.Bg5. The main line is 5.f4 c5 6.Nf3 Nc6 7.Be3; 5.Nce2 is the [Shirov-Anand Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-shirov-anand-variation).
- 4.exd5, the [Delayed Exchange Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-delayed-exchange-variation), and 4.Bd3, the [Swiss Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation-swiss-variation), are less common.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Natural development that puts immediate pressure on e4.
- Against 4.Bg5 Black has three distinct answers: the classical 4...Be7, the Burn 4...dxe4 and the counterattacking McCutcheon 4...Bb4.

**Weaknesses**

- White chooses the structure with 4.e5 or 4.Bg5, so Black must prepare for both.
- In the Steinitz main line White builds a broad pawn centre on d4, e5 and f4.

### Who should play it

The Classical Variation suits French players who want classical development rather than the [Winawer](/openings/french-defense-winawer-variation), and who are ready to study the Steinitz and 4.Bg5 lines.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are mixed: White leads 48.5% against 47.7% under 1400, Black leads 49.2% against 47.2% at 1400–1799, and White leads again at 1800–2199 (48.9% against 46.1%) and from 2200 up (49.1% against 43.8%). In about 31,300 master games White won 33.3%, Black 21.1%, and 45.5% were drawn.
