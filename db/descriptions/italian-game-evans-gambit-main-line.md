The main line of the [Evans Gambit](/openings/italian-game-evans-gambit) is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.b4 Bxb4 5.c3 Ba5. Black accepts the b-pawn and retreats the bishop to a5, where it pins the c3-pawn if White plays 6.d4. The drawback is that the a5-square is no longer available to Black's knight, so Black will often retreat the bishop to b6 later to make room for ...Na5. ECO gives this line its own code, C52.

### 6.d4 exd4

White usually continues 6.d4, offering a second pawn; 6.O-O and 6.Qb3 are the alternatives. After 6...exd4 White cannot recapture with 7.cxd4 because of the pin. 7.Nxd4 is possible but sets Black few problems, so White usually prefers 7.O-O or 7.Qb3, the move preferred by Nigel Short.

- 7.O-O Nge7 is the Mieses Defence. Formerly rare, it was recommended by Vasily Panov in the 1960s and is now one of the main lines. Black meets 8.Ng5 or 8.cxd4 with 8...d5, returning the pawn in many lines.
- 7.O-O dxc3, the Compromised Defence, takes the second pawn. It is well met by 8.Qb3 Qf6 9.e5 Qg6 10.Nxc3 Nge7 11.Ba3, with a very dangerous initiative for White, and is not well regarded.
- 7.O-O Nf6 is the Anderssen Defence.
- 7...d6 and 7...Bb6 usually aim for the Normal Position after 8.cxd4.

### 6.d4 d6

With 6...d6 Black supports e5 instead. After 7.O-O Bb6, Emanuel Lasker's defence, play most often goes 8.dxe5 dxe5 9.Qxd8+ Nxd8 10.Nxe5 Be6. Black returns the pawn and exchanges queens, which takes the sting out of White's attack; Reuben Fine called it "psychologically depressing for the gambit player". White avoids it with 7.Qb3, the Tartakower Attack, the most frequent move here; Black usually defends f7 with 7...Qd7.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.1% of the 1.3 million games and Black 45.7%. From 2200 up Black is slightly ahead, 47.8% against 47.5%. Masters draw more than half their games: in 889 games over the board 54.0% were drawn, Black won 24.6% and White 21.4%.
